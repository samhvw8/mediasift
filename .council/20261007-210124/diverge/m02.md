## Baseline (the obvious answers)
- Embedding veto on camera keyframes plus k-center budget (already proposed items 1 and 2).
- `framesift find` text-to-frame search over stored vectors (item 3).
- Swap MiniLM for the EmbeddingGemma text tower in chapter TextTiling (item 4).

## Assumptions I broke
- "The embedding model's job is to pick or rank frames": the graveyard of video summarisation (DPP/SUM-GAN, then Otani 2019 showing random frame picks score about the same as SOTA) says selection quality was never the bottleneck or the measurable thing. The model can instead audit, calibrate and gate other components, where the old systems had no shared space to do it.
- "Embeddings are a new subsystem to add": framesift's own history (cut-threshold sweep against codec scene-cuts, screen-vs-camera 25% rule) shows its real cost is hand-set knobs that failed across corpora (0.25 floor missed the whole lecture). Through the Historian lens the model's best use is deleting knobs by supplying a zero-shot or self-calibrated replacement, not adding features.

## Ideas
### m02.1 Caption audit: cosine(VLM caption, frame) as a hallucination tripwire
Precedent: CLIPScore / RefCLIPScore (2021) became the standard cheap caption-faithfulness check because it needs no reference; it died as a "quality score" because it is blunt, but survives as a flag. The `meaning` step trusts a VLM's description of each keyframe with no check. Embed each caption with the text tower and the frame with the vision tower; any caption below the video's own low percentile is flagged in storyboard.md (and crashes loudly in strict mode) instead of silently feeding wrong chapter summaries. Now it is nearly free because it is one model, one space, and the vectors are already stored.

### m02.2 Narration redundancy score: spend frame tokens only where words cannot explain them
Precedent: Informedia video skimming (CMU, 1990s) aligned audio and visuals to cut redundancy; it died because ASR was poor and there was no joint text-image space. Now embed each transcript sentence and its nearest keyframe in the same space. High similarity means the narration already says what the frame shows (talking head, "as you can see the cat"), so the frame is demoted from the contact sheet; low similarity means the picture carries information the words do not (diagram, silent action), so it is promoted. This turns the `--max-frames` budget into a per-token information measure, which is the user's stated goal, rather than a pixel-drift ranking.

### m02.3 Zero-shot screen/camera classifier replaces the 25% identical-frames rule
Precedent: CLIP zero-shot content typing ("a screen recording", "a person talking to camera", "an animation") works well for coarse classes and is the reason hand-built content detectors mostly died. screen-vs-camera.md is still status: draft and drives drift, area weight and gap. Per-segment (not per-video) prompt scores would also handle videos that switch between slides and a speaker, which a single whole-video rule cannot. The one-line proof: agreement with the current rule on the 4 bench videos, then look only at the disagreements.

### m02.4 Self-calibrated embedding threshold from codec scene-cuts
Precedent: fixed thresholds killed shot-boundary detection across corpora for decades (framesift's own sweep: 0.25 got 0/22 on CS50). Fix: for each video, codec I-frame scene-cuts are free labels. Take embedding distances across known cuts versus within shots and set the veto/merge threshold per video (Otsu-style split of those two populations). This deletes the one new knob that proposals 1 and 4 would otherwise add. Limit: videos with no codec scene-cuts (screen recordings, fixed GOP) fall back to a documented default, loudly.

### m02.5 Sound queries for videos with no speech
Precedent: AudioSet classifiers (YAMNet and kin) did "find the laughter/applause/music start" but needed a fixed 527-label vocabulary and died when users wanted other words. The audio tower plus text tower gives open-vocabulary sound search. `framesift find` could take `--audio` so an agent asks "when does the music change" or "door slam" on animation and short films, where faster-whisper returns nothing and log-mel only says "something changed". Unknown and must be tested first: whether the 300M audio tower on a Mac runs fast enough, and quality on short non-speech clips.

### m02.6 Make "beats random frames at equal budget" the bench gate for every embedding feature
Precedent: Otani et al. 2019 found random summaries matched published SOTA on SumMe/TVSum, which invalidated a decade of F1 numbers. bench/prep_qa.py already asks questions of the video; add the control arm. For each feature (veto, k-center, narration demotion), run the QA with the feature on, with the current heuristic, and with uniform-random frames at the same count and token cost. A feature that does not beat random on QA accuracy per token does not ship, which also tells the council which proposals are worth the added surface.

### m02.7 Cross-modal boundary agreement as chapter evidence, not another fusion weight
Precedent: Informedia and TRECVID news story segmentation fused audio, visual and text cues with learned weights; it worked only on news (anchor-desk structure) and died on general video, because the weights did not transfer. Do not learn weights. Compute three change curves from the same model (visual tower, audio tower, transcript text tower) and only count a chapter boundary as "strong" when at least two modalities peak within a few seconds. The rule has no weights to tune, and "which modalities agreed" is cheap to print per chapter for the agent to read.

## The provocation I would put to the group
Every past video summariser died on "who judges the frames", so is EmbeddingGemma 2 more useful as the judge of framesift's own heuristics and VLM output than as another picker of frames?
