## Fragile assumptions

Ranked most fragile first.

1. **"Reuse the questions in bench/prep_qa.py" (untested, and false as written).** I read bench/prep_qa.py and bench/evaluate.py. prep_qa.py only prepares material (oracle frame sheets, timed transcript) for a question writer. It contains no questions and no answer time ranges, and no committed question file exists in bench/. So the ground truth does not exist yet. The concept's step 2 is really "a person or an LLM writes ~25 queries and labels answer spans". That is the dominant cost and the dominant bias risk. If an LLM writes them from the oracle sheets, the labels share the VLM's view of the frames, and the test drifts toward circular. Status: untested, and the stated shortcut is wrong.

2. **"~25 queries can pick a dimension" (shaky).** The concept admits no statistical power for a 1 point gap, then substitutes a sign test. With 25 pairs, a sign test cannot show "no worse"; it can only fail to find "worse". "No worse on a sign test" is a pass condition that fails open: small samples pass almost anything. Query set is also split across 4 videos of 4 genres, so each genre has about 6 queries, and the screen-recording row is the one where the risk is highest. Status: shaky. The kill number (">1 point AND loses sign test") is a conjunction that will almost never trigger at n=25.

3. **"128 d is a plausible candidate worth testing" (mostly solid, but the vendor already answered it).** The model card I fetched (https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2) publishes the truncation table: 768d MMEB v2 overall 59.01, 512d 58.38, 256d 56.24, 128d 45.65. MIEB lite 64.64 / 64.32 / 63.13 / 59.06. The card says quality is "close to lossless down to 256 dimensions" and that "128 dimensions degrades multimodal quality substantially and should be validated against your own workload". The concept says the card claims "minimal impact" and that there is nothing for video. That is out of date. MMEB v2 includes a Video task family, so there is already a vendor curve for the multimodal case: 128d loses about 13 points. The concept's own "kill number" is therefore already triggered by prior evidence at 128d. Status: solid, the experiment's headline hypothesis (128 everywhere) is probably dead on arrival. The honest remaining question is 256 vs 768 (a 2.8 point MMEB drop), which saves about 8 MB per video and nothing in runtime.

Side assumption, untested: the veto and k-center flip-rate thresholds (about 2%) are invented and unanchored. Jaccard overlap of greedy k-center sets is also unstable by construction (greedy picks cascade after a single early flip), so low Jaccard can occur even when coverage radius is equal. Radius in 768 space is the better metric; Jaccard adds noise.

## Pre-mortem

A year later: the table was run once in an afternoon. Queries were LLM-written from the oracle sheets. 128d looked worse on the screen-recording row, 256d and 512d looked equal to 768d on the mean, and the sign test was inconclusive, so the rule "smallest dimension that passes" picked 256 or 512. Someone then noticed vectors are only about 12 MB per video and the dimension saves nothing in run time, so the result became a constant (`DIM = 256`) in a file. Nobody re-ran it when the model was updated (or when the sampling rate moved off 1 fps), the CSV rotted, and the query sheet has not been touched. Nothing broke; the experiment's real output was "find works at all, or doesn't", and that was found at step zero (throughput and layout collapse on screen content), then proposals 1-3 were judged on that. The dimension question was a rounding error. Most likely failure is not a wrong answer but a low-value answer that cost the one afternoon of attention that should have gone to the veto and `find` quality, plus an orphaned CSV and query file that need upkeep.

## Who loses

Little to nobody is hurt by it succeeding. If it succeeds (one fixed dimension, no flag), the party that loses is the "per-stage config" design the council's other proposals might add (a `--dim` flag, a per-stage dimension constant). Its supporters would respond by arguing the table is underpowered and the knob should stay "for safety". The bigger practical response: whoever owns proposals 1 to 3 will treat this concept as a blocker and wait for its table before building, which delays the high-value work for a low-value decision. Also, the "step zero" embed cache is the thing that actually benefits everyone, so it will be quietly absorbed into other work and this concept's identity disappears.

## Fixes

| Weakness | Smallest fix |
|---|---|
| Questions do not exist in prep_qa.py | Correct the brief: create bench/queries.json by hand (about 25 queries, `video`, `text`, `start`, `end`), written by a human or by an LLM that sees the transcript but not frame embeddings. Add that to cost: it is the real work. No shortcut. |
| 25 queries cannot power a 1-point test | Stop treating the table as an equivalence test. Use paired per-query rank of the first relevant frame at 768 vs N, and report the count of queries whose rank changes by more than a few places. Or no fix: this is the price; accept "guard against a big loss only". |
| 128d is already known to hurt multimodal (vendor table) | Delete 128d from the plan. Test 256 vs 512 vs 768 only, or just decide: 256d, with the card's 2.8 point MMEB drop as the stated price, or 768 and skip the knob. Cite the card; no experiment needed for 128. |
| Dimension saves no runtime or money | Say so up front and cap the effort: the only non-trivial output is "one global constant". Fold the dimension check into the `find` gate as a 3-row sub-table of the same embed cache rather than a separate concept with its own CSV and per-stage decision rule. |
| Per-stage flip-rate and Jaccard thresholds (2%) are invented | Drop Jaccard; keep coverage radius in 768 space. Pre-register one threshold, or report the numbers and decide by eye. No fix for the arbitrary 2%: this is the price. |
| Screen-recording/layout collapse is lumped as a "side check" | Promote it: it is the real risk for this model and it decides whether the veto is camera-only. Run it at 768 first; truncation is a secondary axis. |
| Maintenance (CSV and query sheet need re-runs) | Add as an optional bench step, not CI, as the concept says; accept the upkeep, or delete the CSV after the constant is chosen and keep only the one-line decision in .okf/decisions. |

## Verdict

**Survives with changes.** Keep it only as a small sub-table of the embed-cache script already needed for `find`, not as a standalone concept. Changes: (a) correct the false claim that prep_qa.py holds reusable questions and count query-writing as the main cost; (b) drop 128d, because the vendor's own table (https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2) shows a 13-point MMEB v2 drop and the card says to validate before adoption; (c) retest only 256/512/768 and treat the sign test as a large-loss guard, not equivalence proof; (d) drop Jaccard; (e) promote the screen-recording check above the dimension question. Realistic expected value: low. The probable answer is "use 256 or 768, no flag", and the knob is deleted either way, which is the only real gain.

Sources used: https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2 (truncation table and best practices, fetched 2026-10-07); local files bench/prep_qa.py, bench/evaluate.py, bench/v01/*.json. I did not find third-party reports of video-frame recall at truncated dimensions for this model (released a day ago); the web search returned only vendor and mirror pages (HF, Ollama, unsloth GGUF, Google blogs, News18).
