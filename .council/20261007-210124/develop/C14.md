## C14 Per-stage MRL dimension chosen by recall, not taste

## The concept
For: whoever builds the EmbeddingGemma 2 work in framesift (Sam plus his agent). It is a one-afternoon bench experiment, not a feature. Truncating an MRL vector to 128/256/512/768 dims is a knob; the pitch is to set it from a measured table instead of an argument. The table is also the first time anyone embeds a bench frame, so it doubles as the gate for proposal 3 (`find`).

What makes it worth doing: the answer may be "one dimension for every stage" (or "128 everywhere"). That deletes the knob and the 6x vector size, which is the outcome that matters under the maintenance-weighted cost rule. The pitch's own framing ("128 for veto/k-center, 768 for find") is a hypothesis; the experiment can kill it. Be honest about the stakes: with a few thousand vectors, 768 d float32 is about 12 MB per video and brute force is sub-millisecond, so dimension saves almost no runtime money. The real prize is the evidence that the veto and k-center decisions do not change when the dimension drops, and the removal of a per-stage setting. If the table shows no difference, the right outcome is to ship one fixed dimension and no flag.

## How it works
1. Step zero (shared with every other embedding test): one script embeds 1 fps frames of the 4 bench videos (animation, lecture, explainer, short film) at 768 d and caches them as .npy. Truncate by slicing the first N columns then L2-renormalising (truncation without renormalising breaks cosine).
2. Queries: do not hand-write 20 from nothing. Reuse the questions in bench/prep_qa.py as text queries and mark each with the time range(s) where the answer is visible (relevant frames = frames inside the range). Add a few queries per video to reach about 20-30 total. Written once, committed.
3. Metric 1 (find): recall@10 and nDCG@10 per query at each dimension. Pick the smallest dimension within 1 point of 768 on the mean, and also report the per-query paired wins/losses with a sign test (parent idea r1.2): with ~25 queries a 1 point gap is inside the noise, so require "no worse on a sign test" rather than trusting the mean alone.
4. Metric 2 (veto): run proposal 1's veto at each dimension on the camera footage with the threshold set per video from codec cuts (parent idea m02.4). Report the fraction of keep/drop decisions that flip versus 768. Flip rate under about 2% means the dimension does not matter for the veto.
5. Metric 3 (k-center): run the greedy k-center for budgets 12/24/48; report the Jaccard overlap of the chosen frame sets versus 768 and the max-min coverage radius measured in 768 space.
6. Decision rule written before running: for each stage take the smallest dimension that passes its metric; if all stages pick the same one, ship that one with no option; if they differ, keep per-stage constants in code (not CLI flags). Result appended to bench/results as a CSV so the choice stays auditable.
7. Side checks the same cache allows: the model's output on screen recordings (does 128 d lose slide-detail more than 768 d? the layout-collapse risk) is reported as a separate row, so the veto stays camera-only if it does.

## What must be true, and the cheapest test
- The model's 128 d prefix keeps ranking on video frames. Google's card says the dimensions are natively supported "with minimal impact on quality" (text benchmarks; nothing for video frames or a bench like ours). Cheapest test: the recall table above. Kill number: if 128 d loses more than 1 point on mean recall AND loses on the sign test, drop the 128 plan and test 256.
- The ground truth is not circular: relevance is by time range of the answer in the video, set from QA, not by the model's own scores.
- Enough queries to see a 1 point gap: they will not give statistical power at 25 queries. Treat the table as a guard against a large loss, not a proof of equality; the sign test and flip rates cover that.
- The model runs locally fast enough on a Mac. Unknown; measure at step zero. If it cannot embed 1 fps of a 10 minute video in a few minutes, every embedding proposal is in trouble, not just this one.
- Vietnamese and screen-recording quality are untested here; English bench only.

## Prior art (with URLs)
- Matryoshka Representation Learning (Kusupati et al.): the original method and the claim that truncated prefixes keep most accuracy, up to 14x smaller for ImageNet retrieval. https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13147
- EmbeddingGemma 2 model card: supports 128/256/512/768 d with "up to a 6x reduction in vector storage costs with minimal impact on quality"; modular text core with optional 170M vision and 300M audio encoders. https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2
- Google Developers blog, developer guide: unified 768 d space for text, code, images, video, audio. https://developers.googleblog.com/embeddinggemma-2-the-developer-guide/
- Warning from a practitioner write-up: MRL training does not mean an arbitrary embedding truncates safely or keeps identical recall on every task, which is why this concept measures instead of assuming. https://supermemory.ai/blog/matryoshka-representation-learning-the-ultimate-guide-how-we-use-it/

Plainly: choosing an MRL dimension by evaluating recall on your own data is standard practice, and Google and the MRL paper already publish the general curves. Nothing found does it for per-stage video-frame selection (veto, k-center) in a CLI like framesift. What is different here is only the application: a stage-by-stage decision with pre-written kill numbers, run on frame sets. I did not find published recall numbers for EmbeddingGemma 2's video/image tower at truncated dimensions; treat that as unknown until the bench runs.

## What it costs to run
- Infra: nothing. No hosted service; one local embed pass during the experiment.
- Ship-time: if the result is one dimension, a constant. If 128 d is chosen, signals.npz vectors shrink 6x (a few MB to under 1 MB per video, a small gain), and the model still has to be loaded to embed, so no per-run compute is saved by dimension.
- Maintenance: the CSV and the query sheet (about 25 queries with time ranges) must be re-run if the model, the sampling rate or the bench videos change; add it to bench/evaluate.py as an optional step, not CI. Risk to name: a per-stage dimension setting is itself a knob, so only keep one if the table forces it.
- Runs: nothing new (a bench script, a query file, a results CSV).
