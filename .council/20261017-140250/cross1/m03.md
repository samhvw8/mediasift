## Builds
### m03.r1.1 Who asserts the claim: a three-tier on-camera test, attached to claims only
Builds on: m03.1 + m07.3 + m06.6
Run m07.3's free voice-to-shot join first; only where it cannot decide (a voice over shots that do not alternate in step) spend m03.1's Apple Vision mouth check, and only on frames under lines that carry a number or name, so the costly tier touches a few seconds per video. Add m06.6's source-vs-score test turned on the voice: a voice whose band and reverb do not match the scene's room tone (studio-dry voice over a street shot) is a narrator, even when a face is in frame. The output is not a tag on every `say` line but one word on each checkable claim (`asserted by: on-camera A` / `narrator over unrelated footage`), which is the first thing a desk writes in a verdict and the only place it pays tokens.

### m03.r1.2 Three hearings of every number: speech twice, screen once
Builds on: m03.3 + m07.6
m07.6 finds numbers the screen says and the voice does not; m03.3 re-hears numbers the voice says. Joined, the on-screen caption becomes a free third, independent witness for every spoken number: when a TikTok says "fifteen percent" and its burned-in caption shows "50%", the card prints `claim @00:12 "15|50 percent" (speech A: 15, speech B: 50, screen: 50%)`, and when all three agree the hedge disappears. Neither parent had a way to break a two-backend tie, or to flag a video whose caption contradicts its voice, which on reels is itself a sign of a re-voiced or re-captioned copy. Judge: TTS clips with scripted numbers and deliberately mismatched captions, scored on wrong-number-stated-as-sure and on mismatch recall.

### m03.r1.3 A local morgue: "first sensed" chains for audio and footage, and the split between them
Builds on: m06.5 + m03.5 + m03.4 + m03.6 + m06.4
Push m06.5 from "series link" to a newsroom clipping morgue: every card stores a music/voice fingerprint, per-shot hashes, the watermark handle and the post's upload date, so a new card can say `audio first sensed in <video> (uploaded 2026-03-02 by @a); this copy uploaded 2026-09-11 by @b`. The new tell, which neither parent had, is the split: the audio fingerprint matches an earlier video while the shot hashes do not (old speech or sound laid on new footage), or the reverse (old footage, new voice-over claiming it is today). That is the commonest misleading reel format, and the morgue grows for free from every `see` the owner already runs. Judge: bench clips re-cut into audio-swapped and footage-swapped pairs, scored on recall and false links. Runs: a few KB per card in the existing cache.

### m03.r1.4 Excerpt alignment: what the clip left out of the source
Builds on: m03.7 + m03.2 + m07.8 + m06.2
Reverse m03.7: instead of claim to moment, take a short clip (the TikTok of a talk) and the long source (found by the morgue or a URL the agent supplies) and align the clip's transcript onto the source's cached transcript. The card for the clip then lists, inside and ±30 s around the excerpt window, the source sentences the clip dropped (`dropped at 00:14: "...but only in mice"`), each cut that joins two non-adjacent source moments (m03.2's seam, now proven rather than suspected), and the chapter it came from by m07.8's slide title or m06.2's sting (`from "Limitations", 41:10`). "Taken out of context" is the verdict a desk most often needs and least often can prove in ten minutes; text alignment over cached transcripts costs nothing until called. Judge: excerpts cut from bench talks with scripted omissions, scored on dropped-sentence recall.

### m03.r1.5 The music bed as a splice detector under B-roll
Builds on: m06.1 + m06.3 + m03.2
m03.2 catches a quote edit only when the picture shows a seam; an editor hides it with B-roll. But a continuous music bed keeps its beat grid and its level, so a mid-sentence break in m06.1's onset grid (the beat jumps phase) or a step in m06.3's measured bed level under one `say` line means the audio was cut even when the picture is clean: `say [voice A] "..." (music jumps beat at 00:12.4 inside this line: audio edited here)`. It reuses signals already computed for the cue block, so it adds no compute, and it turns m06's editorial reading of music into forensic evidence. Judge: the m03.2 splice bench re-made with a music bed under the speech, splices covered and uncovered by B-roll.

### m03.r1.6 Credit line that cross-checks itself
Builds on: m03.4 + m07.6 + m06.4 + m03.6
Three parents each proposed a credit source: watermark OCR (m03.4, m07.6), the post's sound field (m06.4) and the uploader (m03.6). Print one `credit:` line that names all that disagree: `credit: uploader @b; watermark "TikTok @a"; sound "original sound - @c"`. When they all agree, it is one handle; when they differ, the disagreement itself is the re-upload and borrowed-voice signal, with no extra rule. This serves the owner's "script with credit" job and the fact-check job from the same line, and the agent knows which source each name came from.
