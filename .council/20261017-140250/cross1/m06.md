## Builds
### m06.r1.1 The cue sheet as the dub's contract: anydub mixes from it, `check` diffs against it
Builds on: m06.3 + m04.3 + m06.8
The source card's cue block (M1..Mn with in/out, usage and the measured "under dialogue -16 dB") is written to `cues.json`. anydub mixes the Vietnamese voice to it, and `mediasift check DUB --against SOURCE` then senses the dub and diffs the two cue sheets row by row: `M2 missing in dub (00:10-02:00)`, `M1 under dialogue at -29 dB, source -16 dB`, `M3 sting kept`. Neither parent closed the loop: m06.3 only handed the offset over, m04.3 only checked for lost beds. Here one file is both the mixing instruction and the answer key, so every dub grades itself in dB per cue, and the owner's "background lost" complaint becomes a failing row before he presses play.

### m06.r1.2 Two-axis lineage: same picture or not, same music or not
Builds on: m06.5 + m03.5 + m04.2
Store one fingerprint per music cue and one per shot in the same cache index, then read the pair of matches the way a clearance desk reads a cue sheet against picture: same shots + same music = re-upload; same shots + different music or voice = re-cut or re-voiced copy (the Bilibili case from 10-15); different shots + same music = a trending sound reused, a link by audio identity only; same opening cue + different shots = same series or channel. One header line names the case and the matched card with times. Each parent fingerprinted one axis. Together the two axes tell the fact-check and credit jobs *what kind* of copy it is, and that classification is what the agent acts on. Judge: bench clips put through each of the four transformations.

### m06.r1.3 An uncut music bed is evidence the quote is uncut (pushed further)
Builds on: m03.2 + m06.1 + m06.2
m03.2 flags cuts inside a sentence, but B-roll covering a splice hides the picture seam. A music editor knows the music is harder to hide: a bed under speech keeps a steady beat grid, and a splice under it shows as a jump in beat phase, a level step or a phrase that restarts mid-bar. So the music becomes a continuity witness both ways: `M1 continuous 00:00-00:41: no edit inside these 4 lines`, or `beat jumps at 00:12.4 inside a say line: audio edited here even though the picture does not cut`. It reuses the in-music onsets that `sound.py` discards today. Judge: splice music-under-speech clips at known points, on and off the bar, with and without B-roll cover; count hits and false flags on unedited talks.

### m06.r1.4 Spotting session for stills: the owner's screenshots grade the hit points
Builds on: m04.1 + m06.1
A supervisor's spotting notes mark hit points, the frames the editor chose to stress with music. Rank `key stills` candidates by a join of what the card already knows: a music hit or button on a cut, then a long hold after it, then dense OCR text in the held frame. Every screenshot the owner sends with a video (the m04.1 dHash match) is a labelled row, so the ranker's precision is measured on his own picks. m04.1 had the judge without a strong prior, and m06.1 had the prior without a judge. On edit-to-music reels and explainer charts, the frame that lands on the hit is usually the one he screenshots.

### m06.r1.5 Hear the numbers twice where the music is loud
Builds on: m03.3 + m06.3
The ducking measurement already gives music level relative to the voice for every `say` line, and that is the best cheap predictor of where ASR will mishear a digit or a name. Only spans that hold a number or name *and* sit under music above about -12 dB relative to the voice go to the second backend, so the re-hearing budget goes where the risk is. The line then shows `"[15|50] percent" (under loud music)` so the agent knows why it is unsure. m03.3 picked spans by content only. Adding the mix makes the second pass cheaper and more likely to hit. Judge: TTS number clips mixed under music at -6/-12/-18/-24 dB, scored on wrong numbers stated with no hedge.

### m06.r1.6 Fresh: one credit block with three columns, each with its source
Builds on: m06.4 + m03.4 + m04.2
A cue sheet credits title, composer and publisher separately. A reel needs the same split: picture (uploader from yt-dlp, or the watermark handle from OCR), voice (the "original sound - @handle" from the post), and music (the track and artist fields, or "matches M1 of card X"). Each cell carries its source in brackets. When two sources disagree, both are printed (`picture: @abc [watermark] vs @xyz [uploader]`), and that disagreement is itself the re-upload flag. The owner's "credit to him" script job gets the right person per element instead of one name. Judge: 20 real reels with credits read off the page by hand, scored per column.
