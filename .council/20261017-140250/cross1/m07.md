## Builds
### m07.r1.1 On-screen or off-screen: free join first, lip check only where the join cannot tell
Builds on: m07.3 + m03.1 + m03.2
A describer marks a voice as off-screen only when the picture does not already make that clear. mediasift can work the same way, as a cost cascade. First, the voice-letter/shot-letter join (m07.3), which costs nothing, settles shot/reverse-shot dialogue and obvious voice-over. Apple Vision mouth landmarks (m03.1) run only on the say lines the join leaves ambiguous, often a few seconds per video rather than every spoken frame. A jump cut inside one of those lines (m03.2) gets one SDH-style mark on the same line, so the card says `say [voice A, on screen] "..." (cut inside: words may be missing)` and does not spend separate lines on face, speaker and seam. What this adds: the lip model gets a budget (it runs only where the free join fails), and there is a shared judge. Videos with a descriptive audio track (m07.1) already say who is visible when they speak, so the bench can score on-screen/off-screen against a human describer as well as against m03.1's synthetic b-roll clips.

### m07.r1.2 One `claims:` block where each number and name says which witnesses back it
Builds on: m07.6 + m05.2 + m03.3
A fact-checker searches a few claim tokens, not the transcript. Today those tokens are spread over the speech lines, the OCR lines and the subtitle file, and three ideas each mark only their own kind of disagreement. This build gathers every number, date, proper noun and negation in the video into one short footer. Each entry carries its witnesses: `heard` (ASR), `heard twice` (m03.3's second backend agrees), `shown` (OCR or burned-in caption), `subtitled` (platform subtitles), and `[15|50]` where they disagree. Text-only claims (m07.6) appear as `shown` with no `heard`. Two things are new. First, a single ranked list the agent can take straight to web search, with the least-supported tokens first. Second, the second ASR pass runs only on tokens no other witness backs, so a reel with burned-in captions costs no extra speech compute at all. Judge: m05.2's 50 captioned reels plus m03.3's TTS number clips, scored on two counts: wrong tokens marked as fully backed, and claims missing from the block.

### m07.r1.3 The gap map tells silence from lost speech
Builds on: m07.7 + m05.6
A describer never talks over dialogue, even dialogue that is hard to hear. A dubber must not fill a "gap" that really holds a line the ASR missed. Today both would treat the Tears of Steel window at 02:16-02:31 as mostly empty. `gaps.tsv` classifies every speech-free window as `silence`, `music (mood)`, `effects` or `speech heard, not transcribed`. m05.6's flag then becomes a row type in a file that already exists, rather than a separate heuristic, and the card shows only the last class, the dropped-speech warning, as a line. What this adds: one source of truth for two jobs that pull in opposite directions. For the agent, lost speech means hedge or `look`. For anydub, lost speech means keep the original audio there and do not stretch a dubbed line into it. Judge: both of the parents' judges on the same file, m05.6's scripted gaps and anydub's overrun count, plus a new check that anydub never puts a line over a `speech heard` row.

### m07.r1.4 Ten times larger: real sessions say which card lines get used
Builds on: m05.1 + m05.5 + m07.4
m05.1 mines session logs for card failures. Taken further, the same miner records every card line type an agent quoted, cited, acted on or ignored in the owner's real jobs. That turns m07.4's describer omission rules from taste into evidence. A line type nobody used in 30 real jobs (the 49-shot CUTS list, hedged `[owl / vehicle]` ambients, `, male` on every line) is a removal candidate. A missing line that came before repeated `look` calls on the same span is an addition candidate. The bench decides only parity, and m05.5's tokens-per-answered-job meter shows the saving. What this adds: the card's grammar changes from its readers' behaviour, the way describers learn from blind audiences, instead of each line having to argue for itself in a council. Cost: one offline monthly script, no runtime, and the transcripts already exist.

### m07.r1.5 A three-role credit line: who filmed it, whose voice it is, who posted it
Builds on: m03.4 + m03.6 + m07.6
A describer reads the opening credits aloud because the viewer cannot see them. A re-uploaded reel has three credits that are often different people. The picture author is the corner watermark (m03.4). The voice or sound author is TikTok's `track`/`artist`, where "original sound - @x" is someone else's voice (m03.6). The poster is the yt-dlp uploader. Other ideas each found one of these. This build writes all three on one line: `credit: picture @abc (TikTok watermark), voice @xyz (original sound), posted by Page Q (Facebook, 2026-09-30)`, and adds `(re-upload)` when the picture author and the poster differ. What this adds: the owner's "script with credit to its author" job gets the right author when a single uploader field would credit the wrong person. It also gives a fact-check its first lead, because a borrowed voice over someone else's footage is the classic misleading reel. Judge: the owner's real reels plus re-uploaded copies, exact handle match per role, and the line costs nothing when all three roles are the same person (it collapses to `credit: @abc`).

### m07.r1.6 `quote` answers with a name, a face and the other videos
Builds on: m03.7 + m07.2 + m03.5
m03.7's `quote VIDEO "claim"` returns the matching line with voice letter "B". A describer would say the name, and the owner's linking job needs the same moment in the other videos. This build makes `quote` resolve the letter through vocative naming (m07.2, so "Tom said it" instead of "voice D"). It attaches the on/off-screen mark from m07.r1.1 and the one face frame. It then searches every cached card for the same claim, matching words in the transcripts and, through m03.5's shot hashes, the same footage. The output is a citation list across videos: `talk1 @ 12:04 (Lex, on screen), talk3 @ 41:10 (narrator, same b-roll)`. What this adds: one read-time primitive that serves fact-checking (who actually said it, and was the footage reused) and multi-video summaries (link the same point across talks). It costs nothing until called and it reads only cached data. Judge: m03.7's paraphrased-claim set, with a second score for cross-video hits on talks that the owner really linked.
