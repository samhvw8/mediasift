### m08.r2.1 Delete the timeline card: what is left is a contents page and three lookups
Builds on: m08.r1.4 + m01.6 + m07.r1.4 + m06.8
This answers the provocation. Everyone treats the timeline card as the core, so delete it. What is left is `sensed.json`, a contents page of about 10 lines (length, languages, voices with names, music cues M1..Mn, scenes, alarms, credit), and three lookups the agent calls when a question needs them (`grep`, `quote`, `look`), while programs read `cues.json`. Measured on the Tears of Steel card today: 7,353 characters in 97 lines, of which `pic` lines are 2,448 (33%, including one 546-character CUTS line), OCR `text` 1,609 (22%), `say` 1,850 (25%) and `snd` 1,036 (14%). The 47-minute talk and the 1h45 podcast are where the timeline grows without limit, and a summary job reads only a fraction of it. What this adds that the parents lacked: the full card becomes an output the agent asks for when a clip is short enough (`see --full`, and the default under a measured crossover length), not the default. Each job then pays for the lines it actually opens. Judge: m05.5's tokens-per-answered-job meter on real jobs replayed both ways (contents page plus lookups, against the full card), at equal bench score, with the crossover length read off that curve rather than chosen by hand. Runs: nothing new.

### m08.r2.2 Scenes and cues as rows, everything else as columns that pay rent
Builds on: m06.8 + m07.4 + m08.r1.5
m06.8 groups music into cue rows, m07.4 collapses 49 cuts into scenes, and my rent ledger charges each line kind. Together they change the unit of the ledger: the card's rows are scenes (S1..Sn) and cues (M1..Mn), and every other fact is a column on those rows: shot letters, voices present, on/off screen, mood or instruments, ending shape, OCR that is shown but never said. A row costs the same whatever it holds, so the ledger can charge per column with an exact token share. A column with no bench question is dropped from every row at once, which is cheaper to judge than ablating line kinds one at a time. 15% start: rewrite the Tears of Steel card by hand into this shape, count characters against the 7,353 of today, and run the existing bench questions against both versions.

### m08.r2.3 Pushed further: no fixed card shape; the job picks the columns
Builds on: m07.r1.2 + m01.r1.6 + m08.r2.2
m01.r1.6 lets anydub's metric decide which fields stay in `cues.json`, and m07.r1.2 builds a claims block for fact-checks. Taken ten times further, every consumer gets its own field list, chosen by its own metric: `see --for factcheck` shows the claims block and text-only OCR with witnesses first and leaves out mood; `--for dub` writes `cues.json` and prints one line; `--for summary` shows chapters (stings and slide titles) and named voices; `--for bug` shows OCR error text and the UI change events. The skill and the prompt hook already know which job the owner asked for ("verify", "dub", "summarise", "bug report"), so they pass the flag, and the sensing runs once with the same cache. What this adds: the ledger becomes one per job, so a line that is useless for summaries but needed for dubs no longer has to justify itself to both. Judge: each job's own score at fewer tokens than the generic card (fact-check: wrong tokens marked as backed; dub: lines over slot; summary: chapter boundary hits within 10 s; bug: the error string recovered).

### m08.r2.4 One escalation queue with a minute budget instead of five separate cascades
Builds on: m01.r1.4 + m07.r1.1 + m06.r1.5 + m08.r1.6
Four builds each add an expensive step that runs "only where the alarm fires": separation where speech went untranscribed, lip landmarks where the voice/shot join cannot tell, a second ASR pass on numbers under loud music, and the `look` price tags. Run separately, they each spend compute on the same flagged spans and could each grow. Instead, every alarm goes into one queue on `sensed.json` with a priority (number under music, then untranscribed speech, then on/off screen) and an estimated cost. `see` spends at most a fixed budget per card (for example 10% of the card's sensing time, or 60 s on the GPU box), and the rest stays on the card as priced `say?` lines the agent can buy with `look`. What this adds: total cost is capped by one setting however many cascades are added later, and the card always says what was left unchecked, so nothing is dropped silently. Judge: wall time per real card stays within budget, and on the synthetic set (number clips under music, scripted gaps, face present/absent) the share of alarmed errors fixed per budget second.

### m08.r2.5 Fresh: open one tile, not a whole sheet, by who and what
Builds on: m07.3 + m06.r1.4 + m08.5
Images are the largest per-question spend: a frame sheet costs far more tokens than its card lines, and today an agent that wants to see "the woman who says the robot line" opens a full sheet. The voice/shot join (m07.3) and the hit-point still ranker (m06.r1.4) already say which frame matters for a voice, a scene or a cue, so the contents page can carry an index: `B (Celia): tile S4-2; M2 hit: tile S9-1; error dialog: tile 01:12`. `look --tile S4-2` then returns that one crop at full resolution instead of a sheet. What this adds: an image price drops from a sheet to one tile, and the choice of tile comes from data already computed. Judge: images opened and image tokens per question on the real-footage bench at equal score, plus the owner's screenshots (m04.1's dHash match) as a check that the indexed tile is the frame he would pick.
