### m04.r2.1 The ten-year-old version: "the robot underlines what it is not sure it heard"
Builds on: m05.r1.2 + m02.r1.1 + m03.r1.2 + m02.r1.3 (facilitator provocation)
Told to a ten-year-old, the council's leading idea is: "the robot listens for a friend who can't hear; when the video's own subtitles, a second listen, or the amount of talking disagree with what it wrote, it underlines those words and says don't copy them." To say it that simply I had to cut the line leases, the McNemar ablations, the three inline hint styles and the per-source columns, and for the owner they should go for real: his pain is one sentence ("the agent searched a number the video never said"), and he never sees a bench. So ship exactly one mark (`~`) on the doubtful words plus one footer `do not quote: 00:12 "15%" (caption 50%), 02:18-02:31 (heard, not written)`, fed by the caption alignment, the number re-hearing and the words-per-second check. The only bench kept is the one in the sentence: zero underlined tokens reach a web search.

### m04.r2.2 His next move is the label: one joiner over three free by-products
Builds on: m04.r1.6 + m02.r1.5 + m05.r1.3 + m02.5
m04.r1.6 reads the owner's next message, m02.r1.5 reads the search engine's "did you mean", m05.r1.3 saves the URL's chapters and subtitles; none of them alone has enough rows. Join all three in one offline `mediasift gaps` keyed by card and time: a span gets a row when the owner corrected it, when a search rewrote a word from it, or when the platform's own subtitle disagrees with it, and each row records the job kind he was doing (dub, fact-check, summary, credit, bug). What it adds: the first run prints a prevalence count, and if fewer than five real rows exist it says "real-use sample too thin" and recommends nothing, so the system feeds itself only once there is something real to feed on, instead of tuning to two anecdotes.

### m04.r2.3 Links that grow by themselves: every new card is aligned against every old transcript
Builds on: m03.r1.4 + m03.r1.3 + m04.r1.3 (pushed further than they dared)
m03.r1.4 aligns one clip to one source on request. The owner's most repeated words are "link several videos together", so do it for every `see` against the whole cache: a cheap text-shingle index of all cached transcripts (a few KB per card) finds any 15 s run of near-identical speech, and the card gets one line `also said in: <card> @41:10 ("Limitations")`, with the chapter name from m04.r1.3's harvested chapters. The same index answers the morgue's "first sensed" question and the excerpt's "what was dropped" question as by-products. What it adds: the linking job the owner keeps asking the agent to do by hand, re-reading four transcripts at ~150k tokens (m05.5), becomes a line the cache writes for free, and each correct link a later `quote` confirms is a judge row.

### m04.r2.4 A cache that heals the span, not the file
Builds on: m05.r1.1 + m02.r1.6 + m02.r1.2
m05.r1.1 warns that a see-12 card holds a known defect and asks for `--fresh`; m02.r1.6 fails a test when printed commands are dead. From the owner's side, both still leave him with a 47-minute talk he must re-run for one bad span. Instead, on a cache hit the reader checks the card's defects table and the speech-health check, re-senses only the flagged spans (the 15 s ASR loop, the "heard, not written" window) on the current pinned model, patches `sensed.json`, and notes `re-heard 00:12-00:31 with see-13`. What it adds: the cache repairs itself from its own defect list at seconds of cost instead of minutes, the dead-command test becomes the trigger rather than just an alarm, and every before/after patch is a free regression row for the speech guard.

### m04.r2.5 anydub's stems are the answer key the dub check needs
Builds on: m02.r1.4 + m04.r1.1 + m02.7
m02.r1.4 rightly says the dub check is worthless until CED's hearing floor under a voice is calibrated, and proposes mixing 20 synthetic beds. But anydub already separates every real video it dubs into a voice stem and a bed stem, so the true bed level per cue is known on the owner's own footage: re-mix that bed under the Vietnamese voice at -10/-20/-30 dB as a by-product of each dub and the calibration curve grows from real music, real Vietnamese speech, at no extra job. The check then reports in his words: `background lost 01:10-03:40 (bed in source -16 dB, in dub below the -22 dB floor CED can hear)`, and it must reproduce his complained-about file before it is trusted.
