## Builds
### m03.r2.1 The counterview I least want to accept: the desk is empty, and forensic hedges are accusations
Builds on: m03.r1.1 + m03.r1.5 + m02.r1.5 + m02.r1.3 + m05.r1.4
The counterview: in a month of real use the owner fact-checked one TikTok, so a verification desk is a lens on a job that barely exists, and every forensic line (`audio edited here`, `narrator over unrelated footage`, `re-voiced copy`) is itself a confident claim about a real creator that the agent will repeat as a finding. The version that survives it puts zero fact-check lines on the default card: all of them go into m05.r1.4's `claims.tsv` sidecar plus one pointer line, and they are computed only when the agent asks (`see --check` or `quote`), so they cost nothing on the 99% of runs that are summaries. Each forensic flag also carries its measured false-alarm rate from the unedited-talk bench (`cut inside line; fires on 1 in 40 lines of unedited talks`), which turns an accusation into evidence with a stated base rate, and m02.r1.3's deletion rule applies: a forensic flag whose false-alarm rate cannot be measured does not ship. m02.r1.5's join of card tokens to later web searches decides whether `--check` ever becomes default: under 5 real fact-check sessions in a quarter, it stays opt-in.

### m03.r2.2 A do-not-search list that hands the agent the search string
Builds on: m02.r1.1 + m03.r1.2 + m05.r1.2 + m02.r1.5
m05.r1.2's footer tells the agent which tokens not to quote, and m02.r1.1's middle bucket knows the caption spelling, but neither tells the agent what to type instead, so it still searches the misheard word. Each flagged number or name in the footer gets the query to use: `search as: "bát tự" OR "bắt tự"`, `"15%" OR "50%"` (both hearings and the caption OR'd), and the caption form first when the creator typed it. The fact-check failure from m05.2 (a search that "disproves" a video because ASR misheard) becomes a search that cannot miss the right spelling. Judge: m02.r1.5's session join, counting searches whose query held a flagged token verbatim without its alternates; the target is zero, and on the bench, recall of the true token among the OR'd variants.

### m03.r2.3 `check COPY --against ORIGINAL`: the dub checker turned around is a re-voice detector that lists what the re-voicer added
Builds on: m04.r1.1 + m04.3 + m04.r1.2 + m03.r1.4
m04's dub checker compares the owner's dub to its source; the 10-15 fact-check was the same comparison run on someone else's dub (a Vietnamese TikTok re-voicing a Bilibili video). Point the same command at a suspected copy: picture tiles match, bed preserved or replaced, voice replaced, and then the new part, translation fidelity: align the copy's transcript to a translated transcript of the original (m03.r1.4's excerpt alignment across languages) and list the sentences the re-voicer added that the original never said (`added at 00:31: "scientists confirmed..." - not in original 00:20-00:45`). That is the verdict a desk most needs on a re-voiced reel (the copy claims more than its source) and neither parent produced it. The judge is free: every anydub dub the owner already made is a labelled re-voiced copy with its script, and planting 10 inserted sentences into those scripts gives recall and false-alarm numbers.

### m03.r2.4 Peek the candidate originals: settle "who posted it first" at KB cost
Builds on: m04.5 + m03.r1.3 + m04.r1.2
m04.5's `peek` pulls storyboard sprites and auto-captions without media; m03.r1.3's morgue matches shot hashes and audio fingerprints only against what the owner already sensed. Pushed further: when the agent's web search turns up candidate sources (the Bilibili post, three other re-uploads), `peek URL...` hashes their storyboard tiles and caption text against the suspect card, and returns the candidates that match ordered by upload date: `footage matches 2 of 4: bilibili BV.. (2026-10-05, earliest), youtube .. (2026-10-09); voice matches none`. This turns the manual flow-sheet comparison the agent did by eye into one call over a few KB per candidate, and it reaches beyond the local cache without a paid reverse-search API. Judge: bench clips posted as known re-uploads, plus storyboard-only versus full-`see` agreement on match and earliest-date.

### m03.r2.5 Citations that follow the excerpt back to its source
Builds on: m02.4 + m03.r1.4 + m04.7
m02.4's `cite-check` verifies that `@12:31` says what the summary claims, and m04.7 links cards by word overlap. The owner's summarise-and-link job often starts from a clip (a TikTok of a talk), so its citations point at a re-upload that may vanish and whose time means nothing in the talk. When excerpt alignment finds the clip inside a cached long source, `cite-check` also rewrites each citation to the source time and URL (`clip @0:14 = source talk @41:23, chapter "Limitations"`) and warns when the cited sentence sits next to a sentence the clip dropped (`clip omits "...but only in mice" 2 s later`). This adds canonical, context-checked citations to the summary job, which neither parent had, at text-alignment cost over cached transcripts. Judge: bench talks cut into excerpts with planted omissions, scored on correct source-time mapping within 2 s and on omission-warning recall.
