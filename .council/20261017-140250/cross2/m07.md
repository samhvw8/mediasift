## Builds
### m07.r2.1 The kill test first: a speech-only card on the owner's real jobs
Builds on: m08.2 + m08.8 + m07.4 + m07.r1.4
One thing would kill most of this council's picture and sound work: in the owner's real jobs, the agent gets its answers from the `say` lines and ignores everything else. m08.8 already hints at this, with 0 `look` calls and 0 transcript reads outside the bench. So before anything new is built, run the extreme ablation that nobody proposed. Strip a card down to speech, claims and credit, then replay the real job set on it: fact-check the TikTok, summarise the 47-min talk with timestamps, write up the crash recording. Score it against the full card, both for correct answers and on m08.2's tokens-per-correct column. The describer's question "would the story break without it?" then gets one measured answer per job type rather than per line. If speech-only holds on talks and podcasts (the heaviest users), their default card becomes speech-first, with picture and sound lines moved behind `look`. Every picture idea keeps its place only on the job type where removing it broke an answer (screen recordings and speechless reels, presumably).

### m07.r2.2 Gaps ranked the way a describer ranks them: free, coverable bed, or story beat
Builds on: m07.r1.3 + m06.2 + m06.3 + m01.r1.1
A describer will talk over a music bed but never over a plot sound or a sting, because the sting *is* the story beat. The gap map (m07.r1.3) and the ducking map (m06.3) each give anydub room to overrun, but neither says where it must not overrun. This build gives each `gaps.tsv` row a rank. `free` is silence. `bed -16 dB` is music the dubbed line may cover at the measured duck. `beat` is a button or sting (m06.2), or a short sound synced to a `pic` event, and it may never be covered. Vietnamese runs longer than English, so this is the constrained extreme: when a line must overrun, anydub takes `free` first, then `bed`, and condenses the line rather than step on a `beat`. m01.r1.1's invariance check gains one exact rule: every `beat` row in the source must still be audible above the voice in the dub. Kills it if: stings and plot sounds are too rare in the owner's YouTube talks to change a single overrun decision. Count the beat rows on the cached real cards before building.

### m07.r2.3 For captioned reels, the creator's captions become the subtitles of record (pushed further)
Builds on: m08.r1.3 + m01.r1.2 + m02.1
m08.r1.3 deletes the captions that agree with speech. The bolder version reverses who is the witness. On a reel with burned-in captions, the creator has already written the deaf subtitles by hand, in the right language, with the chunking the creator chose. Run ASR on only three caption-bearing lines. If those agree, the OCR caption track becomes the `say` text (`say (from captions)`), and ASR runs only on uncaptioned spans and on the disagreement tokens (`[15|50]`). That skips most of the 3.3 GB MLX pass on the owner's most common real input, short Vietnamese social video, and it fixes the case where ASR does worst (Vietnamese names and the "bắt tự" mishearing). The extreme user served is the most constrained one: the M1 under a heavy run, and a language where ASR is weakest. Kills it if: Apple Vision does not actually read Vietnamese diacritics. `ocr.py` asks for `"vi-VT"`, which may not be a valid code (Vietnamese is `vi-VN`), so the first test is whether Vision accepts it or silently falls back to English. Judge: m05.2's 50 captioned reels, scored on per-token WER of the hybrid track against hand-corrected captions, with seconds of ASR saved per reel.

### m07.r2.4 Chapters only where two witnesses agree, as the cite unit for linking talks
Builds on: m07.8 + m06.2 + m08.7 + m08.r1.4
A describer of a lecture says "new slide", and of a podcast says "the music returns". Each signal works for only one extreme. Slide titles carry the talk and are silent on the podcast. Stings carry the produced podcast and are silent on the slide talk. A camera-angle change (m08.7's 5-angle summary) carries the multi-camera interview. This build gets a chapter boundary from any of the three, prints it only when two agree or one is a sure slide title, and uses it to replace the 300-entry CUTS list on long videos, so the card gets shorter. Each chapter then becomes a cite handle in m08.r1.4's cache grep (`3b5f#ch4 "Memory hierarchy" 04:12-11:30`). "Link these four talks" becomes matching chapters across cards, not reading four transcripts. Judge: YouTube creator chapters from yt-dlp, measured as boundary hits within 10 s, reported separately for talks, podcasts and interviews so that no single extreme hides the others.

### m07.r2.5 The speechless reel: describe the edit, not the pixels
Builds on: m06.1 + m06.7 + m07.5 + m08.4
For the extreme the card serves worst today, a TikTok carried only by music, text overlays and action, a describer would never say "medium gray area changed". They say what the edit does: "quick cuts on the beat, text cards, a crowd, music builds". Every part of that already exists or costs milliseconds: cuts on the beat (m06.1), instruments plus an energy verb (m06.7), the Vision scene classifier on the first frame of each scene (m07.5), and recurring overlay text folded into one line (m08.4). So when a video has under about 10% speech, the card swaps its `pic` geometry lines for one edit block: `edit-to-music montage, 11/12 cuts on beat; drums+synth, builds 00:08-00:14; where: street, night, crowd; text: "..." (5 cards)`. It costs tokens only where speech is absent, which is where today's card has the least to offer. Judge: speechless reels from the owner's cache plus m07.1's describer tracks on trailers, using "what happens in this video" questions graded against the describer's own words.

### m07.r2.6 One voice roster: name, face, pace and credited owner, introduced once
Builds on: m07.2 + m01.7 + m07.r1.1 + m06.r1.6
A describer introduces each person once ("Tom, a lanky man in his thirties") and then uses only the name. Today each voice is spread over the card: a letter on every line (m08.6), a gender in the footer, a name if vocative binding fires (m07.2), a pace on m01.7's casting sheet, and the voice author in the credit block (m06.r1.6). This build gathers them into one roster line per voice: `D = "Tom" (named by C 00:22.9), on screen shot X, 3.1 syl/s, male; C = narrator, off screen, voice of @xyz (original sound)`. After that, every `say` line carries only the bare letter. One roster serves three jobs: the agent cites names, anydub casts voices and sets each speaker's base rate, and the fact-check knows that the off-screen narrator is a borrowed voice. Kills it if: the owner's real videos are mostly one speaker. In that case the roster collapses to a single line, which costs almost nothing, so the remaining risk is only build effort.
