## Builds

### m01.r2.1 The better question: "what must survive when the owner transforms this media?"
Builds on: m01.r1.1 + m08.r1.5 + m07.r1.4
"What does an agent need from media" has no judge, so every line argues for itself. A localization studio asks a narrower question with an answer key: what must stay the same when the video is translated, re-voiced, summarised, quoted or credited? Every one of the owner's real jobs is a transform, and each one names the facts it must keep: the dub keeps the bed, cuts and slots; the summary keeps times and claims; the credit keeps handles; the fact-check keeps numbers and names. So m08.r1.5's rent ledger gets one more column, "which transform's invariant does this line kind protect", filled from m07.r1.4's real-session miner. A line kind that no transform needs to preserve is evicted, and a new sensor earns a card line only by joining an invariant that a `check` (m01.r1.1) can break. The question has a judge built in: run the transform, re-sense the output, and count broken invariants.

### m01.r2.2 Elastic slots: a dubbed line may run into a tail, never across a button, a cut or lost speech
Builds on: m06.2 + m07.r1.3 + m06.r1.1 + m01.r1.5
Assumption flipped: "a dubbed line must fit the source line's slot". Vietnamese often runs longer, and anydub answers by speeding up, which produces the "reading as if it were English" pace (m01.r1.5). A studio editor knows the picture has slack, but only some kinds. From the gap map (m07.r1.3) and the cue endings (m06.2), `cues.json` gives every line an elastic budget: the source slot plus the following gap, ended by the first hard wall, which is a music button, a picture cut while the speaker is on screen (m07.r1.1), or a `speech heard, not transcribed` row. A tail, silence or ambient effects can be borrowed; a wall cannot. Neither parent had this: the gap map listed room and the cue sheet listed endings, but nothing said which room is legal. Judge: anydub's count of lines above 1.15x speed-up and the native-pace score of r1.5, before and after, with zero lines allowed to cross a wall.

### m01.r2.3 Claim tokens are language-invariant, so the dub check needs no translation judge
Builds on: m07.r1.2 + m01.r1.1 + m06.r1.5
Grading a translation needs a bilingual judge, which mediasift does not have. But numbers, dates, @handles, URLs and most proper nouns survive translation unchanged, and m07.r1.2 already gathers them, with their witnesses, into the source's `claims:` block. `check DUB --against SOURCE` runs ASR on the Vietnamese dub and diffs claim tokens per slot: `00:41 source "15%" (heard + shown), dub says "50 phần trăm"`, `01:12 "Altman" missing from dub`. This catches the dub error that hurts most (a wrong number now spoken with a native voice) at the cost of one ASR pass the check already runs. m06.r1.5's under-loud-music flag carries over: when the source token itself was hedged `[15|50]`, the dub row says the source was unsure rather than blaming the translation. Judge: TTS-dubbed bench clips with numbers and names swapped on purpose, scored on recall and false alarms.

### m01.r2.4 A series term base from the cache: one rendering per name, corrected by the other witnesses
Builds on: m08.r1.4 + m08.3 + m07.r1.2
Pushed further than cache grep. A dubbing studio keeps a term base so a recurring name is translated the same way in every episode. The owner dubs and links several videos from the same channels, so `mediasift grep` over every cached `sensed.json` can also return, for each recurring name or term, every way it was heard, shown and subtitled across the cache. When OCR in one video shows "bát tự" and ASR in another wrote "bắt tự" (m08.3's loop), the majority across witnesses and videos is the spelling the summary should cite and anydub should speak. The new parts are cross-video correction of mishearings with no new model, and consistent terms across a series of dubs. Judge: the owner's cached Vietnamese reels with hand-corrected term lists, scored on mishearings fixed and correct terms changed by mistake (which must be zero).

### m01.r2.5 Declared provenance: anydub stamps its output, so mediasift reads the credit instead of guessing it
Builds on: m07.r1.5 + m06.r1.2 + m01.r1.3
Assumption flipped: "provenance must be detected after the fact". For the dubs the owner makes, it can be declared at creation. anydub writes a small metadata stamp into its output (source URL, source card key, the four roles: picture author, original voice, dub voice marked synthetic, music credit). When that file, or a re-upload of it that keeps the container metadata, comes back through `see`, the credit line reads it directly: `credit: picture @abc, voice @xyz re-voiced in Vietnamese (synthetic, anydub), music "Track"`. When the stamp has been stripped, the two-axis fingerprint (m06.r1.2: same shots and same bed, different voice) still says "re-voiced copy of card X", so detection becomes the fallback rather than the only route. This adds a fourth credit role the parents lacked, the dub voice, plus an honest synthetic-voice disclosure for the "script with credit to its author" job. Judge: a round trip, anydub output then `see`, where every role must match, and the same with the metadata stripped.

### m01.r2.6 The bed is a clock: beat continuity checks the dub's own stretching
Builds on: m06.r1.3 + m01.r1.1 + m06.r1.1
m06.r1.3 uses an unbroken beat grid as evidence that a source quote was not spliced. Turned toward the dub: when anydub time-stretches a span to fit Vietnamese, or cuts the M&E to re-time it, the music's beat phase jumps where the source had none. `check` compares the source and dub beat grids per cue and reports `M1 beat phase jumps at 01:03.2 in dub only: bed was stretched or cut here`. That is the defect a listener hears as a "wobble" in the background, which neither the level check nor the slot check can see. It reuses the in-music onsets that `sound.py` already computes. Judge: dubs with known stretches and cuts inserted into the bed at and off the bar, with zero flags on dubs that left the bed untouched.
