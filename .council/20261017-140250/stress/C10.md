## Fragile assumptions

Ranked from most fragile to least.

1. **"Fetches fail often, so the ladder is needed." Shaky. The evidence is real, but it points at a bug, not a missing feature.**
   The 5-of-7 count holds on disk: 3 empty dirs and 2 that hold only `video.en-orig.vtt` in
   `~/.cache/mediasift/url/`. But the session logs show the cause. Every real failure I found
   (sessions `05daf211…` in `~/workspace` and `b442a48c…` in `apps/gym-log`, both 2026-10-13) ends with:
   `ERROR: Unable to download video subtitles for 'en'|'vi': HTTP Error 429: Too Many Requests`.
   That comes after the JS-runtime WARNING. The video itself was fine.
   - **Why it happens.** `fetch()` asks for `--write-auto-subs --sub-langs "en.*,vi.*,orig"`. On a
     non-English video, that requests YouTube's *auto-translated* captions. A yt-dlp maintainer
     confirms that only auto-translated captions get the 429. Manual subtitles and captions in the
     original language do not:
     https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/13831#issuecomment-3712613129
   - **Why the whole run dies.** yt-dlp aborts the video download when a subtitle download fails,
     unless `-i`/`--ignore-errors` is passed. The fix confirmed in that thread is exactly that:
     https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/13831
   - **The concept misreads the code path.** The two caption-only dirs did not reach
     `"no merged video"`. yt-dlp exited non-zero, so `fetch()` raised `"yt-dlp failed"` first. The
     captions on disk are the ones that downloaded before a translated track got the 429.
   - **So the "middle rung lying on disk" is a side effect of the bug.** Fix the subtitle request
     and those runs reach rung 3. Other tools hit the same 429 and solved it at fetch time, not
     with a degraded output: https://github.com/LiskinLabs/autodubstudio/issues/45 ,
     https://github.com/chelaxian/tg-ytdlp-bot/issues/355 .
   - **What is left once it is fixed.** The JS-runtime warning ("some formats may be missing";
     `deno` is still not installed here) and login walls on Facebook/TikTok. Neither is a measured
     failure in the logs yet.

2. **"Agents obey a rung line." Untested.** The concept says so itself, and it is the decisive row.
   - There is a worse version of the risk. Today a failed fetch is a loud crash. The agent knows it
     has nothing and goes to the web page.
   - A rung-1 "card" built from the uploader's description gives that post text a mediasift header,
     a timeline-shaped layout and a cache entry. That could raise the agent's trust in unsensed text,
     not lower it.
   - The card already opens with a `Sensed:` line, and the agents still need the "How to use" block.
     A one-line warning at the top is easy to skim past.
   - Until the A/B in the concept's own table runs, rung 1 could *cause* the failure it targets.

3. **"Per-track revision strings keep the cache honest." Shaky.**
   - For model pins (`QUAN_REV`, `CED_REV`) it is solid: the string can be built from the pin.
   - For heuristics (`grid-v3`, `mood-v1`) it is a hand-bumped constant. The day someone tunes a
     threshold and forgets to bump it, the header says "current" for a stale track. That is false
     assurance, which is worse than saying nothing.
   - The payoff is small by the concept's own numbers: a full re-sense of the 47-minute talk takes
     68-147 s and the 1h45 podcast 246 s, so `--refresh <track>` saves seconds to a couple of minutes.

The solid parts:
- The 2.49 GB of unreachable cards is real. `du` says 2.5 G across 737 dirs, and `content_key()`
  hashes `CARD_VERSION`.
- Cards that print a dead `framesift look` command are real. The real card
  `3b5fd29ca2c9be19/card.md` prints `framesift look` and a path under `tools/framesift/`.

## Pre-mortem

It is 2027-10. Nothing broke loudly. This is the most likely story:

- **Week 1.** The read-time header and `cache prune` shipped and worked. Both are worth keeping.
- **Week 2.** The ladder shipped with rung 1 and the stderr-to-hint table. Around the same time,
  someone fixed the subtitle request (`-i`, or original-language captions only) in a separate
  commit, because anydub hit the same 429.
- **After that, real fetch failures almost stopped.** The ladder fired a handful of times a year,
  mostly rung 1 on Facebook login walls.
- **The "do agents obey it" A/B never ran.** There were too few rung-1 cards in real use to notice
  either way.
- **The storyboard rung was never built.** It was scheduled last and needs an mhtml unpacker. The
  yt-dlp requests for jpg storyboards are still open: https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/2048
  (open since 2021-12) and https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/11386 .
- **The hint table drifted.** yt-dlp's error wording changed twice, so every hint fell back to
  `yt-dlp -U`.
- **The track-revision constants went stale.** Two heuristic retunes (grid, mood) never bumped them,
  so old cards were served as "current".
- **What failed was the honesty layer.** It cost a fixture, a hint table, a revision constant per
  track and a cache-walk test to keep up. It fired rarely, and when it fired on the heuristic tracks,
  it was wrong.
- **The one part that kept paying was the plain one:** re-render the header, split the cache key,
  prune.

## Who loses

- **The owner's maintenance budget.** The rung variants (`rung=2` cache variant), a stderr
  classifier, a known-defects table and per-track revisions are each a knob that must keep up. The
  owner's scoring weighs that above build effort. Their response would be to cut it back to the
  parts that fire weekly.
- **The bench.** Every rung is a new card shape. Each needs its own judge under the house rule ("a
  feature without a judge cannot earn a card line"). The bench owner, who is also the owner, would
  refuse rung 2 until the agreement test exists. That is correct.
- **The sibling `anydub`.** It has its own yt-dlp fetch and hit the same JS-runtime and 429 errors
  (session `tools-anydub/54a40686…`). A mediasift-only ladder leaves anydub broken and splits fetch
  policy across two copies. Anydub's response would be to copy the ladder, and then there are two
  copies to maintain.
- **Agents in a hurry.** A rung-1 card gives them something to summarise instead of a stop. A
  careless agent that would have searched the web page now cites "mediasift" for the uploader's
  claims. This hurts most in the fact-check job ("check the content from this TikTok, then verify"),
  where it is worst.

## Fixes

| weakness | smallest change that fixes it |
|---|---|
| The 5/7 failures come from translated auto-subs getting a 429 and aborting the download | Change `fetch()` in `src/mediasift/see.py`. Ask for manual subs plus *original-language* auto captions only (`--sub-langs` for the original language, or `orig`, not `en.*,vi.*`), and pass `--ignore-errors` so a subtitle failure never kills the video. First write a failing test: a stubbed yt-dlp that exits 1 with the 429 line but leaves `video.mp4` must give a card. Then re-run the 5 failed URLs. Expected result: rung 3 on all YouTube ones. Do this **before** any ladder work; it may remove the need for rung 2 entirely. |
| JS-runtime warning: "some formats may be missing" | `mise use -g deno` on the owner's machines. `fetch()` keeps the WARNING line from stderr and puts it in `s.holes`, so the existing `Not sensed:` line names it. No new rung is needed. |
| A rung-1 card may raise trust in post text | Do not build rung 1 as a card. On fetch failure, keep crashing, with one stderr line: the failure class, the fix hint, and the sentence "nothing in the video was sensed; do not attribute claims to it". If the agent A/B later shows agents do better with a card, add it then. |
| Rung 2 (storyboard) has no judge and a fragile unpacker | Defer it until the subtitle fix has run for a month and `usage.jsonl` shows failed fetches where captions alone were available. If that never happens, no fix is needed: it was never needed. |
| Hand-bumped heuristic revisions go stale | Derive each track revision from a hash of the module source (`events.py`, `visual.py`, `sound.py` mood table) and the model pin, computed at import time. No constant to forget. Announce staleness only; drop `--refresh` (it saves 1-4 min per long file, per the concept's own numbers). |
| Dead `framesift` command on cards that can still be served | Keep the read-time header and the split cache key. They are the strongest part. Note the token cut from shrinking "How to use this card" (~240 tokens) is a separate claim and must pass the sense bench on its own. |
| Fetch logic duplicated in anydub | Fix the subtitle flags in both, and record the yt-dlp flag policy once in `~/workspace/knowledge/` (the 429-on-translated-captions rule is a durable cross-project lesson). Sharing code between them is out of scope. |
| Orphaned 2.49 GB | `mediasift cache prune` (drop dirs whose `sensed.json` `card` version cannot be re-rendered), as proposed. No change needed. |

## Verdict

**The concept survives, with changes.**

- **Keep part 2** (read-time header, `CARD_VERSION` out of the key, `flows`/`refs` in
  `sensed.json`), **`cache prune`**, and **the cache-walk invariant test.** These fix measured,
  recurring damage at no running cost.
- **Replace part 1 (the ladder) with the fetch fix.** That means precise `--sub-langs` plus
  `--ignore-errors`, deno installed, and the JS-runtime warning recorded as a hole. The headline
  evidence for the ladder (5 of 7 failed fetches) is a self-inflicted subtitle 429 with a two-flag
  fix, per the yt-dlp maintainers. Rung 1 as a card is dropped unless the agent-obedience A/B shows
  it helps. Rung 2 is deferred until real logs show a need.
- **Part 3:** keep staleness announcement only, with revisions derived from source and pin hashes
  rather than hand-bumped constants. Drop `--refresh` and the known-defects table until a defect
  actually ships.
