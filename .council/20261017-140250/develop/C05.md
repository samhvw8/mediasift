## C05 The creator's burned-in captions become the speech track and the free judge

## The concept

**Who it is for.** The owner's most common real input is a short vertical social video: a TikTok or
Facebook reel in Vietnamese or English, sent with "listen to and watch this", "check this and verify it",
or "turn it into a script with credit". The agent reading the card has one witness of what was said,
our ASR. On these videos it is often the weakest witness.

**What it is.** Many creators have already typed what they said onto the picture, in the right
language, with names spelled right, digits instead of number words, and the line breaks they chose.
mediasift reads that text with OCR today, then throws it away (`_drop_captions` in `see.py` deletes any
OCR text that shares 60% of its words with nearby speech). C05 turns that deletion into a measurement.
One alignment of the caption track against the ASR words sorts every caption run into one of three
buckets:

| bucket | test | what the card does |
|---|---|---|
| **echo** | caption syllables match ASR (>= ~0.8) | deleted, as today, but now *counted*: the per-video agreement rate is a free WER on real Vietnamese social video |
| **near-miss** | partial match, and the differing tokens are a name, a number or a tone mark | kept on the `say` line as the creator's spelling: `say "... bát tự ..." [heard: bắt tự]` |
| **standalone** | no speech overlaps it | kept as on-screen text: the graphic to translate and the claim to fact-check that was never said aloud |

**The surprising core, kept.** Pushed one step further, the witnesses swap places. When the first few
caption lines agree with ASR *and* the caption has passed an independence check, the caption track
becomes the `say` text (`say (captions) "..."`). ASR keeps the times, fills uncaptioned spans, and is
printed only where it disagrees. The creator wrote the deaf subtitles by hand; mediasift should read
them as the subtitles of record, not delete them as noise.

**What changed while developing it (honest corrections to the pitch).**

1. *The compute saving is gone for Vietnamese.* The pitch said the 3.3 GB MLX pass would run only on
   uncaptioned spans. Vietnamese on the Mac now runs QUAN on Core ML: **1.3 s for the 2:44 reel**,
   peak 2.2 GB (`.okf/decisions/speech-and-speed.md`). Skipping it saves about a second. The case for
   C05 is *accuracy and a judge*, not compute. ASR still runs everywhere; the caption replaces its
   *text*, not its *run*.
2. *The `vi-VT` worry is settled.* I asked Vision on this Mac (macOS 27.0.1, `VNRecognizeTextRequest`
   revision 3): `vi-VT` is Apple's own code and appears in `supportedRecognitionLanguages`; `vi-VN` does
   not. `ocr.py` is right.
3. *The real failure is bigger than names.* On real reel `c264d4bd705ed746` (1:17, Vietnamese, the
   framesift-era card made with faster-whisper large-v3-turbo on the box), ASR wrote the YouTube outro
   hallucination `"Hãy subscribe cho kênh Ghiền Mì Gõ Để không bỏ lỡ những video hấp dẫn"` over the
   first 20 s and `"bắt tự"` about 40 times in a loop at 28-36 s. The burned-in captions on the same
   frames read `"xong rồi các bạn ý hỏi thì mình toàn trả lời là"` and `"thực ra bây giờ mình sử dụng
   bát tự là nhiều ấy"`. The card shows none of that caption text: changed-region OCR picked up only
   fragments (`"ng có"`, `": hơn"`). So the near-miss bucket also needs a fourth case: **ASR line
   contradicted by a caption on the same span**, which is how a hallucination becomes visible for free.

## How it works

All of it runs on data `see` already has, plus one targeted OCR read. No new model.

1. **Find the caption band.** From the OCR boxes in the first ~10 s of speech, a caption track is text
   that sits in one horizontal band (centred, lower or middle third), changes every 0.5-3 s, overlaps
   `say` lines, and is *not* a recurring string. m08.4's fuzzy grouping removes the recurring ones first:
   titles (`"Tử Vi - Bát Tự"`), series cards, `@handles` and platform marks. Those go to one header line
   instead (and the handle feeds the credit job).
2. **Read the band, not changed regions.** Once a band is found, read only that region of interest each
   time the band's pixels change (cheap crop diff), with the existing `ocr.read(path, box=...)`. On long
   videos (> 10 min) read it once per ASR line midpoint instead, which bounds the cost by line count.
   Word-pop captions (the English reel `5687bb2b3ad6663b` shows 1-3 words at a time: "don't know",
   "capacity") are concatenated into runs before alignment.
3. **Align once.** Normalise both sides (Unicode NFC, lowercase, strip punctuation; digits and number
   words mapped to one form, because QUAN writes "năm mươi tám" where the caption says "58%"), split into
   syllables, and align the caption stream to the ASR word stream with a time-windowed edit-distance
   alignment (captions lead or lag speech by up to ~1 s). Score each caption run by syllable match.
4. **Sort into buckets** (table above), plus the *contradiction* case: a caption covers a `say` line
   that it matches below ~0.2, while CED hears speech. Whisper's known outro and loop hallucinations
   ([whisper-ghosts](https://github.com/jcpsimmons/whisper-ghosts),
   [135 collected phrases](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1rlqfd7/we_collected_135_phrases_whisper_hallucinates/))
   land exactly here.
5. **The independence gate (from m02.r2.1).** CapCut's Auto Caption is itself speech-to-text
   ([CapCut help](https://www.capcut.com/help/how-to-recognise-subtitles)), so an unedited auto-caption
   can share our mishearing, and agreement then proves nothing. Signs a caption was hand-edited: it
   differs from raw ASR on fillers, line breaks, casing and digits. Until the error-correlation test
   below passes, the card treats caption and ASR as **one witness, not two**: it prints near-misses as
   both spellings and never claims "confirmed". It also never "corrects" toward a caption that looks
   deliberately misspelt (creators bend words to dodge moderation; the c264 title reads `"Tử Ui"`).
6. **Card output, kept short.**
   - one header line: `captions: burned-in, vi, agree with speech 91% of 212 syllables`;
   - echoes: no lines (as today);
   - near-misses: a bracket on the `say` line, about 8 tokens each;
   - contradictions: `say (captions) "..." [ASR heard something else here]`;
   - standalones: on-screen text, as today;
   - when the gate is passed: `say (captions)` text for captioned spans. Caption digits are shorter than
     QUAN's number words, so this usually *saves* tokens.
   - Crash rather than corrupt: the raw ASR text stays in `sensed.json` and `transcript.txt` for every
     line the caption replaced, and the header says which source each line used.
7. **The free judge.** Every echo and near-miss pair is written to `sensed.json`. A small
   `bench/captions.py` collects them across the cache into a real-Vietnamese and real-English
   caption-vs-ASR set: agreement per backend (QUAN vs mlx-whisper vs faster-whisper on the box), and
   names-and-numbers recall. This is the bench the brief says is missing. QUAN's measured weak spots
   (English names deleted, numbers as words) are exactly what captions spell out.
8. **What it gives each real job.** *Fact-check:* standalones and near-miss tokens are the claims to
   search, spelled the way the creator wrote them. *Script with credit:* caption text is already the
   creator's script, and the handle is in the header. *Dubbing (anydub):* echo spans are where the
   creator's captions will clash with a Vietnamese voice, and the caption chunks are ready-made line
   splits (from m01.r1.2). *Summaries with timestamps:* fewer hallucinated lines to cite.

## First move

This week, offline, with no change to the card: write `bench/captions.py` and run it on the three real
captioned reels already in the cache.

1. For `0a3b3623e0837d04` (2:44 Vietnamese TikTok, yellow-highlight captions), `c264d4bd705ed746`
   (1:17 Vietnamese, outlined captions) and `5687bb2b3ad6663b` (0:26 English word-pop captions), sample
   frames at 2 fps, OCR the band with `ocr.read(box=...)`, and collapse identical reads into runs.
2. Re-sense the two Vietnamese reels with today's default (QUAN) so the alignment runs against current
   ASR, not the framesift-era whisper cards.
3. Align, sort into buckets, and print counts per bucket plus every near-miss and contradiction.
4. The owner (a Vietnamese speaker) listens to 10 near-misses and 10 contradictions and marks which
   side is right. About 30 minutes of listening.

That gives three numbers in one afternoon: OCR syllable accuracy on real caption fonts, caption-ASR
agreement on real speech, and whether the caption side wins disagreements.

## Prior art (with URLs)

Something close exists for the *mechanics*, not for the *use*.

- **submerge** ([github.com/rixwoodling/submerge](https://github.com/rixwoodling/submerge)): EasyOCR on
  hard subtitles plus MLX Whisper, merged by timestamps, confidence, text similarity and "arbitration
  logic" for "fewer hallucinations, better proper nouns". This is the "caption becomes the speech track"
  half, built to produce subtitle files. It does not sort into echo / near-miss / standalone, has no
  independence check, and does not feed an agent card or a bench.
- **OCR'd burned-in subtitles as an ASR reference:** a public PR scores Chinese ASR against OCR of
  burned-in subtitles on one 15 min VOD, reporting ~5% OCR character error and subtitles covering only
  ~58% of the speech ([zkaelxz/U00#1022](https://github.com/zkaelxz/U00/pull/1022)). This is the
  free-judge half, with the same two caveats C05 must handle: OCR is noisy, and captions cover part of
  the speech.
- **Subtitles as weak supervision for ASR:** broadcast subtitles are useful but not verbatim, so they are
  used as context or weak labels rather than ground truth
  ([arXiv 2509.04491](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04491v1), [arXiv 2502.03212](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03212v1)).
  This supports the "one witness until proven" rule.
- **Hard-subtitle extraction tools:** Subtitle Edit's Video OCR
  ([subtitleedit.github.io](https://subtitleedit.github.io/subtitleedit/features/video-ocr.html)) and
  videocr on macOS Vision ([github.com/peterc/videocr](https://github.com/peterc/videocr)) pull the text
  out but do not compare it with speech.
- **Why captions can share ASR's errors:** CapCut's Auto Caption is speech-to-text
  ([capcut.com/help/how-to-recognise-subtitles](https://www.capcut.com/help/how-to-recognise-subtitles)).
- **Why the contradiction case matters:** Whisper writes outro phrases ("subscribe", "thanks for
  watching") over silence or music because subtitle files in its training data end that way
  ([whisper-ghosts](https://github.com/jcpsimmons/whisper-ghosts),
  [yage.ai explainer](https://yage.ai/share/whisper-repetition-hallucination-en-20260526.html)). The c264
  reel shows the same thing in Vietnamese.

**Still different:** no tool found uses the disagreement itself as the output for an agent (the spelling
fix and the never-spoken claim), gates the caption on measured independence, or builds a real Vietnamese
ASR bench out of a cache as a side effect of normal use.

Not researched: how often TikTok or Facebook exposes the creator's captions as a separate track that
`yt-dlp --write-subs` can fetch. Risk: for URL inputs, a text track may exist that is cheaper and exact,
which would make band OCR unnecessary for those videos. Check with one `yt-dlp --list-subs` on a
captioned TikTok URL before building step 2.

**Runs:** nothing new as a service. It adds one OCR read pattern inside `see`, one module, and one
offline bench script.
