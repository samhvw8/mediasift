## Baseline (the obvious answers)
- Pull the claims out of the transcript (numbers, names, dates) so the agent can web-search them.
- Keep the source URL and timestamps on every line so a summary can cite `@ 01:12`.
- Translate Vietnamese speech and on-screen text so English sources can be checked against it.

## Assumptions I broke
- "The evidence is in the pixels and the sound": a verification desk spends half its ten minutes outside the content: who posted it first, when, what the file's own tags say, whose watermark sits in the corner, whether the audio is a reused sound and whether the footage appeared earlier somewhere else. mediasift already touches all of this (yt-dlp, ffprobe, OCR, the 32x18 grid) and throws it away.
- "A transcript is a transcript": for a fact-check, almost all the risk sits in a few tokens: numbers, names and places. ASR is weakest exactly there ("fifteen" vs "fifty", "mười lăm" vs "năm mươi"). A fluent line with one wrong number is worse than a line that says it is unsure, because the agent will "verify" the wrong claim.
- (Reading picked: "verify" means the owner's TikTok job: confirm or debunk what a short social video asserts, and say what in the video supports each verdict. I read "ten minutes" as the agent's budget: the card plus at most a few `look` calls.)

## Ideas
### m03.1 Is the voice on screen? (Reverse)
Reverse the question from "what is said" to "who visibly says it": for each `say` line, check whether a face in frame moves its mouth with the speech envelope (Apple Vision face landmarks, only on frames under speech, 2-4 fps, macOS; Linux skips and says so). The line becomes `say [voice A, on screen]` or `[voice A, off screen]`. Voice-over laid on unrelated footage is the most common misleading format on TikTok and reels, and the agent cannot notice it from 1 fps tiles. Judge: a synthetic bench of the same audio as talking head, over b-roll, and shifted 2 s out of sync.

### m03.2 A cut inside a sentence means words may be gone (Modify)
The card already has shot letters, so a cut from A back to A (a jump cut) during one `say` line, or a step in room tone mid-sentence, is cheap to flag: `say [voice A] "..." (cut at 00:12.4 inside this line: words may be missing)`. A desk always asks "is this quote edited?", and right now the agent sees two clean lines and no seam. Judge: splice long talks at known points (with and without B-roll cover) and count hits, plus false flags on unedited talks.

### m03.3 Hear the numbers twice (Eliminate blind trust in ASR)
After ASR, pick only the spans with digits, number words or capitalised names (a few seconds per video) and run a second backend on them (the other local model, or the GPU box with `--on`). Where they disagree, print both: `"[15|50] percent"`, `"[Hà Nội|Hà Nam]"`. Cost stays tiny because only claim-bearing spans are re-heard, and the card gains a hedge exactly where verification fails. Judge: TTS clips with scripted numbers and place names in English and Vietnamese, scored on wrong-number-stated-as-sure.

### m03.4 Watermark trail and credit (Put OCR to another use)
Text that stays in the same corner for most of the video is not content, it is a handle or platform mark: `TikTok @user` inside a Facebook reel means a re-upload, and that handle is the original author to credit (the owner's real "script with credit" job) and the first place to look for context. One header line: `watermark: "TikTok @abc" (bottom-right, 00:00-00:41)`, and those boxes drop out of the `pic` text lines, saving tokens. Judge: the owner's real reels plus re-uploaded copies, exact handle match.

### m03.5 Shot fingerprints across everything already sensed (Adapt reverse image search)
Store a perceptual hash per shot from the 32x18 colour grid `see` already computes, keyed in the cache. A new card can then say `shot C also appears in <other video> @ 01:12 (sensed 2026-09-20)`, which catches recycled footage and also links the owner's several videos on one topic. For the open web, write one clean keyframe per shot with overlays cropped out (`search_NN.jpg`) ready for a reverse image search through the browser. Judge: re-encoded, cropped, mirrored and captioned copies of bench clips, scored on recall and false matches.

### m03.6 Provenance header from data mediasift already downloads (Combine)
`fetch` runs yt-dlp but keeps only the video. The info JSON holds uploader, upload date, title, description and, on TikTok, `track`/`artist` (an "original sound" from another account means the voice belongs to someone else). For local files, ffprobe gives `creation_time`, make/model, encoder and Apple's ISO 6709 location. One or two header lines, zero compute, and they answer "when and where was this filmed, and by whom" before anything else. Judge: fixture files with known tags, plus a check that the header stays at two lines or fewer.

### m03.7 `mediasift quote VIDEO "claim"` (Reverse: claim to moment)
A desk starts from a claim, not a video: given a sentence (from the user, a web article or another video), return the matching lines word for word with ±1 line of context, the speaker letter, on-screen text at that moment, one frame of the speaker, and a ready citation (`URL&t=73`). It serves fact-checks and "summarise with timestamps, link several videos" with one primitive, and costs nothing until it is called (it reads the cached transcript). Judge: a question set of paraphrased claims against cached real talks, scored on time hit within 2 s.

## The provocation I would put to the group
The card should say what can be checked and how sure each word is, not what the video feels like: one wrong number stated with confidence costs the owner more than every missing mood word put together.
