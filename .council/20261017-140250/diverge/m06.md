## Assumptions I broke
- "Describing music means naming its mood and genre": a music supervisor's cue sheet barely has a mood column. Its columns are cue number, in/out timecode, usage (source vs score, featured vs background), what the cue syncs to (hit points, button, tail) and title/composer/publisher (credit). Mood is the least actionable column. Placement, sync and identity are what someone downstream (an editor, a dubber, a lawyer, an agent) acts on. Today's 88% mood work is the right foundation, but it is one column of six.
- "Music is background, and speech and picture are the content": on a cue sheet every music entry is an editorial decision. Where the editor started music, cut it, ducked it or landed it on a cut marks where the video thinks something matters: chapter turns, emphasis, an ad read, a reel's punchline. For "summarise with timestamps" and "link several videos", music is structural metadata about the whole video.
- (Bonus) "A card is about one video": cue sheets roll up across a series. The same intro cue across a channel's uploads is the cheapest link between videos there is.

Reading I picked where the brief is ambiguous: "what the agent needs from media" = what lets it act (cite, credit, re-voice, chapter), not what lets it describe the feeling. All ideas reuse signals `see` already computes (CED scores per window, RMS dB, onsets, cut list, speech lines) unless stated.

## Ideas
### m06.1 Cut-on-the-beat and hit points: the edit's rhythm as one line
`sound.py` already finds onsets inside music and discards them as "beats are not events". `see.py` already has every cut. Join them. If most cuts land within ~80 ms of a music onset, say so once: `pic CUTS ... (11 of 12 on the music's beat)`. Mark a cue whose entry or exit coincides with a cut (`enters on cut G`, `buttons on cut A`). For short social video this is the single biggest tell that a clip is an edit-to-music montage rather than footage, and a music hit on a cut is where the editor put the emphasis. It is not obvious because both signals exist in separate modules and nobody asks the supervisor's question, "what does this cue sync to?". Judge: synthetic clips cut on/off beat with scripted answers, and the hit-point recall on Tears of Steel.

### m06.2 Ending shape: button, tail, or cut off (stings mark chapters)
A supervisor marks how every cue ends: a button (hard stop on a hit), a tail (fade or ring-out over seconds), or cut off by the picture. Classify it from the RMS slope over the last 3 s of each bed (abrupt drop within 0.3 s vs a steady decline vs ending on a cut time). A short scored cue with no speech that ends on a button is a sting. In podcasts and talks, stings and intro/outro beds are the produced segment boundaries. That gives the "summarise with timestamps" job chapter candidates no transcript-only method sees. Judge: synthetic beds with scripted endings, plus 3 podcasts with published chapter times (YouTube chapters exist and are free ground truth via yt-dlp).

### m06.3 Ducking map for re-voicing: "under dialogue (-16 dB)" handed to anydub
Cue-sheet usage separates background (under dialogue) from featured (up full). Measure it: inside each music bed, compare RMS in the speech gaps (music alone) with RMS during `say` lines (music plus voice) to estimate music level relative to the voice, per cue and per ducking change. Write it to a sidecar (`cues.json`) that anydub reads to mix the Vietnamese voice over the original M&E at the same relationship, instead of dropping the background. This answers the owner's literal complaint. It is not obvious because the card is for reading, while this is a machine hand-off from the same measurement. Judge: synthetic mixes at known offsets (-6/-12/-18/-24 dB), error in dB. Card cost: a few words per cue. Runs: nothing new (the RMS is already computed).

### m06.4 Needle-drop credit column from the post itself
The cue sheet's title/composer/publisher column is the credit the owner needed for the shared reel script. For TikTok, YouTube and often Instagram, yt-dlp's metadata already carries `track`, `artist`, `album`, and TikTok's "original sound - @handle". `see` downloads with yt-dlp but keeps no info JSON today. Keep those fields and print one line on the bed they cover: `snd [music] needle drop: "Track" - Artist (from the post's metadata)`. No fingerprinting and no API, so no cost. The tag says where the name came from, so the agent does not over-trust it. Judge: 20 real social URLs with the sound name read off the page by hand.

### m06.5 Recurring cue = series link (local fingerprints across the cache)
Cue sheets roll up per series, and the same theme opens every episode. Fingerprint each music bed (Chromaprint `fpcalc`, a few-MB C binary, or a hash of CED's own per-window embedding to add zero dependencies) and store it next to the card. When `see` runs, report `M1 also opens 4 cached videos: <titles>`. This links videos (asked many times) by production identity rather than topic, so a channel's episodes and a creator's reels cluster for free, and a reused trending sound flags "same sound as video X". Judge: a held-out set of 10 episodes from 2 channels plus 10 unrelated videos, precision/recall of links. Runs: one small file per card, local only.

### m06.6 Source vs score (diegetic) as a usage column
A cue sheet's usage code separates music heard in the scene (a radio, a phone speaker, a busker) from music laid over it. The cheap tells are already in the data: source music co-occurs with CED classes like `radio`, `telephone`, `crowd`, room reverb or a band-limited spectrum (high-frequency roll-off above ~4 kHz from a phone speaker), and it is usually cut mid-phrase by edits, where score buttons on a cut. One word, `(source)`, tells the fact-check and summary jobs that the music is part of what happened, not commentary. The same test applied to speech (voiceover vs on-camera, VO vs OC on a cue sheet) tells the agent whether the TikTok's claim is narrated over stock footage. Judge: synthetic clips with phone-band-limited vs full-band music, plus a handful of real reels.

### m06.7 Describe the cue by instruments and energy, not adjectives
A supervisor briefing a composer says "low strings drone, pulse enters, builds to full drums at the cut", never just "epic". CED already scores the AudioSet instrument classes (strings, piano, synthesizer, drum kit, choir, brass) in every window, and they can be benchmarked on AudioSet eval exactly as mood was (same 742-clip method, same script). Pair the top 1-2 sure instruments with an energy verb from the RMS/onset-density slope (`drone`, `pulse`, `builds 01:02-01:08`, `drops out`). This is the answer to "epic/heroic" that has a judge today: the agent can infer "epic" from "choir, brass, drums, builds" with its own world knowledge, and every word is measured. Judge: AudioSet eval instrument precision at a threshold (target >= 85% when stated), and the build verb on synthetic swells.

### m06.8 A cue block instead of scattered `snd [music]` lines
Group music into numbered cues (M1, M2, ...) with one line each: in-out, usage, ducking, ending, sync point, mood/instruments, credit. Each later idea fills a column instead of adding a line, so card tokens grow by columns, not by rows, and agents can cite `M2` the way they cite `@ 00:11.9`. On Tears of Steel this replaces 3 music lines with 3 richer ones. It is the format decision that keeps ideas 1-7 within the "add a line only when a question needs it" rule. Judge: the existing sense bench must not lose any answers, and the token count of the music lines must not grow by more than the bench questions it newly answers.

## The provocation I would put to the group
Stop asking what the music feels like and ask what the editor did with it, because entry, exit, ducking and hit points are measurable today for free, and they tell an agent where the video's structure, emphasis and credits are better than any mood word can.
