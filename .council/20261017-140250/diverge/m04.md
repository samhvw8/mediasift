## Baseline (the obvious answers)
- Put vocal sounds on the speech line they overlap (`say [laughing] "..."`), so the agent knows how a line was said.
- Let `fix` take mood and speaker corrections so real use grows a test set.
- Send one hard span to an audio LLM on the GPU box when the card is not enough.

## Assumptions I broke
- "The user is the agent": the brief says the card is for an agent that cannot watch or hear. The session logs show the agent is only the middle of the chain. The person who suffers when the agent cannot hear is the owner, and today the owner fills the gap with his own ears and eyes. He hears that the dub is wrong ("sao nghe như người việt đọc tiếng việt bằng cách đọc tiếng anh?", 09-23; "tiếng anh nhỏ quá ... mất đi tiếng background", 09-25). He sees that a tutorial uses a bench ("trong vid youtube hướng dẫn bảo thế", 10-13, gym-log). He takes the screenshot of the frame that matters himself (IMG_4191.PNG sent with two TikToks, 09-29, menhly). So the best feature is one that takes a QA job off the owner, not one that adds a richer label.
- "Media in means input": everyone assumes mediasift reads videos other people made. In the owner's real jobs half the media is the agent's own output: anydub dubs, ElevenLabs voice-overs, the encoded copy "quá nặng để share". Nobody checks that output except the owner's ear. The same sensor pointed at its own output gets a judge for free, because the input (the source card and the dub script) is the answer key.

Reading I picked: "real use" means the owner's Claude Code sessions from 09-17 to 10-17 (I read them under `~/.ccp/profiles/shared/projects`), not the 730 bench cards. Two numbers that shaped my picks: of 41 real `look` calls with a card still in cache, 40 zoomed on a span where the card already had a line, so the card does steer attention well and is not the pain. And no card records who posted a video: `~/.cache/mediasift/url/*/` keeps only `url.txt` and `video.mp4`.

## Ideas
### m04.1 The owner's screenshot is the answer key: learn "key stills" from the frames he picks
The moment: on 09-29 the owner sent two TikToks *and* a screenshot of one frame (a chart laid out by year). What he wanted to learn from was that frame, and the agent could not have found it. Whenever a session has a video and an image together, mediasift can match the image to a frame (a dHash over frames it already decodes) and log "the human picked 00:41.2" as a bench row. That builds a ranker for "frames worth a screenshot" (long holds, dense OCR text, a chart or table) from by-products the picture pass already computes, and the card gets a `key stills:` line with 2 or 3 times. The judge grows itself: each screenshot the owner sends is one more labelled example, so he never has to point twice.

### m04.2 Who made it and where it came from: credit and provenance from the cache
The owner's words: "share to another but i still make credit to him" (FB reel, 09-23) and "give me the source" (10-15 fact-check). In the fact-check the big finding was that the Vietnamese TikTok was an uncredited, re-voiced copy of a Bilibili video from 10-05. The agent found that by comparing flow sheets by eye. Two steps, both from by-products. (a) yt-dlp already fetches the uploader, title, upload date and canonical URL, so keep them and print one credit line in the card header (`by @79sdkhaisangtrithuc, posted 2026-10-14`). (b) Store a small perceptual hash per flow tile with every card. Then any new `see` answers "this footage matches card X 00:03-01:10, voice track differs" without anything new running. The more the owner watches, the better the cache becomes as a reverse video search, and the judge is free: re-uploads and crops of bench clips.

### m04.3 Ear for the agent's own output: `mediasift check DUB --against SOURCE`
The owner had to say "the English is too quiet, the Vietnamese nearly covers it, the background sound is lost" (09-25), and before that "it sounds like a Vietnamese person reading Vietnamese as if it were English" (09-23). Both were knowable before he pressed play. Diff the two cards: music and sound beds in the source that are missing from the dub ("background lost: [music] 00:10-02:00"), the loudness of the original speech under the dub, and an ASR pass on the dub in `vi` scored against the dub script anydub already has. A high word error rate against your own script is a cheap stand-in for "wrong accent or wrong language model", and it needs no emotion or prosody model. It costs one extra `see` per dub, and the judge is built in because the script is the answer key. This is one call anydub can make before it tells the owner "done".

### m04.4 Harvest the owner's complaints into the bench, not just the agent's `fix`
`fix` needs the agent to know it was wrong. But in the logs the corrections come from the owner, in Vietnamese, in the next message ("hình như vẫn cần bàn", "sao nghe như..."). The prompt hook mediasift already ships can notice a user message that follows a `see` within one session and disputes it. It writes a pending row (video key, time if one was said, the owner's sentence) to `~/.cache/mediasift/complaints.jsonl`, and the agent turns it into a bench question when it is confirmed. Real pain then becomes the test set in the owner's own words. Today's measured gap ("no test set exists for 'epic' on real film scores") fills itself only with the questions he actually asks. The repo stays free of personal data: the rows live in the cache, and only the scrubbed questions are copied into `bench/`.

### m04.5 Peek before fetch: vet many candidate videos without downloading them
The moment: in gym-log (10-13) the agent had to check whether four YouTube tutorials "really show the exercise without a bench. Titles alone aren't enough, and that mistake is how we got here". YouTube then rate-limited the downloads, and the agent fell back to hand-building strips from YouTube's own preview frames. Make that fallback the first step: `mediasift peek URL...` pulls the platform's storyboard sprite and auto-captions (a few KB, no media) for N candidates and writes a small card for each. A full `see` runs only on the winner. The judge: run peek and see on the same bench URLs and check that peek gives the same answer to "does it show X" questions. Every full `see` after a peek adds one more agreement row, so the bench grows itself.

### m04.6 The card says what it could not sense, in the owner's job words
The owner's jobs are verify, credit, re-voice, summarise and bug repro, not "sense". When `see` finishes, it already knows the gaps: speech in a language the ASR was not sure of, OCR off on Linux, music over speech that hurt ASR confidence, a vertical reel whose burnt-in captions were cut off. One footer line turns these into job warnings: `for re-voicing: speech sits under loud music 00:20-00:48; for fact-check: 3 on-screen numbers were not in the speech`. These come from signals the pipeline throws away today, so nothing new runs. The judge: on the real-footage questions, do the warned spans line up with the questions the card got wrong.

### m04.7 One video across many cards: the owner's "link several videos" from the cache, not from embeddings
"extract transcript then detail summary then link then add it to artifact" (10-02) was asked many times. Each time the agent rereads whole transcripts to find where talks touch. Every card's transcript is already on disk. A plain word-overlap pass over cached transcripts (BM25, no model) can add one line to a new card: `also in: card Y 12:30 ("same claim"), card Z 03:10`. Results are limited to cards the owner has already seen, so the links point to his own library and each one has a time to cite. The judge: the owner's past "link these" sessions give the pairs he expected.

## The provocation I would put to the group
The owner is the only working ear and eye in the loop today (he hears the bad dub, sees the bench, screenshots the key frame), so stop asking "what else can the card say" and ask "what does he still have to check by hand after the agent says done", because each of those checks is a free judge we keep throwing away.
