## Baseline (the obvious answers)
- Put vocal sounds on the speech line they overlap (`say [laughing] "..."`) and add a music build/peak finder.
- A multi-video command that links several cards (summarise and cross-reference talks with timestamps, for the YouTube jobs).
- An on-demand audio LLM on the GPU box for one span ("what is the tone here?"), off by default.

## Assumptions I broke
- "The benches already prove the card is right, so new work is about new senses": the real-footage key is written from mediasift's *own* transcript (`bench/sense/real.py` `transcript()` reads `sensed.json`), and arms B and C get that same transcript. A speech error therefore costs zero points. Vietnamese real-speech accuracy, the owner's most-used sense, has no reference at all ("2:44 reel, no reference, about even by reading"); the only Vietnamese WER (5.45%) comes from a clean TTS dub. The weakest-measured part is the most-used part.
- "Real use will grow a test set if we let `fix` take more kinds of correction" (the assistant's idea 3): `~/.cache/mediasift/feedback.jsonl` does not exist. In a month, `usage.jsonl` logs 1013 `see` and 61 `look` events, and `fix` was never run. The measured rate at which agents file corrections is 0, so widening `fix` to mood multiplies zero.
- (bonus) "A sense that scores well on its own bench earns its card line": today's mood words were judged on 10 s AudioSet clips. No real-footage question was shown to need them, and the house rule says a line is added only when a measured question needs it. Nobody has measured whether they change any answer.

## Ideas
### m02.1 Burned-in captions are free ground truth for Vietnamese speech
Many TikToks and reels the owner sends carry creator-typed captions, and the OCR pass already reads them with times. Aligning OCR caption text to the ASR line it overlaps gives a WER and a names-and-numbers recall on *real* Vietnamese and English social video, with no human and no new model. This matters because QUAN's measured errors are deletions of English names (ChatGPT, OpenAI) and numbers written as words. Those are exactly the tokens a fact-check needs, and they are invisible to today's benches. Cheapest settling test: grep the owner's ~7 real cached cards for `text appeared` lines that overlap `say` lines. If 3 or more clips have caption overlap, a real-speech judge exists today. If none do, fall back to YouTube creator-uploaded (not auto) subtitles via `yt-dlp --write-subs`.

### m02.2 An "untranscribed speech" alarm: compare what the tagger heard with what ASR wrote
The card claims "the timeline is complete for what the sensors detect", but the Tears of Steel card shows `02:16.1-02:31.6 "It's not my fault, you know."` (6 words in 15 s) and `03:45.7-03:50.7 "Tom."` (1 word in 5 s). That is speech heard and not written. CED already scores the Speech class in every window, so seconds where speech is high and ASR words are sparse can be listed as `say? speech 02:18-02:31, not transcribed - look or re-run --on HOST`. It costs no new model and follows the crash-rather-than-corrupt rule. Cheapest test: Blender publishes official subtitles for Tears of Steel. Diff them against the card's 29 lines and count words the card dropped, then check that the alarm fires on those spans and stays quiet elsewhere (precision and recall on one film, an afternoon's work).

### m02.3 Break the circular oracle: key real-footage questions from an independent transcript
Rewrite `real.py oracle` so the writer agent gets a transcript mediasift did not make: official SRT (Tears of Steel), the lecturer's captions (CS50), or whisper-large on the GPU box for the Vietnamese reel. Arm C keeps mediasift's own transcript. Then speech mistakes finally cost points, and the real bench measures speech instead of assuming it. Cheapest test: re-grade the *existing* r15/r17 answers against keys rebuilt from the independent transcript, with no new arm runs. If no grade changes, speech is fine on these 6 videos and we have learned that cheaply. If grades drop, the bench has been overstating C.

### m02.4 A timestamp-citation checker for the owner's actual job (summarise and link with times)
The owner's most-repeated request ends in a summary with `@ 12:31` citations across several videos, and nothing checks that a cited moment says what the summary claims. `mediasift cite-check summary.md` would read each `@time` and its sentence, pull the transcript and OCR within ±5 s from the cached `sensed.json`, and flag citations whose content words are absent. It is deterministic, adds no card tokens, and the agent runs it before replying. Cheapest test: take 3 real summaries from session logs, plant 20 timestamps shifted by 30-120 s, and measure how many it catches against false alarms on the untouched ones. That is a number for the job itself rather than for a sense.

### m02.5 Mine Claude Code session logs as the real-use bench (replacing `fix` as the label source)
Corrections already happen, but in chat ("no, she said ...", "that's not what the video says"), not through `fix`. A local script over `~/.claude/projects/*framesift*|*mediasift*` and other projects' sessions can find mediasift runs followed within N turns by owner pushback, and extract (video key, time, card claim, correction). Cheapest test: one grep pass that counts such turns. Ten or more gives a real-use regression set built from the owner's own failures. About zero means agents' answers are either right or never checked by the owner, and either way widening `fix` is wasted. Runs: nothing new (an offline script, local files only).

### m02.6 Ablate a line before keeping it: the mood-word test
Run the real-footage bench twice on see-12, with mood and genre words and without them, and add 4 to 6 questions whose key comes from the owner's own rating of 10 real clips ("does this music feel scary / sad / none?"). If no question flips and the owner agrees with fewer than ~8 in 10, the words cost tokens on every music bed for no measured gain, and they move behind `--mood` or off the card. Tears of Steel reads `scary` on all three beds, including the tender breakup dialogue scene (00:42-01:09), which is one observation already pointing at "not discriminating". With noise at 1-3 questions in 32, the bench is too small to see a 1-question gain. So the owner-rated 10 clips are the real judge, and they take about 10 minutes of his time.

### m02.7 `mediasift compare ORIGINAL DUB`: turn "too quiet, background lost" into two numbers
The dubbing complaint is a sensing problem for an agent that cannot hear: it cannot tell whether anydub's output lost the bed or buried the voice. Two numbers settle it. Per 10 s span, report speech loudness against the bed in the original and the dub (ffmpeg `ebur128` on the separated or mixed track), and report CED's non-speech presence (music, ambience classes) in each. Print `bed lost 01:10-03:40 (music 0.41 -> 0.02)` and `voice -9 LU vs original`. Cheapest test: build a dub with the bed deliberately removed and the voice at -10 dB; both must be flagged, and the owner's real complained-about file must reproduce the complaint. Runs: nothing new (ffmpeg plus the tagger that already runs).

### m02.8 Stale cards point agents at a command that no longer exists
733 of 737 cached cards say `framesift look "..."`, and `framesift` is not on PATH. The cache key covers file size, head and tail bytes, and `CARD_VERSION`, not the tool name or the model revisions. So a cache hit after today's rename (and after any un-bumped ASR pin change, e.g. `QUAN_REV`) serves a card whose zoom command fails and whose speech came from an older model. Render command hints at print time from the current binary name, and fold `QUAN_REV`, `CED_REV` and the ASR model id into the key. Cheapest test: `mediasift see samples/tears_of_steel.mp4` today, then grep the printed card for `framesift`. It is a one-minute observation, and its failing state is the regression test.

Before we give the agent a new sense, prove that the senses it already trusts are right on the owner's real videos: today the most-used sense, Vietnamese speech on social clips, is graded by nothing, and the bench grades the card's speech against itself.
