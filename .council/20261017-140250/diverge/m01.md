# m01 - Dubbing engineer, assumption reversal

Reading I picked: "re-voice" means the sibling tool `anydub` does the dubbing; mediasift's job is to
be the **sensor a localization studio would want before and after the mix**, not to synthesize audio.
Evidence I worked from: anydub today runs one TTS voice per language for every speaker
(`cli.py`: `lang:voice`), fits lines to a slot "from its start to the next line's start", splits
voice/background with demucs htdemucs on CPU (~2 min per 5 min of audio on the M1 Pro, MPS fails),
and ducks the original voice to 0.3 under the dub, which came back only after the owner said "the
English is too quiet ... the background sound is lost" (`anydub/.okf/architecture/pipeline.md`).
mediasift already senses most of what a studio's spotting list holds: speaker letters with
male/female, `[radio]` on a line (Tears of Steel 00:01.8, the "main engine start" line), music beds
with mood, laughs/shouts/crying/singing as sounds, burned-in text by OCR, cuts. None of it reaches
anydub.

What a studio separates, times and keeps, in one line: it **separates** dialogue from M&E (music
and effects); it **times** every line against the picture (on-camera mouth vs off-camera, cuts,
breaths) and against its acoustic space (phone, radio, far, reverberant); it **keeps** what is not
dialogue untouched (songs, efforts, walla, burned-in graphics) and replaces only words. Every idea
below is one of those three verbs turned into something mediasift can sense and a bench can judge.

## Baseline (the obvious answers)
- Put vocal sounds on the speech line they overlap (`say [laughing] "..."`), already on the table.
- Let anydub call `mediasift see` and reuse its transcript and speaker letters instead of running its own whisper pass.
- Give each speaker letter its own TTS voice in anydub (male/female from the card).

## Assumptions I broke
The five everyone makes (technique, step 1), each flipped (step 2):
1. **The reader of mediasift is an agent reading a card.** Flipped: the reader is another program
   (anydub) reading a spotting list. -> m01.6. This also dissolves the token constraint: a file
   nobody reads in-context costs zero per turn.
2. **mediasift describes; it never produces audio.** Flipped: its most valuable output for re-voicing
   is a stem it already had to compute to sense well. -> m01.8.
3. **Speech is the signal and the background is what you tag and move past.** Flipped: in a dub the
   background is the thing you must not lose, and the owner's one real complaint was exactly that.
   -> m01.1, m01.2.
4. **Timestamps are for citing.** Flipped: timestamps are constraints: where a line may stretch,
   where a mouth is on screen, where a cut forbids spill-over. -> m01.3.
5. **The card describes the video for someone who understands its language.** Flipped: it describes
   what a Vietnamese viewer would *miss*: English burned into the picture, the radio filter on a line,
   a song that should not be translated. -> m01.4, m01.5.

The two that matter most for the council: (3) everyone will optimise the speech line, while the
measured user pain in dubbing is the M&E bed; and (1) everyone will fight over card lines, while the
dubbing consumer needs zero card lines and a file.

## Ideas

### m01.1 The card diff as dub QC: "what did the re-voice lose?"
Run `mediasift see` on the original and on anydub's output and diff the two sound timelines: every
`snd` event (music bed, laugh, siren, crowd) present in the original but missing or 10+ dB quieter in
the dub is a QC failure with a timestamp, plus a dialogue-vs-bed loudness number per span ("dub
voice 14 dB over bed at 01:20-01:40, original was 6 dB"). This is what a mix stage's M&E check does,
it would have caught "the background sound is lost" before the owner did, and it costs only a
second tagger pass (already cheap, cached). The judge exists for free: synthetic dubs with scripted
dropped sounds, and anydub's own `--orig-voice 0` vs `0.3` history as a real before/after.

### m01.2 A "do not dub" keep-list: songs, efforts, walla, chants
Studios never translate a song, a scream or a crowd; they keep the original and subtitle the lyric.
mediasift already tags singing, laughter, shouting, screaming, crying and crowd, so it can emit the
spans where the original voice stem should stay at full level and no TTS line should be placed, and
mark ASR lines that sit inside a `[singing]` bed as lyrics, not dialogue. For TikTok/reels (half of
which ride a song) this stops anydub from voicing a chorus in Vietnamese newsreader prose. Judge:
count of lyric lines anydub translates on a set of music reels, before and after; CED singing
precision is already on the sound bench.

### m01.3 Elastic slots: on-camera vs off-camera per line, from the picture
anydub's slot is "this start to the next start", which forced 30/78 lines over slot before the
silence fix and still forces Vietnamese (longer than English) into tight boxes. A studio knows a
line spoken off-camera, over a cutaway or by a narrator can run long, and a line with a visible
mouth cannot. mediasift could mark each `say` line ON/OFF with Apple Vision face (and mouth
landmark) detection run only on frames inside speech lines at ~2 fps, plus "a cut lands inside this
line" from the cut list it already has. anydub then lets OFF lines borrow the following gap. Judge:
anydub's existing metric (lines over slot, mean/max lag, Claude condense calls billed) on the same
78-line video; mediasift side judged by scripted synthetic clips with a face present/absent.

### m01.4 Acoustic space per line: the futz tag
The card already said `[radio]` under "We have main engine start", but anydub will voice that line
studio-clean. Dubbing mixers match the space: phone band, radio, PA, far-mic, big reverb. A per-line
space word from things already computed (CED's telephone/radio/reverberation classes on the window
under the line, plus the voice's spectral band edges) tells anydub which of a handful of fixed
filters to apply. Cheap, no new model. Judge: synthetic clips where scripted lines are band-passed
or convolved with a room impulse, answers known; the sense bench can carry it as one question type.
Card cost: none unless a line is not "plain", and then two words on a line that already exists.

### m01.5 Forced narratives: burned-in text a Vietnamese viewer would miss
Localization keeps a "graphics insert" list: on-screen text that carries meaning and needs a
translated subtitle (a sign, a headline, a phone screen), separate from burned captions that only
echo the speech. mediasift has both OCR text and ASR text with times; matching them labels each OCR
run `echoes speech` (skip; it is the TikTok caption) or `standalone` (translate). Tears of Steel's
"ENRAGED ROBOTS TAKE ... CITIES" headline is exactly a standalone insert. The same label serves the
fact-check job: standalone on-screen claims are the ones the voice never says aloud. Judge: a small
set of reels with hand-marked caption vs graphic text; the match itself is string similarity, no model.

### m01.6 The spotting list is a file, not card lines
Everything above is a studio cue sheet, and a cue sheet is read by a tool, so it should be
`cues.json` next to `card.md`: per speech line its speaker, gender, ON/OFF, space, overlapping
efforts, music under it with mood, the free gap after it, keep/dub flag, and the burned-in text
nearby. The agent never pays tokens for it; anydub reads it and drops its own transcribe step (one
ASR pass per video instead of two, one cache). The one rule it needs: it carries only fields that
already earned a bench number, so it cannot become a dumping ground. Judge: anydub end-to-end on its
reference video, compared on lag and on the m01.1 QC diff.

### m01.7 A casting sheet per voice letter (pace, pitch band, level), not emotion
Studios cast by voice type and pace, not by per-line emotion, which is the cheap and honest side of
the rejected prosody work. For each letter: median pitch band, syllables per second (from ASR word
times, measured, not inferred), and level relative to the mix. anydub picks a distinct voice per
letter and sets each speaker's base rate so a fast talker's Vietnamese is not stretched to the same
tempo as a slow one, which cuts overruns at the source. Judge: rate and pitch are measurements, so
check them against the voice bench's known speakers and against scripted synthetic speakers at set
rates; anydub's lag metric judges the payoff.

### m01.8 Separate once, only where it pays, and share the stems
Speech over a music bed is where both tools hurt: ASR misses words, CED's music mood is diluted by
speech, and anydub runs demucs on everything for 2 min per 5 min. mediasift already knows the spans
where speech and music overlap; separating only those (on the GPU box via `--on` when it answers, else
skipped with one stderr line, never silently) and caching the stems by content hash lets ASR read the
vocal stem, CED read the bed, and anydub reuse both. On a talk with no music it costs zero. Judge:
WER on synthetic speech+music mixes at set SNRs, and the sound bench stray count; cost judged by
seconds of separation per real cached card. Not researched: whether a lighter separator than
htdemucs (an MDX/ONNX vocal model) is good enough; risk: the cheap path may not beat plain ASR.

## The provocation I would put to the group
The owner's only measured complaint about re-voicing was about the sound they lost, not the words,
so stop asking "what should the card say about speech" and ask "what must survive when the speech is
replaced", because that list is also exactly what an agent that cannot hear is missing.
