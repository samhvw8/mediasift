## Baseline (the obvious answers)
- Caption frames with a small VLM (Florence-2 / SmolVLM / Moondream) so `pic` lines say what is in the shot, not "medium gray area at bottom-left".
- Put vocal sounds and loudness cues on the speech line they overlap (`say [laughing] "..."`, the owner's ideas 1 and 2).
- Send hard spans to an audio/video LLM on the GPU box on demand (the owner's idea 5).

## Assumptions I broke
- "The agent needs more description": an audio describer's craft is mostly omission and placement. Human AD speaks only in dialogue gaps, only about what the soundtrack does not already carry, and never about what is just texture. Read that way, the Tears of Steel card has too much, not too little. It has a 49-shot CUTS list that no describer would read out, `[owl / vehicle]`, `[maybe animal]` and `[insect / fly]` lines that carry no story, and pixel geometry ("medium black area at right changed") in place of the three things a describer always says: WHO, WHERE and WHAT THEY DO.
- "The agent is blind, so describe the picture": the agent is deafblind. It needs subtitles for the deaf (SDH: speaker IDs, off-screen marks, plot sounds) and audio description, both at once, and above all the links between them. Which voice belongs to which face? Is the speaker on screen or a voice-over? Does the on-screen text say something the voice does not? Both tracks reach the agent as text, so the useful lines are the ones that cross-reference the tracks, not more lines on either track.
- (Ambiguity, my reading) "what it needs from media" = what it needs to follow the *story* of this video for the owner's job (who claims what, where, in what order). It is not a full inventory of the media.

## Ideas
### m07.1 Use human audio description as the judge for picture lines
Every picture-line idea (VLM captions, scene words, pruning) currently dies on "a feature without a judge cannot earn a card line". Human describers have already marked, with times, which visual moments matter to the story: YouTube videos and trailers that carry a second "descriptive" audio track (yt-dlp lists it as its own audio format), and the MAD dataset's timed AD sentences. Run the existing local ASR on the AD track and on the main track. The difference is the describer's own lines, each with a time and the words they chose (who, where, action). The bench then scores, for example: "share of describer moments within 2 s of a `pic` line, and share of describer nouns that appear on the card". That gives a scripted, real-footage judge for "what matters visually" at zero running cost, for the extreme user who cannot see at all.

### m07.2 Name the voices from the dialogue itself (vocative binding)
Describers switch from "a man" to "Tom" as soon as the film names him. The card already contains the names: voice C says "You're a jerk, **Tom**" and voice D answers, "Look, **Celia**". A name in direct address at the start or end of a line belongs to the *next* or *previous* other speaker. One footer line can do it: `Voices: C (called Celia by D at 00:24.5), D (called Tom by C at 00:22.9)`. Only show it when two or more address events agree; otherwise leave the letter alone. It is a text rule on the transcript, so there is no model and no measurable CPU. It also works in Vietnamese ("anh Tuấn ơi", "chị Lan"). For podcasts ("thanks, Lex") it gives the summary real names to cite instead of "voice B". Judge: name questions on the bench dialogues and on a podcast intro, scored for precision.

### m07.3 Join voice letters to shot letters: who is on screen, who is off-screen or voice-over
In shot/reverse-shot, a describer never has to say who is talking because the cut tells the viewer. The card has both alphabets (voices A-F, shots A-AS) and never joins them. When the voice turns and the shot letters alternate in step (C speaks on shot X, D on shot Y, repeated), write `conversation C<->D, C on shot X, D on shot Y`. That tells the agent which flow tile shows which person. A voice that keeps talking across unrelated shots and never lines up gets `(voice-over)`, as subtitles for the deaf mark `(off-screen)`. This matters for fact-checking: is the claim made by the person on camera or by a narrator over stock footage? Cost: a join over data already computed. A later cheap step is mouth movement from Apple Vision face landmarks, sampled only during speech. Judge: "who is on screen when X says Y" questions on the real-footage bench.

### m07.4 Story-weight pruning: a describer's omission rules applied to the card
Subtitles for the deaf caption only sounds that matter to the plot, and audio describers describe scenes, not cuts. Rules to test: (a) the CUTS list collapses into scenes, where shots that share a sound bed and recur in letters form one scene, with one line per scene and its shot letters; (b) hedged ambient sounds (`maybe`, A / B, under a music bed, not synced to a `pic` event and not after a speech gap) fold into one footer count with a `look` hint; (c) `, male` is said once per voice, not on every line. The extreme users here are the 1 h 45 podcast and a braille-display-sized budget. The test is token count against bench parity: the change ships only if the synthetic and real-footage scores hold while the card gets shorter. This idea removes lines instead of adding them.

### m07.5 WHO and WHERE per scene from Apple's built-in Vision classifier
A describer opens each scene with place and people: "Night. A bridge in Amsterdam. Two people." macOS already ships `VNClassifyImageRequest`, roughly 1,300 scene and object labels on device, plus human-rectangle and face counting. Nothing to download, and it costs milliseconds per image. mediasift already calls Vision for OCR. Run it once on the first frame of each *scene* (not each frame) and write `where: outdoor, bridge, night; 2 people`. That covers the hardest extreme user: a speechless TikTok carried by music, text overlays and action, where today's card reads as pixel geometry. Pin by recording the macOS build in the `Sensed:` line and rerunning the bench after an OS upgrade, because the model changes with the OS. On Linux, emit no line. Judge: where and how-many-people questions in m07.1's judge and the real-footage bench.

### m07.6 Text-only claims and credit: the on-screen words speech never said
Describers read signs aloud because sound cannot carry them. mediasift already drops captions that repeat speech. Turn the remainder into a signal: OCR text that brings numbers, names, dates, @handles or URLs the speech never says gets marked `text-only` (e.g. `text-only: "Theo WHO 2023: 1/3 ..."`). These are the claims a fact-check must verify and the agent would otherwise miss. The platform watermark and handle become one `credit:` line, for the owner's "turn the reel into a script, with credit to its author". Cost: string comparison on data already computed. Judge: fact-check questions on `tiktok_vi` and `reel_en`, and checking the credit handle against the uploader field in yt-dlp metadata.

### m07.7 Gap map: where the soundtrack leaves room
A describer's first pass over a film is a map of speech-free gaps, because that is where description can go. The same map is what the owner's dubbing job needs. Vietnamese often runs longer than English, and the complaint was "background sound is lost". Emit (as `gaps.tsv`, with only a one-line pointer on the card) every speech-free window of 1.0 s or longer and what fills it: `00:42.6-00:44.2 1.6 s music (scary)`. anydub can then stretch a line into the next gap instead of speeding it up, and it knows which gaps hold music bed that must be kept. Judge: in anydub, the count of dubbed lines that overrun their slot or need over 1.15x speed-up, before and after.

### m07.8 Slide-title chapters for talks, judged by YouTube's own chapters
In a lecture, a describer's main job is "new slide: <title>". In a 47-minute talk, the largest OCR line (Vision returns box heights) on each new still screen is a chapter heading with a time: `chapters: 00:00 Intro, 04:12 "Memory hierarchy", ...`. That is what "summarise with timestamps, then link several videos together" needs to cite sections across videos. It reuses OCR already run. Judge: YouTube chapter markers from yt-dlp metadata, scored as time-boundary hits within 10 s, on talks that have creator chapters. This is a free external ground truth.

## The provocation I would put to the group
Before adding a model, ask a describer's two questions of every line: "Does the agent already get this from the other track?" and "Would the story break without it?" A card built by cutting and cross-referencing will beat a card built by describing more.
