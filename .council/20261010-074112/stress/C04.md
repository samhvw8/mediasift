## Fragile assumptions

Ranked most fragile first.

1. **A small AudioSet tagger gives trustworthy tags on long, speech-dominant audio, English and Vietnamese. Untested, and the likeliest to break.** CED and YAMNet are trained on 10-second YouTube clips. Their headline mAP (CED 49.0, YAMNet 0.306 balanced) averages over 500+ classes and says little about "laughter under a voice" or "music bed under a host". A podcast is the worst case, because speech masks everything else. The concept's own table concedes that an invented `[laughter]` is worse than silence. A 1 h 45 m episode has about 630 ten-second windows. A 1% false-positive rate is therefore about 6 false tags per episode, and the agent cannot tell them from true ones. The concept mitigates this with a confidence floor, but the floor is tuned on a hand-labelled 10 minutes and then applied to hours. Whisper-AT, the one candidate that rides on ASR, does not fit framesift's backends (PyTorch Whisper only, per the concept's own prior-art note), so there is no free tagger.
2. **Sam's daily videos ask enough non-speech-sound questions to pay for a new model and dependency. Untested, and the concept admits it.** The concept's own table says "I did not have this data". The bench has 55 short-video questions and 8 long ones. Podcasts and lectures are the stated primary case, and there sound is speech plus a few music and laughter events. For screen repros, the useful audio facts (a notification chime, an error beep) usually appear in the transcript, in the UI, or in the next frame. If the answer is "almost never", the honest outcome is Stage 0 only: silences and a loudness sparkline.
3. **A vision model reads a labelled spectrogram strip for timing and shape (beep count, rising vs falling tone, onset lag). Shaky.** The concept already narrowed this to timing and shape, and that is sensible. But the evidence it cites is about naming sounds, not counting or reading contours. The 1-1.5k-token image also has to pass a test against text tags, so it is optional by design. The weakness is low-cost: Stage 3 is gated, and if it fails it is dropped. Cost of being wrong: a few hundred lines of throwaway renderer.

Solid parts: the audio signals already exist in `audio.py` (50 ms RMS, log-mel, silent runs), and Stage 0 needs no new dependency. The Gemini rate is verified against the vendor page (32 tokens per second, 1,920 per minute, non-speech understanding, 9.5 h max) - https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/audio . The synthetic-clip generator with a known key is a real, cheap test. The "spend follows audio redundancy" rule is a sound budget idea.

## Pre-mortem

A year later, nothing dramatic happened. Stage 0 shipped and works: the storyboard lost its useless stat lines and gained silence and loudness lines. The CED tagger shipped too, passed the synthetic-clip `doctor` check (a clean beep on silence is easy), and looked fine on the first podcast. In daily use it produced a few `[music]` and `[laughter]` tags per episode on speech with background noise, and on Vietnamese podcasts it hesitated around tonal speech and stayed mostly silent or noisy. Sam noticed two wrong tags, stopped trusting the sound score, and the agents (which grep the storyboard rather than read it, per the long-video bench) kept finding answers in the transcript. The strip was built, scored about even with tags on the 20 synthetic questions (the questions were designed around what it could do), and is rarely called because `look` on a speech span never uses it. The model file went stale, `onnxruntime` pinned an old Python wheel on the next macOS/Python upgrade, and the `doctor` self-test was the only thing keeping the module alive. Meanwhile the harness gained native audio or a hosted video MCP was good enough, and the module was neither removed nor used. Net: a quiet maintenance liability with a free half (Stage 0) that did the real work.

## Who loses

- **Transcript-only workflow (status quo).** It loses nothing except the claim that it is enough. It wins by default when the tagger is noisy, and Sam's agents already grep text, so inertia favors it.
- **Hosted audio APIs (Gemini, Speechmatics Audio Events - https://docs.speechmatics.com/speech-to-text/features/audio-events).** A free local sound score undercuts their use case for Sam. Their response is not competition but being good enough: the Gemini valve at 1,920 tokens per minute is about 230k tokens for 2 h, but a targeted 50 s span is about 1.6k, and the agent receives only a short text answer, so the harness token cost is small. This is the strongest alternative to Stages 1 and 3: a doubt-only path with no model to maintain.
- **Harness vendors.** If Claude Code or its models take audio natively, the whole of Stages 1-3 loses value; only the cheap map survives. The concept says so itself.
- **Sam, as maintainer.** He loses time to a vocabulary-collapse table and a model download, and could lose trust if a wrong tag misleads him once.

## Fixes

| Weakness | Smallest fix |
|---|---|
| Tagger false positives on speech-heavy and Vietnamese audio (assumption 1) | Gate the tagger behind a measured number: run on 10 min of one English and one Vietnamese podcast, count false tags per hour. Ship it only if the count is near zero at the chosen floor. Otherwise print holes (`[sound under speech, unclassified]`) and ship Stage 0. This is already in the concept as a kill rule; make it a hard ship gate, not a note. |
| Tags are not calibrated across hours of audio | Never print a tag from a single window: require a minimum duration plus the energy curve agreeing, and emit tags only outside ASR speech spans for music and laughter detection. Print `n windows, mean score` in a debug file, not the storyboard. |
| Unknown demand for sound questions (assumption 2) | One cheap check before any model work: grep the last month of the user's real session transcripts or video list for sound-related questions. If under a handful, stop at Stage 0. No fix beyond that; it is a decision, not a build. |
| Strip value unproven (assumption 3) | Keep the concept's gate: build the strip only if arm 3 beats arm 2 on rhythm/pitch/lag questions. Write those questions without using the strip's strengths as the filter, and include a muted and a reversed control. No change needed beyond honest question design. |
| Maintenance of onnxruntime and a model file | Pin the model by hash and keep the `doctor` self-test. Alternatively reuse the Gemini valve and skip the local model entirely. No fix beyond that: this is the price of owning a tagger. |
| Diarisation absent, so "speaker 2 louder" in the sample score is not buildable in v1 | Remove speaker lines from the sample output; print turns by pause only, as the concept's own limits section says. |
| Agents grep rather than read the storyboard | Make sound lines grep-friendly (consistent bracket tags, timestamp first). A one-line format rule, not a feature. |

## Verdict

**Survives with changes.** Keep Stage 0 (free signals, replace the stat lines, silence and loudness map) unconditionally: it adds no dependency and fixes a known flaw. Make the tagger conditional on two measured gates: (a) real demand for sound questions, and (b) near-zero false tags per hour on the real English and Vietnamese podcasts. Keep the strip behind its A/B test. Weigh the Gemini `ear:` valve against the local tagger before building it, because a text-only answer on a 50 s span may make Stages 1 and 3 unnecessary. The concept is honest about what it did not run; it should be judged on that first measurement, not on the design.
