## Fragile assumptions

Ranked most fragile first.

1. **Boundary detection yields 5-15 pairs and finds the step a question is about (shaky, close to untested).**
   - The 5-15 figure is a target, not a measurement.
   - The detector rests on 128x72 pixel stats. The repo's own decisions show those stats already failed on small changes: a whole-frame mean "missed a ChatGPT screen recording entirely" (`.okf/decisions/keyframe-drift.md`). At 128x72 a toggled checkbox, a cursor or a one-line error toast is a handful of pixels.
   - Real screen recordings scroll, type, animate spinners and carry webcam overlays. `screen-vs-camera.md` already flags a live overlay as a failure of the screen/camera test. These produce dozens of "events", not 5-15.
   - The thresholds will be tuned on synthetic clips. Synthetic solid-colour clips already misled the cut floor once: 0.25 missed every same-stage camera switch (`cut-threshold.md`). Expect the same transfer gap.
   - The kill rule (recall under 90 percent on synthetic UI clips) tests the easy case.

2. **A pre-drawn before|after|crop beats two plain frames (shaky, untested, and the concept admits it).**
   - I found no published test on current models either, so the claim stays open.
   - What I found points in both directions.
     - VDiff-Bench (https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06245) reports that for the weakest models 48-81% of low-level questions get the "no difference" answer even though a real change exists. Models often fail to notice a subtle change at all. That supports a drawn box.
     - The same failure also says a mislocated or oversized box misleads, and a pixel-diff box shows a changed region, not a meaning. A spinner, a clock or a cursor blink gets the box.
   - The headroom is small on the stated bench: uniform contact sheets already score 93% and framesift sheets 96% on short videos. The crop has to beat that on temporal questions, where nothing is measured yet.
   - Control D (no crop) is the right test. If D equals C, the crop is dead weight, as the concept says.

3. **The sound half of the user's gap is closed (shaky; mostly not addressed).**
   - The user named audio as half the problem. The concept ships speech / non-speech / silence plus an energy novelty score. That is not "sound that goes with it".
   - Tone, laughter, music change and alarms are listed as out of scope. The 10 audio questions will therefore test the thing the design does not do.
   - Burst size "follows audio redundancy". That makes a wrong speech/non-speech call into a wrong image budget. A podcast with a screen share, or a lecture with a loud demo, sits near the 70 percent line.
   - The +300 ms "click -> modal" lag lines need a click sound. Screen recordings often have no system audio. The cause-and-effect claim then falls back to "two adjacent pairs", which the concept itself calls a hypothesis.

4. **The still-changing flag is a safe escalation switch (shaky).**
   - It compares t_after with t_after+0.3 s. A slow scroll, a 20 s fade and a 2 px/s slide each look still over 0.3 s.
   - The concept already lists this as must-be-true 3 and adds a "slow-drift channel" only if a test shows a miss. The synthetic set contains those cases by design, so a miss is likely. That channel then becomes a second detector with its own threshold.

5. **Cost and local-run claims (solid).** Python, numpy, Pillow and ffmpeg are already in the stack, the work is CPU-only, and `_stable_pick` exists in `keyframes.py`. The "$0, nothing to watch" claim holds. The image-token estimate is flagged as unmeasured, which is honest.

6. **Prior art is thin, so the idea is not already done (solid, but with a catch).** I checked the closest repo, https://github.com/littler00t/mcp-deep-video. Its README lists 14 tools for motion detection, scene cuts, frame diffing, Whisper and annotated frames, exposed to the agent as primitives. It has 2 commits, which fits "no one finished this". The catch is that "no one shipped it" also fits "it did not pay off". I did not verify the other MCP servers the concept names.

## Pre-mortem

A year later `framesift pairs` exists, is zero-flag, and is rarely used.

- **Most probable path:**
  1. The synthetic suite passes: recall above 90 percent on clips with clean A-to-B-to-C changes.
  2. On Sam's real bug-repro recordings the detector over-fires on scrolling, hover states and loading spinners, or under-fires on small changes. The timeline has 4 lines on one recording and 60 on the next. Sam adds a sensitivity knob "just for now". The no-flag promise erodes.
  3. The arms test shows C roughly equal to B (contact sheets) on temporal questions, within noise. With 30 questions the 95 percent interval is about plus or minus 9 points, so a 3-5 point gain cannot be told from luck. The result is "no clear win".
  4. The audio questions show no gain, because nothing was added for them.
  5. Sam keeps using `analyze` for podcasts and lectures, where there are few visual events, and `pairs` only for screen repros.
  6. Nothing breaks and nobody is paged. A second timeline format sits next to the storyboard, and the repo now has two.

- **Why it fails quietly rather than loudly:** every output looks plausible. A timeline with the wrong 8 boundaries reads as a clean timeline. Missed events print nothing unless both detectors were quiet, and the "nothing happened" line is the only guard.

- **The agent-side reality check.** In the long-video bench the agents grepped the storyboard and answered from context; the visual questions did not separate the arms. A pairs timeline can fall into the same habit: the agent greps the words column and never opens a pair image.

## Who loses

- **The existing storyboard / keyframe path.** It gets a rival with a different shape. Sam is the one who pays, in maintenance (two outputs, two sets of thresholds). Likely response: keep both, then drift to whichever is faster, which is `analyze` for non-screen content.
- **Hosted video-understanding services and native-video models.** If a harness later takes video input, the whole sensor is obsolete. Claude Code could add it at any release. That risk applies to every option in the brief, but a purpose-built pair format is the least reusable one.
- **Frame-extraction MCP servers** (claude-video-vision, vidmcp, mcp-deep-video). Little at stake: they offer primitives and unfinished packaging. They are unlikely to react.
- **Whisper-only / transcript-first workflows.** The bench says transcripts alone score 49% on short videos, so they lose on visual questions, but the long-video bench had all arms at 8/8. Users of those workflows will say the transcript is enough, and for podcasts they are right.

## Fixes

| Weakness | Smallest fix |
|---|---|
| Detector count and recall on real recordings (1) | Before any new module, run the existing `segment.py` on 5 of Sam's real repros and hand-label the true steps. Count how many existing boundaries match. Add 2-3 real clips to the kill rule, not only synthetic ones. If over 20 pairs result, merge by region (same changed region within 2 s is one event) rather than adding a threshold. |
| Small changes missed at 128x72 (1) | Run the delta at the decode size of the pair frames (768 px), but only inside windows where the cheap signal moved at all. Or add a second cheap signal: per-tile max change on a 16x9 grid, which a one-line change still moves. No fix without a second signal; the whole-frame mean is known to miss. |
| Crop benefit unproven (2) | Run arms C vs D before writing the crop code. Start with D (plain before|after plus a drawn box on the after frame) and add the third panel only if the data says so. Delete the crop if D matches C. |
| Pixel-diff box shows spinners and cursors (2) | Mask regions that changed in more than N boundaries (a spinner, a clock). Cheap, but it is one more heuristic: accept it only if the real-clip check shows it is needed. |
| Audio claim too small (3) | Reword the goal to "speech, silence and loudness changes" in the README, or ship the sound score on the existing contact-sheet path (the baseline guard in the concept). A real tagger is a separate pinned dependency: no fix inside this concept, this is the price. |
| Burst size depends on a speech call (3) | Use a fixed 4-frame burst when flag=yes, and drop the audio-driven rule until the 10 audio questions show 2 vs 6-8 frames differ. One fewer branch. |
| Slow-drift misses (4) | Make the flag compare against t_after + 1.0 s as well as 0.3 s, and report the larger. The synthetic fade and slide clips decide if it is enough. |
| Arms test too small to separate arms (Pre-mortem) | Raise to about 60 temporal questions, or pick only questions where A and B are known to fail, and report counts plus intervals, not percentages. Add the reversed/shuffled controls the concept already has. A tie is an answer: ship the cheaper arm. |
| Agent greps the timeline and skips images | Put the question-relevant pair in the timeline as text first (region, words, still flag); count how many pair images the agent opens in each arm as a metric. If it opens none, the images added nothing. |
| Two formats side by side | Fold `pairs` into the `analyze` output as one extra file, not a second command, so there is one place to look. |

## Verdict

**Survives with changes.**

The idea is cheap, local and testable, and it is the only council concept that names the temporal gap directly. But its two core claims are untested, and the audio half of the brief is mostly out of scope. Required changes:

1. Check the detector on 5 real repros before any renderer work. The pair-count target is the first thing that must be measured.
2. Run arm C vs D (crop vs no crop) first. Delete the crop if there is no gain.
3. State the audio limit up front in the README and the pitch: speech, silence and loudness only. Drop the audio-driven burst rule until the audio questions show it matters.
4. Enlarge the question set, or report counts with intervals, so a tie is read as a tie.
5. If contact sheets plus one sound-score line match C, stop and ship that. The concept already says this; treat it as the likely outcome, not a footnote.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06245 (VDiff-Bench), https://github.com/littler00t/mcp-deep-video (README only), and local files `.okf/decisions/{keyframe-drift,cut-threshold,screen-vs-camera}.md`. A social search on the topic returned nothing, so there is no user-sentiment evidence either way. I did not read the papers beyond their abstracts and result snippets.
