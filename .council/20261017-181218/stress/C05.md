## Fragile assumptions

Checks I ran (read only): `src/mediasift/see.py` (`look`, lines 923-955), `src/mediasift/asr.py` (all four backends, `words_to_cues`, `serve_stdin`), `bench/sense/real/reel_en/key.json` and `transcript.txt`, the r17/r18 reel_en answers, and the 61 `look` rows in `~/.cache/mediasift/usage.jsonl` joined to their `sensed.json`.

**1. Untested, most fragile: the q06 miss is a missing phrase-to-span map, so `--say` fixes it.**
- The q06 line on the card is `00:08.9-00:10.0 say "Don't stay up late doing nothing."`. It is only 1.1 s long, and the card prints its span.
- Today's `look VIDEO 8.9-10.0` with the default `-n 8` spreads frames at `a + (b-a)(i+0.5)/n`: 8.97, 9.10, **9.24**, 9.38, 9.52 ... The 9.24 s frame falls inside the 9.20-9.33 s face-cover pose that the developer keyed at 15 fps.
- So the tool that exists today already returns the answer frame if the agent zooms on the line it quotes. Arm C answered "fist in front of his chest", `sure: false`, with 5-6 images read (r18 `reel_en.C.json`, `C2.json`). That fits the 8.61 s tile and no zoom at 9.2 s. `[INFERENCE]`: the answer file does not record which `look` calls ran.
- The miss is therefore most likely an *agent behaviour* gap (it did not zoom, though it was unsure), not a *precision* gap. Word times add value only when the phrase sits inside a long line. Lines run up to the 8 s cap in `words_to_cues`, and parakeet lines were seen stretched to 15.5 s (roadmap step 4). No bench question tests that case today.
- The concept's own check list has no control arm for this. It compares `--say` against the old hint, not against a hint that says "zoom on the say line's span".
- The usage log cannot settle it either. 31 of the 41 zooms that still have a `sensed.json` overlap a speech line, but speech covers most of those videos, so overlap does not show that zooms are phrase-anchored. The 20 zooms on the code screencast have no `sensed.json` left. Many of the rest are on 143 s and 26 s bench videos, so they are bench runs, not real jobs.

**2. Shaky: word times are good to about 0.2 s on every backend and every input.**
- Measured: 1 boundary, 1 file (reel_en, 26 s), 1 backend (parakeet). That file is shorter than parakeet's `chunk_duration=120.0`, so it never crosses a chunk seam. Builders of other parakeet wrappers report that "neighbouring chunks timestamp the same word up to about 0.5 s apart" at seams ([ParakeetSTT PR #3](https://github.com/JohanesSetiawan/ParakeetSTT/pull/3); a similar fix in [starling PR #441](https://github.com/sims1253/starling/pull/441)). A 0.5 s error is outside the 0.3 s pad.
- QUAN word times come from CTC frames (`ctc_words`, 40 ms). CTC places a spike, not a span, so word *ends* are the weak half. Not measured.
- Whisper word times are evaluated at tolerances "of more than 200 ms" ([arXiv:2509.09987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09987)). mlx-whisper does accept `word_timestamps=True`: a real project used it for lip-sync cuts ([diffusers-workflow issue #483](https://github.com/dkackman/diffusers-workflow/issues/483)). That `[INFERENCE]` in the concept is likely true, but accuracy is unmeasured.
- Site subtitles: the concept says they have no word times and stores `words: null`. This is wrong for the most common case. YouTube automatic captions carry per-word offsets in json3/srv3 (`tOffsetMs`) and inline `<c>` tags in VTT ([subtitle-toolbox issue #135](https://github.com/yama6a/subtitle-toolbox/issues/135), [Stack Overflow 9611397](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/9611397/how-to-extract-closed-caption-transcript-from-youtube-video)). On URL jobs, mediasift takes `-orig` captions in place of ASR (`.okf/decisions/see-sensor.md`). Real jobs are mostly URLs, so `words: null` would be the common path, and `--say` would fall back to the line span. That is today's behaviour.

**3. Shaky for English, contradicted for Vietnamese: the loudest word (peak RMS) marks the gesture.**
- The research the concept cites ties the gesture apex to the *pitch accent*. Pitch accents are "acoustically predominantly defined by positive excursions in the fundamental frequency (F0)", not by loudness ([Pouw et al. 2021](https://nyaspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/nyas.14532)). Apexes also "show greater variability in alignment" than strokes ([Lingua 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024384123001079)).
- In Southern Vietnamese, corrective focus "is systematically realized by means of increased duration and lower F1, but does not cause significant variation in intensity" ([JIPA, Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/stress-and-phrasal-prominence-in-tone-languages-the-case-of-southern-vietnamese/13F3BF93FCA2C9BD365B2AC012C35B7D)). The concept says part 3 suits Vietnamese because it needs no word list. The acoustic measure it picks is the one Vietnamese does not use for focus. Peak RMS also follows tone and vowel type, and reels often add a caption "pop" sound or a music bed over the voice.
- Evidence so far: n = 1 ("stay" on reel_en).

## Pre-mortem

October 2027. Nothing broke, and nobody can say the feature helped.

1. Word times went into `sensed.json` for parakeet and faster-whisper. QUAN and the URL caption path stored `words: null`, so `--say` fell back to the line span on Vietnamese reels and YouTube links. That covered most real jobs.
2. The owner re-keyed q06 to "raised fist that moves up across his face" before the bench round. Phone videos went to 16/16. The round also changed the zoom hint. Agents zoomed on the q06 line, and some used `look 8.9-10.0` instead of `--say`. Both answered. Nobody ran a hint-only arm, so the gain was credited to `--say`.
3. The 10 new "what is X doing when they say Y" questions were keyed from 15 fps strips: the same view `--say` gives. They favoured the tool, so they scored well and taught nothing about the grid.
4. Over the year, `usage.jsonl` showed `--say` a few times, mostly in bench runs. It did not log `--say` apart from plain spans, so this needed a manual read. Real prompts asked what a talk says, not what a hand does.
5. On vertical reels, the 24-frame strip shrank each tile. `look` uses 320 px tiles in 4 columns when `n > 4`. A 9:16 sheet of 24 tiles is 1280 x ~3530 px. Claude scales it to a 1568 px long side, so each tile is about 142 px wide, against 320 px for today's 8-frame sheet. Agents squinted at hands and zoomed again with fewer frames.
6. Part 3 never shipped, because step 3 did not show that agents skip `--say`. That part was fine. The quiet cost was the word-shape test and the sensed.json field across four backends and the remote JSON, kept for a tool with little use.

## Fixes

| weakness | smallest change that fixes it |
|---|---|
| No control for "the hint alone fixes q06" (assumption 1) | Add arm C-hint to step 3: the new hint text says "for what happens while a line is said, `look` that line's START-END". Ship `--say` only if it wins over C-hint on questions whose phrase sits inside a line of 3 s or longer. If it does not win, ship the hint and part 1, and stop. |
| No bench question where `--say` can beat the line span | Of the 10 new questions, make at least 5 target a short phrase inside a line of 4 s or longer. Otherwise the round cannot tell `--say` from today's `look`. |
| New questions keyed from the tool's own view | Key them from the source at full frame rate (every frame, e.g. `ffmpeg` at native fps), and write the key before any arm runs. Include some answers that last over 0.5 s, so the fixed grid has a fair chance. |
| q06 re-key after seeing answers | Freeze q06 as is. Add q06b ("...when he says 'stay up'") with `resolution_s`. Score both. |
| 24-tile strip shrinks tiles on vertical video | Cap `--say` at 8 frames: 2 per word, placed at word quarter points, so a 4-word phrase gets a ~0.1 s step. Keep today's 320 px tile. For phrases longer than 4 words, take the frames around the word the agent names, or return 2 sheets. |
| Word times unmeasured across chunk seams, QUAN, whisper | Run the burned-caption check on one file over 120 s at a seam (parakeet), on tiktok_vi (QUAN), and on the box (faster-whisper). Set the pad from the worst measured error, not a fixed 0.3 s. |
| URL captions stored as `words: null` | Parse word offsets from YouTube automatic captions (json3 `tOffsetMs`, or VTT inline `<c>` timestamps). Keep `words: null` only for captions that really have none, with the stderr line. |
| Old cached cards lack words | Make "speech has words" part of the speech track's revision (roadmap step 3). On an old card, `--say` prints one stderr line ("no word times in this cache; using line span") and continues. |
| Adoption cannot be measured | Log `say` (the phrase and the matched span) in `usage.jsonl` as its own event. |
| RMS stress is the wrong cue (assumption 3) | Replace peak RMS with word duration divided by its syllable count (Vietnamese: syllable = word; English: vowel groups), and score English and Vietnamese apart. Keep part 3 gated as the concept says. Kill it per language if it wins fewer than 6 of 10. |
| Part 4 (screencast suffix) and part 5 (ear gate) | No fix needed: both are gated or parked with clear reopen conditions. This is correct. |
| A second flag next to `look START-END` | No fix: this is the price, if `--say` beats the control. |

Runs: $0/month; 0 new models; 0 card lines (one hint reworded); +~40 B/word in sensed.json; `look --say` = one ≤2 s ffmpeg decode of 8 frames on demand; watch = the word-shape test per backend, the YouTube caption word parser, and the `say` usage event.

## Verdict

**Survives with changes.**

1. Ship part 1 (word times) as shared plumbing. Roadmap steps 4 and 5 can use it too. Include YouTube caption word offsets, and tie it to the speech track revision.
2. Gate part 2 (`--say`) on beating a hint-only arm (C-hint), on questions where the phrase sits inside a long line. On q06 as keyed, today's `look 8.9-10.0` already samples a frame (9.24 s) inside the pose.
3. Cap the `--say` strip at 8 word-placed frames at the current tile size, not 24.
4. Key new questions blind, from full-rate frames. Freeze q06 and add q06b.
5. If part 3 is ever tried, base it on duration, not RMS, and score it per language. The RMS rule is contradicted for Vietnamese.
