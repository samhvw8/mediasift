# Stress test: C07 Text for the agent's own eyes, and the text that never changes

Checked against: `develop/C07.md`, `develop/c07_settle.py` and `.json`, `develop/c07_caretcrop.png`,
`src/mediasift/see.py` (`_drop_captions` line 396, `strip_watermarks` line 697), the VN TikTok card
`~/.cache/mediasift/see/3d219a48b6b4f7e3/` (card and `flow_01.jpg`), `.okf/benchmarks/sense-bench.md`,
and `.okf/decisions/roadmap.md`.

## Fragile assumptions

Ranked from most fragile to least.

**1. Untested: a blink chain on the 32x18 grid finds the line the agent needs (Part B).**
- Demand is absent. The concept's own check shows that no real agent zoomed into code; the 20 "code"
  zooms were a unit test. Real use has one bug-repro screen recording, which is an app UI, not an editor.
- The anchor is coarse. One grid cell is 40 x 40 px on a 720p frame. "Line height comes from the 2-cell
  caret height" gives 80 px, which is about 3 code lines, not one. The developer's own proof crop
  (`c07_caretcrop.png`, 640 x 158) shows `def have_fun():` and `for i in range(5):`. The caret line
  `print(i)` is cut off at the bottom edge, under an autocomplete popup. The one crop offered as
  evidence misses the line in focus.
- The caret often does not blink when it matters. Editors hold the caret solid while the user types,
  and the concept saw this on `python_100s` (100.7-102.1 s, no chain). Terminals often use a steady
  cursor, and on macOS the blink rate of Terminal and iTerm2 cannot be set with the system default
  (https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/355936/macos-the-cursor-in-terminal-won-t-blink); iTerm2
  makes blinking a per-profile choice and can fade the cursor instead of toggling it
  (https://iterm2.com/documentation-preferences-profiles-text.html). A fading caret gives no clean
  on/off change event. VS Code redraws its caret on a 500 ms timer
  (https://developers.slashdot.org/story/17/03/24/1511252/blinking-cursor-devours-cpu-cycles-in-visual-studio-code-editor),
  so `python_100s` fits the editor, not proof that the rule generalises.
- The rule is tuned on one edited explainer (Fireship). The plan adds 2 screencasts, which is still a
  small set for 4 gates (screen only, ≥ 5 fps, text beside, chain length).

**2. Shaky: Part A gains questions over today's arm C (Part A's ship rule).**
- Arm C opens images (55 images on the 4 landscape videos, `sense-bench.md` line 44). I opened
  `flow_01.jpg` of the VN TikTok. The banner `"Treo biển" Tử Vi` / `"Bán thịt" Bát Tự` is legible in
  all 12 tiles, even at about 180 px per tile. The question "What claim does the headline make?" is
  likely already right in arm C after one sheet (about 900 image tokens).
- So the real gain is not "can read the banner". It is (a) the span: "on screen throughout" without
  opening all 7 sheets (about 6,300 image tokens), (b) agents that read the card only, and (c) a fix the
  concept does not mention: the banner spells `Bát Tự` correctly while the speech line loops
  `bắt tự` about 40 times (card line 28). The banner is a check on the speech.
- The kill rule "gains at least 2 questions" sits inside the bench noise: real-footage runs vary by 1-3
  questions in 32 (`sense-bench.md` line 36). A +2 can be noise, and a 0 can hide a real token win.

**3. Shaky: fold, vote and same-box give the right string.**
- The fold does not do what the concept says. `strip_watermarks` folds with `re.sub(r"\W", "", lower)`,
  and Python's `\W` keeps letters with tone marks. `TRẮNG` and `TRĂNG` fold to different keys, so no
  vote happens between them. The settle data lists them as two strings (75 and 33 hits).
- A vote fixes random misreads, not repeated ones. The same font in the same place gives the same
  misread in every frame. Today's card already shows one: `"hơi hơi kiểi"` for the top label
  `Kênh này (hơi hơi kiểu)`. In Vietnamese one tone mark changes the word. A wrong header line in a
  fact-check is the "corrupt" case that the owner ranks below a crash.
- The settle shares may be high. `c07_settle.py` appends one time per OCR line, not per frame. `Quan`
  has 68 hits between 40.0 and 55.4 s, which is about 15 frames. The 69% and 53% banner shares are
  plausible, but "frames" in the table means hits.
- Speech exclusion attacks the target. A fact-check speaker often reads the claim aloud. `_drop_captions`
  drops text when 60% of its 3+ letter words were said within ±6 s. Over a 73 s banner, the topic words
  will be said. The banner survived here only because the ASR heard `bắt` for `Bát`. Roadmap step 2
  (correct Vietnamese speech) will make the claim drop itself.
- The "exact to 1/15 s" span is an overclaim. On this reel an inserted screenshot covers the banner at
  5.3-6.6 s (`flow_01.jpg`), and a talking face makes events around the box all the time. A sample of
  one frame in 10 cannot say which event was the boundary.
- The plan is silent on ranking. The VN reel has 58 lasting strings that are not on the card, and the
  cap is 5 lines. Junk such as `Quan` (1% of the frame) and `„waouo` (the other VN reel) competes for
  the lines.
- "No gap over 10 s" and "cap of 30 calls" conflict on any video over 5 minutes, which includes the
  talks in real use.

## Pre-mortem

October 2027. Nobody removed C07, and nobody uses Part B.

Part A shipped after a bench round where C+A scored +2 over C. That is inside the run noise, but the
token count also fell, so it went in. Over the year the owner ran about a dozen real reels. Most
headers were right. Two carried a wrong tone mark or the losing variant of a split vote. One agent
quoted a header string in a fact-check summary, because a line in the header reads as measured fact.
No bench question checked strings letter by letter, so nobody saw it.

Part B was built on `python_100s` and two more screencasts, and it passed its keyed stops. The owner's
real screen recordings were app and browser bug repros, driven by the pointer, with text typed into
fields. Part B found no chain on almost all of them. It printed `Not sensed: caret (no blinking caret
found)` on every screen recording, and `text_NN.jpg` appeared perhaps twice. Its thresholds stayed
tuned to one Fireship video. It cost test time and a card phrase, and it gave nothing. It was not a
loud failure, so it was never removed.

| The owner's maintenance time | Part B adds 4 gates and a chain rule with no real user | Low cost per month, but it is the "needs watching" kind the owner weighs most |

## Fixes

| weakness | smallest fix |
|---|---|
| Part B has no demand | Park Part B. Unpark it when a real session asks a code question that the card and one `look` cannot answer. Put the owner's bug-repro recording in the bench first: it is the only real screen job |
| Part B anchor off by a line or more | Get the caret box from the native frames, not the grid. Diff the two native frames of one blink (caret on, caret off), which the crop seek already decodes. The diff is the caret rectangle: exact x, y and line height. Crop ±5 line heights. Keep the "asked-about line inside the crop" rule, and raise the bar from 60% to 80% |
| Part B silent-caret cases | No fix: this is the price. A caret that does not blink gives no chain. Record the miss in `sensed.json`, not on the card. Do not add a `Not sensed: caret` phrase until a question needs it |
| Part A may not gain answers over arm C | Run 3 arms: C (images allowed), C+A, and C card-only. The ship metric is right answers at equal or fewer images and image tokens per answer. Repeat each arm twice, because the noise is 1-3 questions. Add 2 questions that need the span ("Is the headline still on screen at 1:05?") and 1 that needs the banner to correct the speech ("What term does the speaker repeat?") |
| Fold keeps tone marks, so no vote | Group strings by a key without tone marks (NFD, then drop combining marks, letters only, lower case). Vote on the full reading with tone marks. If the top reading has less than 2/3 of the votes, print both: `"CŨNG TRẮNG TAY NHƯ 0%" (OCR; also read "TRĂNG")` |
| Repeated misreads look like fact | Mark the header as read text: `On screen throughout (OCR, read once)`. Add a letter-exact check to the bench: every printed string against a native-frame key, counting tone marks. Ship rule: 0 wrong strings on the bench videos |
| Speech exclusion drops a claim that is read aloud | Do not apply the speech-word test to lasting strings. Captions change every 2-4 s, so persistence already excludes them. Require 3 reads over at least 8 s in the same box |
| "Exact to 1/15 s" span | Print the span as first and last read (`seen 00:00.5-01:13.4`). To get exact edges, bisect with 2-4 extra Vision calls per header string. Note a gap when a read inside the span misses the string (the overlay at 5.3-6.6 s) |
| No ranking, 58 candidates | Rank by share x box area. Drop strings under 4 letters or under 0.5% of the frame. Count stray strings on `v1c044…nnvg` (junk `„waouo`), Tears of Steel and `reel_en`. Ship rule: 0 junk lines on all 3 |
| Cap of 30 vs gap of 10 s | State the rule: gap = max(10 s, duration / 30). Accept that long talks only catch strings that last for minutes. Slides already change, so today's change OCR reads them |
| Overlap with roadmap step 5 | Make Part A the single full-frame text pass on reels. Label each lasting string with a role (title, caption, watermark, scene text), as the BOVText benchmark does for KuaiShou and YouTube video (https://github.com/weijiawu/BOVText-Benchmark). Step 5 then reads the caption-role strings instead of running its own OCR |
| Phase 2 pointer detector | Keep it parked. If it ever runs, check the licence first: the repo's licence is "Other", and its YOLO baselines may carry AGPL-3.0 terms. Its own README says the metrics are synthetic only (https://github.com/ldenoue/cursor-motion-detector) |

Prior art checked: psc2code's cross-frame voting and its view of the cursor as noise
(https://soarsmu.github.io/papers/2020/Bao2020psc2code.pdf) fit the concept's use. Voting there works
with a code language model on top, which C07 drops, so C07 has less defence against repeated misreads.
The cursor-motion-detector figures (1.29 M parameters, about 1.3 MB ONNX, 98.82% precision within 8 px,
synthetic only) match the concept's claims.

Runs: Part A runs inside `see` on macOS only, after the picture pass. It makes at most max(30, duration / 10 s)
Vision calls on cached flow frames, plus 2-4 bisect calls per header string. It writes header or
timeline lines with an `OCR` mark and a role-labelled `lasting_text` list in `sensed.json`. Part B does
not run until a real code question unparks it. Then it runs on screen recordings at 5 fps or more, as a
pass over events plus one native ffmpeg seek that also gives the caret-box diff, and it writes
`text_NN.jpg`. Neither part adds a model, a download, a network call or a background process.

## Verdict

**Survives with changes.**

- Part A survives. The settling count is real, and the banner also corrects the speech. It needs these
  changes: a fold without tone marks plus a hedged vote; an `OCR` mark on the header; no speech-word
  exclusion for lasting strings; honest spans; ranking and junk rules; a 3-arm bench that scores tokens
  per right answer and letter-exact strings over repeated runs; and one shared text pass with roadmap
  step 5.
- Part B does not survive as written. It has no real user, and its own proof crop misses the line in
  focus. Park it behind real demand, and use the native-frame caret diff for the anchor before any
  bench run.
