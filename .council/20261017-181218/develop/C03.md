## C03 The dub as a forgery that must pass

## The concept

**For whom.** The owner's re-voice job ("re-voice YouTube into Vietnamese, keep the background"), run by an agent through his own tool `anydub` (`~/workspace/tools/anydub`). Today the agent says "done" and the owner listens. On one day, 2026-09-25, the owner heard four mix and timing faults after "done" (anydub `.okf/log.md` and `architecture/pipeline.md`):

1. The original English was audible under the dub, so voice separation became the default.
2. The voice was then removed completely. The owner "found the English too quiet and missed the ambience", so it came back, ducked to 0.3.
3. 34 of 78 lines overran their slot, with a mean lag of 3.67 s (anydub `decisions/timing-fit.md`).
4. QuickTime played the English track instead of the Vietnamese default.

The agent could measure every one of these from the two files. Nobody measured them.

**What it is.** Turn the forensic layer map around. A forensic examiner asks "was this voice laid on top later?" A dubber wants the answer to be *no*, even though the answer is yes. So the dub is a forgery that must pass, and **the original file is the answer key**. mediasift gets two dub-only modes. Neither mode adds anything to the default card.

- `mediasift see ORIGINAL --mix` prints the targets the dub must hit. It runs before the dub is made, and anydub's gains and speeds can be tuned to it.
- `mediasift see DUB --against ORIGINAL` prints **only the places where the forgery fails**, per line or per span, with the original's number beside the dub's number.

**What stays surprising.** You do not need a model of "good dubbing" or a human listener. A dub is good when every measure that would expose it as added in the edit reads the same as the original. You also get every bench case for free: break a dub on purpose in a known way (no ducking, voice removed, bed cut, line sped up, line late, dry voice), and the parameters you used are the key.

**A change from the pitch.** The pitch compared syllables per second across languages ("7.2 syll/s vs original 4.1"). English and Vietnamese syllable rates do not compare, so that number would mislead. On the one cached clip, the English original read 5.4 syll/s (vowel-group count) and the edge Vietnamese dub read 4.3. The dub was not rushed: the median dub line filled 0.76 of its slot. Pace is therefore judged against the dub voice's own median and a Vietnamese reference, and timing is judged against the original's line starts.

## How it works

Everything runs on the two files mediasift already decodes (16 kHz mono), plus `ffprobe`. One check needs one extra native-rate decode. No new model is used.

| check | signal | needs | example failure line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **original under the dub** (voice-over depth) | least-squares gain of the original's waveform inside the dub, per 0.5 s window: `g = <dub, orig> / <orig, orig>`. anydub keeps the timeline and passes the bed at gain 1.0, so `g` reads how much of the original survives | sample alignment (checked; crash if offset > 50 ms) | `00:41-01:10: original voice at 1.0 under the dub (target 0.3): English competes with Vietnamese` |
| **bed kept / cut / reset** | the same projection, but only in windows where the original has no speech (CED speech < 0.3): the bed's own gain. Bed level just before and after each picture cut (cut list already computed) | the original's CED speech runs and cuts | `bed gone 02:10-02:40 (original carries music)`; `bed resets at cut 00:41 (original carries)` |
| **ducking depth** (m05.6) | bed level in short pauses (< 0.35 s, ducker held) vs long pauses (released), on both files | ASR word gaps | `bed 4 dB under the dub; original sat 15 dB under and ducked` |
| **line timing** | onset of each dub speech run vs the original line it replaces (CED speech runs + ASR line times, paired by order and overlap) | ASR on both | `line 14 starts 2.1 s late; 9 lines late > 1 s, mean 1.4 s` |
| **pace** | dub syllables per second per line (QUAN/whisper word times; one Vietnamese word = one syllable) against (a) this dub voice's median, (b) a Vietnamese reference built from real Vietnamese speech in the cache | ASR word times | `line 22: 1.45x this voice's median (sped up); 6 such lines` |
| **loudness** | dialogue-gated level of dub vs original (BS.1770 style) | PCM | `dub 5.2 dB louder than the original` |
| **voice band** (m05.1) | highest band carrying the voice, from a native-rate decode of 2 s around each line | one native-rate ffmpeg pass | `dub voice band ends at 11.7 kHz; original voice ran to ~16 kHz (24 kHz TTS)` |
| **room** (m01.2/m05.2) | decay tail after line ends and noise floor in word gaps, dub vs original | PCM | `dub dry; original voice had room 1 (tail -18 dB at 100 ms vs dub -40)` |
| **track order** | ffprobe audio streams, language tags, default flag | probe only | `2 audio tracks, eng first: QuickTime on an en-VN Mac plays English` |

**Output shape.** One block, failures only, capped at about 15 lines. Repeats collapse to counts with times: `9 lines late > 1 s at 00:14, 00:52, ...`. If nothing fails, it prints one line that lists what was checked and what could not be checked. This comes from the m02.r2.4 counterview: a silent result must never read as "authentic". Example: `passes: depth, bed, timing, pace, loudness; not checked: room (original voice is dry, nothing to match)`.

**`--mix` targets.** The same measures on the original alone: `voice 37 dB over bed, bed ducks 0 dB, voice band to 16 kHz, room: dry close mic, 5.4 syll/s (EN), loudness -16 dBFS`. The voice-over depth is the owner's taste, not a property of the original. It is passed as `--voiceover 0.3` (anydub's `--orig-voice` default) and never hard-coded. The industry convention for UN-style voice-over is to let the original play 1-2 s first and then lower it to about 20%.[^un] anydub starts the dub at the line start with no lead-in. The check can report that difference when the owner asks for it.

**What the agent does with it.** The agent runs `--against` after anydub finishes. It fixes what it can, for example by re-mixing with `--orig-voice` or `--bg`, or by re-voicing late lines. Only then does it say "done". mediasift and anydub share no code and no contract file. The check reads any original/dub pair. That includes YouTube's own auto-dubbed tracks, which yt-dlp can fetch next to the original.[^yt]

**Measured already (this run, on cached anydub material, no files written).** I used `~/.cache/anydub/tDmjz6HB-yw` (286 s; it has the stems `voice.wav` and `background.wav`, `dub.vi.wav`, 78 edge and 78 ElevenLabs TTS clips, and the translation with times):

- **Projection works through real codecs.** I encoded the original to AAC 128k and the dub to AAC 160k, then decoded both to 16 kHz mono. The best alignment offset was 0 samples. The original's gain inside dub-speaking windows read **0.30 (-10.4 dB), IQR 0.28-0.34, n = 321**. anydub's true setting is 0.30.
- **It reads planted faults.** I re-rendered the dub from its stems with the original-voice gain set to 0 / 0.1 / 0.3 / 0.6 / 1.0. The projection read **0.005 / 0.103 / 0.298 / 0.591 / 0.983**.
- **Voice band.** Edge TTS falls from -24 dB (10-11.5 kHz, re 1-4 kHz) to **-77 dB at 11.5-12 kHz**. The original voice is still at -32 dB at 13-15 kHz, and ElevenLabs at -24 dB. This fits edge-tts's 24 kHz MP3 output.[^edge] The 16 kHz decode cannot see it, so this check needs the native-rate pass.
- **Voice over bed.** Original 37.7 dB, dub 37.0 dB (median per line). Bed and voice match on this clip because it has almost no bed (-52 dBFS).
- **Loudness.** On `douyin-clip-38359` the Vietnamese dub is **5.2 dB louder** than the original.
- **Room: no signal on this clip.** The tail 30-150 ms after a line end read -37.8 dB for the original voice and -41.5 dB for edge TTS, with overlapping IQRs. A close-mic podcast is as dry as TTS, so "room" correctly has nothing to report here. The room check needs a bench with a roomy original before it can show anything.

**Honest corpus note.** The owner's real dubs are mostly podcasts. In the three separated dubs, the bed sits 35-45 dB under the voice. The only music is the first minutes of `rMjVAnHmUeE` (bed 7 dB under the voice). On the owner's real jobs, therefore, depth, timing, pace, loudness and track order carry most of the value. Bed, ducking and room matter for reels and films with a score. Without the bench, "carry most of the value" is [INFERENCE].

## First move

This week, in `bench/dub/`, with media regenerated the way `bench/` already works:

1. `forge.py` reads an anydub cache (stems, TTS clips, translation times) and re-renders dubs with known faults: original-voice gain {0, 0.1, 0.3, 1.0}; bed muted over one span; bed swapped at one picture cut; 5 lines atempo 1.3x/1.45x; 5 lines shifted +0.8 s / +2 s; dub +6 dB. For room, it also renders a "roomy original" by convolving `voice.wav` with a public room impulse response. It then pairs dry TTS against TTS convolved with the same impulse response. The render parameters, per line, are the key.
2. Controls that must stay silent: the original against itself re-encoded (AAC 64k, two generations), and the real anydub dub against the value its stems give.
3. `check.py` contains the projection, the bed projection, line timing, pace, loudness and track order. That is about 200 lines of numpy over existing decode and ASR output. Run it on `tDmjz6HB-yw` and `douyin-clip-38359` first. Then run it on the first 10 min of `rMjVAnHmUeE`, which has an intro bed.
4. Write 8 agent questions per pair: "Is English audible under the Vietnamese? Was the background kept? Which lines are late or rushed? Is the dub louder? Which track will QuickTime play?" Grade the answers with and without the `--against` block, using the existing blind-grader method.

Runs: `see ORIGINAL --mix` and `see DUB --against ORIGINAL` only, never in the default `see`. The bench is an offline script with regenerated media.

## What must be true, and the cheapest test

| must be true | cheapest test | status |
|---|---|---|
| A dub and its original share one timeline, or can be aligned | cross-correlation on a 60 s span | **true** for anydub (offset 0). YouTube auto-dubs are unknown: crash with one line if the offset is > 50 ms or the durations differ by > 1 s |
| The original's gain survives AAC on both sides | encode both, project | **true**: 0.30 read for 0.30 set |
| Bed gain and voice gain can be read apart when both are present | project only in the original's non-speech windows; test with a forged dub that has a loud bed | open. The test clip has no bed. Use the `rMjVAnHmUeE` intro and a forged music bed |
| Line timing pairs dub lines with original lines | known shifts of +0.8 s / +2 s on 5 lines; recall at line level | open |
| A Vietnamese pace reference exists that marks "rushed" | QUAN word times on the cached real Vietnamese reels; check that the atempo-1.3x lines fall outside the reference's 90th percentile | open. I found no published Vietnamese syllable rate I would trust, so build the reference from real speech |
| The room check separates dry and roomed voice when the original has a room | the impulse-response bench above | open. It has **no** signal on a close-mic podcast (measured), so it must stay silent there |
| The agent acts on the lines | the 8 questions, with and without the block | open |

**Ship rule per check:** at least 0.9 recall on planted faults at line level, and at most 1 false flag per 20 lines on the controls. Any check that misses this stays in `sensed.json` and is not printed.

## Prior art (with URLs)

- **iZotope RX Dialogue Match** copies the reverb, EQ and ambience of the production take onto ADR or localized dialogue.[^dm][^dmrev] This is the *fix* side of the room check. It is a paid DAW plug-in that a mixer operates by hand. It does not report where a dub fails, and an agent cannot read it. The difference here: a local, read-only check that writes numbers for an agent.
- **Federico et al., IWSLT 2020**: Amazon's automatic dubbing pipeline adds background noise and reverberation taken from the original to the TTS output, and measures how natural listeners find it.[^fed] This supports the idea that background and room transfer matter to listeners. It renders the dub; it does not check one.
- **Brannon, Virkar, Thompson 2022**: a study of 319.57 h of professional dubs. It found that vocal naturalness and translation quality matter more than isometric and lip-sync constraints, gave a qualified view of timing constraints, and found that the source audio influences human dubs.[^bra] This is one reason the pace check compares within the dub voice and against Vietnamese, not against English syllable counts.
- **Isochrony-aware MT** and **VideoDubber** control translation length so that dubbed speech fits the source timing.[^iso][^vd] These work on the generation side. anydub already does a version of this (time budgets in the prompt, plus condense).
- **Malik & Farid, ICASSP 2010**: audio forensics from reverberation, used to detect splices.[^mf] This is the forensic idea this concept turns around.
- **Netflix sound mix spec**: for dubbed versions, the dialog stem plus M&E must equal the mix, at -27 LKFS ±2 LU dialogue-gated.[^nf] This is a ready loudness and stem rule. The projection check is a cheap way to test "M&E kept unchanged" without stems.
- **Dubbing QA checklists** (Ollang): after the mix, a human reviewer compares the dubbed audio with the M&E track and the video for loudness, lip-sync and naturalness.[^oll] Today this is human work. I found no tool that takes an original and a dub file and prints failures per line for an agent. That is the gap.
- **UN-style voice-over**: the original plays 1-2 s first, then is lowered to about 20%.[^un] This gives a convention-based target for the depth check.
- **edge-tts** outputs `audio-24khz-48kbitrate-mono-mp3` by default.[^edge] This explains the measured 11.7 kHz voice-band edge.
- **YouTube automatic dubbing** creates translated audio tracks, labelled "auto-dubbed".[^yt] These are real third-party pairs for realism checks. They have no stems, so they give counts only, not keys.
- **Inside this project**: the previous council's C01 "dub that checks itself" (`.council/20261017-140250/pool.json`) is kept low on `roadmap.md`. All three judges marked it down for coupling anydub through a `cues.json` contract, an auto-remix loop, and a calibration harness that must exist before anything can act. This version has no contract and no auto-remix. It only prints. Its core measure (projection) already has a first measurement.

## What it costs to run

| item | cost |
|---|---|
| money | $0/month. Local DSP only, no API, no quota |
| default-card tokens | 0. Never on the default card |
| per-check tokens | about 150-500 tokens (≤ 15 lines) per `--against` run, and only on dub jobs, which happen a few times a month |
| compute | the original's card is usually cached already. The dub needs one `see`: the okf records 120 s on a Mac for a 47 min Vietnamese dub, and less on a 5 min one. Projection, loudness and timing are dot products at 16 kHz, under a few seconds per hour of audio [INFERENCE: not timed]. The voice-band check adds one native-rate ffmpeg decode of short spans |
| memory | 16 kHz float32 is about 230 MB per hour per file. Process in 30 s blocks (as anydub's `mix` does), so a 91 min podcast pair stays flat |
| models | none new. It reuses CED, QUAN/parakeet/whisper and TitaNet, which are already pinned |
| things that break or need watching | (1) timeline mismatch on dubs anydub did not make: crash with one clear line, never a guess. (2) The owner changes `--orig-voice`: the target is a flag, not a constant. (3) ASR errors in the dub move line pairing: timing uses CED speech runs first and ASR text second. (4) The room check is silent on dry originals by design. Its "not checked" note must stay, so silence never reads as a pass. (5) An anydub change (for example, adding a lead-in) changes the timing truth: the bench re-renders from caches, so rerun it when anydub's mix changes |
