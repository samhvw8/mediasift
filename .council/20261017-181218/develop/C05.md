## C05 One deixis gate for eye and ear: the telestrator at the spoken word

## The concept

**The core idea:** the speaker's own words tell the blind sensor where to look and listen. The agent cannot watch, but it can read the transcript. When someone says a phrase, a gesture, a pointer or a sound is usually tied to that exact instant. mediasift already computes the word times, then throws them away when it folds words into sentence lines. This concept keeps them and uses them as an index into the picture and the sound.

**Who it is for:** the agent that reads the card and must answer questions like these:
- "What is he doing when he says X?"
- "What did she click when she said 'this one'?"
- "What was the sound after 'nghe này'?"

These are the deixis questions the bench asks and the agent misses. They also fit the real jobs: fact-checking a TikTok, turning a reel into a script, a bug-repro screencast.

**So the concept that survives is a telestrator driven by speech timing, not by a word list.** It has three parts, ordered by evidence:

1. **Word times kept** in `sensed.json`. This adds no card tokens. Every other part depends on it.
2. **`look VIDEO --say "phrase"`.** The agent quotes the words it read on the card. mediasift finds them by word time and returns a 15 fps strip over that span. This is the q06 fix: a delivery to the agent's eyes, not a new sense.
3. **Speech-snapped flow tiles** (the telestrator's freeze frame). On short camera footage, each ~1 fps flow tile moves by up to 0.5 s to the most stressed word of the speech line under it. The tile count stays the same and the card gains no line. For q06, the tile at 9.62 s would move to about 9.2 s.

The card **suffix** (the pointing gate) is kept as a gated fourth part. On screencasts only, a strict pointing phrase gets the pointer stop, selection or drawn-in overlay within ±0.4 s of the word. It ships only when screencast deixis questions exist on the bench, because no recorded question needs it today. The **ear gate** is parked with a clear condition for reopening it (see below).

## How it works

**1. Keep the word times (all backends, one shape).**
- `sensed.json` → `speech[i].words = [[start, end, text], ...]`. The card keeps sentence lines exactly as today.
- faster-whisper (local and on the GPU box) already returns words and folds them in `words_to_cues`. Keep them on the `Cue`.
- QUAN: `ctc_words` already returns timed words. Keep them.
- parakeet-mlx: `AlignedSentence.tokens` are subword tokens on an 80 ms grid. A token that starts with a space starts a new word, so merge on that rule.
- mlx-whisper: pass `word_timestamps=True`. That is the openai-whisper API, which mlx-whisper mirrors. `[INFERENCE]`: verify it in the pinned version.
- `serve_stdin` (the remote `--on HOST` path): add `words` to the JSON.
- Site subtitles have no word times. Store `words: null` and let `--say` fall back to the cue span. Say so on stderr. Never invent word times by spreading them evenly without saying so.
- Size: about 40 B per word. A 47-minute talk is about 11k words, so about 450 KB more JSON. No card tokens.

**2. `look VIDEO --say "phrase"` (the telestrator).**
- Match: lowercase, strip punctuation, and match a normalised token sequence against `words`. Fold Vietnamese tone marks for matching only. QUAN output has no punctuation, so do not use punctuation cues. Pick the best fuzzy match over the whole video. If two matches tie, list both with times and stop. Crash rather than guess.
- Span: from the first word's start minus 0.3 s to the last word's end plus 0.3 s. The margin covers word-time error: published Whisper word-timestamp evaluations use tolerances above 200 ms. Cap the span at 2 s and the strip at 24 frames.
- Frames: 15 fps through the existing `decode.frames_at`, which does one ffmpeg pass. Each tile is labelled with its time *and* the word being spoken at that time ("9.27 s · up"). The agent then reads the gesture against the words. Frames are full-frame at native resolution, cropped only when one event region covers the span (m03.4's crop rule). On q06 the only region is a 16%-area blob, so no crop.
- Sound strip: the existing `_sound_strip`, plus a tick at each word start.
- Card: one line in the existing zoom hint changes from "`look` ... for a gesture" to "`look VIDEO --say \"words\"` for what happens while a phrase is said". About 10 tokens, and they replace existing text.

**3. Speech-snapped flow tiles (stress, no word list).**
- This applies only to camera content with speech, and only under 5 minutes (reels and short clips). Long talks keep the fixed grid. Their tiles are not where the misses are, and a snap per line would mean thousands of decisions.
- For each flow tile time *t*, take the speech line that covers *t*. Find its most stressed word: the highest peak RMS of the word against this speaker's median word RMS. When two words tie within 1.5 dB, the longer word wins. Gesture research says a pointing gesture's apex anchors on the pitch accent (Esteve-Gibert & Prieto 2013). If that word lies within ±0.5 s of *t*, move the tile to the word's loudest 80 ms. Never move a tile across a cut, and never closer than 0.4 s to another tile.
- Cost: RMS over PCM already in memory. No model, no extra decode, because the tiles are picked before `frames_at` runs.
- Vietnamese: the rule needs no word list. That matters because the pointing word is often left out, and "này/đây" mostly carry the discourse sense (73 of 931 lines in the cache).

**4. Gated: the screencast pointing suffix.**
- This applies on `content == "screen"` only. A strict phrase opens a ±0.4 s window at the word's time, not the line's time. English triggers: "look at this/that", "this one", "that one", "right here", "click this", "like this". Vietnamese triggers: "xem đây", "nhìn ... này", "cái này" plus an imperative verb, "ở đây".
- The first pointing thing found in the window becomes the suffix: a pointer stop (the jitter filter in `see.listed` already finds pointer-sized motion), a selection-coloured block, or a drawn-in overlay with a lifetime. Example: `say "click this one" (pointing: stop over "Deploy", +0.2 s)`. The gap is shown to 0.1 s (m06.r1.6), so the agent and the bench both see how tight the match is.
- This is the only door the pointer gets onto the card. `see.listed` stays as it is for every other line.
- It ships only after 2 screencast deixis questions are on the bench and answered better with it than without it.

**5. Parked: the ear gate.**
- The design is unchanged: after "nghe này" or "hear that", read the next 3 s of CED scores at a lower threshold and print `(next: engine idling)` or `(next: nothing above the noise floor)`.
- It is parked because the 11 transcripts hold 0 real listening cues. Every English hit is rhetorical, and a 3 s window after a speaker who keeps talking is dominated by speech. The tagger names sounds under speech correctly 72% of the time (144 of 200 on the sound bench), so it would add a hedged line to a rhetorical phrase.
- **Reopen it when** a real job's transcript has a listening cue followed by a non-speech gap of 1 s or more. Then add that clip to the bench with a key written from listening.
- Meanwhile, `look --say` already draws the sensed sounds under the strip, so the agent can see whether anything sounded after the phrase.

## First move

All three steps fit in one week and need no new model.

1. **Fix q06's key.** The owner decides this, because it changes a bench file. My re-key: the hand sweeps from a fist at the chest (8.93–9.13 s), across the face covering nose and mouth (9.20–9.33 s), to a pinch (9.40 s on). Either accept "raised fist that moves up across his face", or narrow the question to "...when he says 'stay up'". Add m07.6's rule: each question records the time resolution its answer needs (`"resolution_s": 0.07` here). A question finer than its key is flagged, not scored.
2. **Failing test, then word times.** Write a unit test first: `words_to_cues` output keeps each cue's words, and a parakeet token list merges into the words "Don't stay up late". Then add `speech[i].words` across the four backends and the remote JSON. Check it: on reel_en, every word that starts a burned-in caption change lands within 0.1 s of that change. Today "doing" is at 9.68 s and the caption changes at 9.67 s.
3. **`look --say`, then one bench round.** Rerun reel_en and tiktok_vi arm C twice with the new zoom hint. Score q06 (re-keyed), q04 ("when that thing comes"), q02, q07 and q08. In the same round, write 10 new questions of the form "what is X doing / what is on screen when they say Y" on reel_en, tiktok_vi and cs50, all keyed from 15 fps strips.

Tile snapping (part 3) follows only if step 3 shows that agents skip `--say`. It would then be measured the same way: the same questions, snapped tiles against the fixed grid, with images read counted.

## Prior art (with URLs)

- **Gemini samples video at 1 fps by default.** Google's docs say this "may miss details in videos with rapid motion" and let the caller raise the fps for the whole clip. Gemini's opt-in *agentic video understanding* "selectively inspect[s] transcripts and adaptively adjust[s] frame rates" ([Gemini API video understanding](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding)). This is the closest product to transcript-indexed zooming. What still differs: Gemini is a paid cloud model and the model itself decides where to look. mediasift is local, deterministic, and anchored to word times the agent can quote. It costs 0 card tokens and its result is scored on a bench.
- **EgoPointVQA** (2026): a benchmark for answering questions from pointing gestures in egocentric video. It has 4,000 synthetic and 400 real videos. It finds that current multimodal models struggle and adds 3D hand-keypoint tokens; HINT-14B scores 68.1% ([arXiv:2603.12533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12533)). That work aims to make the model better at reading hands. C05 avoids hand models: speech timing chooses the frames, and the agent's own vision reads them.
- **Waken** (UIST 2012): finds cursors, icons, menus and tooltips in screen-captured tutorials by frame differencing, with no app knowledge ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2380116.2380129), [PDF](https://www.tovigrossman.com/papers/2012%20UIST%20waken.pdf)). This is prior art for the screencast pointer half. mediasift's grid already finds pointer-sized motion; C05 adds only the speech gate that lets it onto the card.
- **Gesture–prosody timing.** In contrastive focus, the apex of a pointing gesture anchors on the intonation peak, and prosodic phrasing bounds the gesture ([Esteve-Gibert & Prieto 2013, JSLHR](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/1092-4388%282012/12-0049%29)). Listeners quickly notice when a beat gesture comes later than its speech, but notice much less when it comes earlier ([Leonard & Cummins 2011](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2011-28879-002)). Together these support anchoring on the stressed word, with a window that reaches a little before it.
- **Word-timestamp accuracy.** Earlier Whisper word-alignment evaluations used tolerances "of more than 200 ms"; filtered attention heads reach 20–100 ms ([Yeh et al. 2025, arXiv:2509.09987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09987)). WhisperX gets word times by forced alignment with an extra phoneme model ([arXiv:2303.00747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00747)). C05 avoids that extra model and pads spans by 0.3 s instead.
- **parakeet-mlx** exposes `AlignedToken` start and end times per word or token in the same `transcribe()` call mediasift already makes ([senstella/parakeet-mlx](https://github.com/senstella/parakeet-mlx)). Word times cost nothing extra on the default Apple backend.

Nothing I found does the full combination: keep the ASR word times, let an agent quote a phrase to get a dense strip of that instant, and move sparse frames to the stressed word, all locally with 0 card tokens.

## What it costs to run

- **Card tokens:** parts 1–3 add 0 lines. The zoom hint changes its wording by about 10 tokens. Part 4, if it ever ships, adds about 8 tokens per strict screencast hit. Part 5 adds nothing while parked.
- **Images:** `look --say` is one sheet of up to 24 tiles, read only when the agent asks. That is no more than the zooms agents already run (61 recorded, median span 1.4 s). Tile snapping keeps the same tile count.
- **Compute:**
  - Word times are already computed by every backend.
  - Stress is RMS over PCM already in memory, at under 1 ms per line.
  - `look --say` decodes at most 2 s at 15 fps in one ffmpeg pass.
  - No new model, no GPU time, no ssh. The extra MLX memory is 0.
- **Disk:** about 40 B per word in `sensed.json`, about 450 KB on a 47-minute talk.
- **Money and quotas:** $0. No cloud API.
- **Things that break or need watching:**
  - A backend upgrade that changes token or word shapes. Mitigate it with the merge-rule test and the pinned model revisions.
  - Phrase-match failures on ASR mistakes. These crash with the candidate matches listed, never a silent wrong span.
  - Site subtitles with no word times. These fall back to the cue span with a stderr line.
  - Snap rules that drift from the bench. Re-check them on each real-bench round, the same as every heuristic, and record the result in `.okf/decisions/picture-sensing.md`.

Runs: $0/month; 0 new models; 0 card lines (one hint reworded); +~40 B/word in sensed.json; `look --say` = one ≤2 s ffmpeg decode on demand; snapping = RMS on in-memory PCM; watch = the parakeet/whisper word-shape test and the phrase-match crash path.
