Reading I picked: "give it that sense" includes new ways to *deliver* what is already measured (to the card, to `look`, to `transcript.txt`), not only new models. Through the Incumbent lens, every idea below keeps what the current design quietly gets right: one flagless command, a content-keyed cache that makes a second question free, a card that claims to be "complete for what the sensors detect", hedge words (`maybe`) tied to measured precision, camera footage kept to cuts and text (2-7 KB of texture noise removed), and an agent that is itself a better vision model than anything that fits in 16 GB.

Extreme users I designed for, run one by one:
- **Heaviest:** the agent linking timestamps across several 47-minute talks. It lives in `transcript.txt` and the cache, and rarely opens a sheet.
- **Most reluctant:** the agent that answers from the card alone to save tokens and never runs `look`. It is the one that missed "gesture timed to a phrase".
- **Most constrained (machine):** Linux with the GPU box booted into Windows: no OCR, no Core ML, CPU tagger only.
- **Most constrained (content):** the fast-cut code screencast that drew 20 of the 61 zooms. Dense text changes faster than ~1 fps tiles can show.
- **Most demanding of proof:** the TikTok fact-checker, who must know what the card *could not* have seen.

## Baseline (the obvious answers)
- Run a small local vision-language model to caption frames or shots ("a man points at a screen") and add the captions to the card.
- Detect faces, hands or pose so gestures and on-screen speakers become `pic` lines.
- Separate speech from music (stems), so dubbing keeps the background and the tagger hears music under speech better.

## Assumptions I broke
- **"A new sense means a new model that writes new card lines."** The incumbent's best assets are instruments it already pays for: Apple Vision (already loaded for OCR), CED's 527 scores in every window, word-level ASR times, the shot letters, the usage log and `fix`. Through this lens the cheapest new senses are new *joins* of signals already computed, written as suffixes on lines that already exist. They add no model, no download to pin and no new line type.
- **"The gap is in what the card says."** The reader is a stronger vision model than anything that fits next to MLX's 3.3 GB. The card's job is time, counts and sound; the images' job is content. Many gaps are pixels that never reach the agent's eyes at a readable size: dense code, a face during a phrase. A local captioner would throw that split away and would also break the card's "complete for what the sensors detect" promise, because a sampled caption is neither complete nor measured.
- **"The bench shows where the card falls short."** The bench asks only about picture timing. The real list of gaps is already being written down: every `look` call is an agent saying "the card was not enough here". The incumbent has a feedback channel (`fix`, the usage log) that a new-sense project would bypass.

## Ideas

### m03.1 Speech-gated motion: let a gesture onto the camera card only when a deictic word is spoken
On camera footage the card lists only cuts and text. That rule is right: it removed kilobytes of texture noise. It is also why the reluctant agent missed "gesture timed to a phrase", because a hand moving is never a line. Keep the rule, but open one gate: a local `motion` or `change` event inside about 0.4 s of a deictic word ("this", "here", "look", "that one"; Vietnamese "đây", "này", "kia", "nhìn") becomes a suffix on that `say` line, for example `say "put it here" (moved: small area at right, 00:12.3)`. The word times are already in the ASR output and the motion events are already in `sensed.json`, so the cost is close to zero and nothing new reaches the noisy footage. Judge: phone reel q06 plus about ten new "what moved when they said X" questions on the 6 bench videos.

### m03.2 A blind-spot ledger: the card says where its own senses were deaf or blind
The card's quietly valuable sentence is "complete for what the sensors detect". The fact-checker's problem is not knowing where the sensors could not detect anything. From signals already computed, write one short `Blind:` block: speech under music above X dB (ASR recall drops there, so lyrics are not transcribed), stretches too dark or blurred for OCR, camera footage where OCR ran only at cuts, `maybe` stretches of the tagger, sound that CED scored below every threshold yet was loud. This is the one "new sense" that makes the card shorter in effect, because the agent stops spending `look` calls proving a negative. Judge: add questions whose right answer is "the card cannot know" to the bench, and score whether the agent abstains instead of inventing an answer. That also fills the bench gap the brief names.

### m03.3 The zoom log as the sense-finder and bench-writer
61 recorded zooms are 61 labelled "the card failed here" moments, and the incumbent already logs usage and already has `fix`. Generalise `fix` to `mediasift note VIDEO A-B "what I needed / what I saw"`. The skill asks for it only after a `look` that answered the question. Notes group by content kind (screen or camera, the kind of span), and each one becomes a draft bench question with its answer. The heaviest users then write the bench for the senses they need. No council has to guess, and no new sense ships without a judge built from real use. Runs: one local JSONL, as `fix` does today.

### m03.4 Glyph sheets: give the agent's own eyes the dense text, not a model's reading of it
The fast-cut code screencast drew a third of all zooms because ~1 fps 320 px tiles cannot hold code. Keep what works (the change regions, OCR on changed regions only, every shot) and add one image kind for screen content. For each distinct text state of a changed region, deduplicated with the existing block grid, write one crop at native resolution: `text_NN.jpg`, labelled with times. Vision OCR still writes the card lines, but the agent reads the code with its own eyes from a sheet built only from distinct states. On Linux, with no OCR, this is the only text sense at all, and it needs no model. Judge: the code screencast's questions, counting how many zooms the glyph sheets replace and the image tokens they cost.

### m03.5 On-screen or off-screen speaker, attached to the `say` line
"Who is talking: the person on screen or a narrator?" is a deixis question the card cannot answer, and it matters for credited scripts, re-voicing and fact-checking a reel. Do not add a face model. Apple Vision's face-landmark request runs in the process that already does OCR. For each speech line on camera footage, sample 3-5 frames and measure how much the mouth landmarks vary. Write the result as a word on the existing tag: `[voice A, male, on screen]` or `[voice A, male, off screen]`, said once per letter when it is stable. Mac only, behind the existing platform guard. Linux leaves the word out, and the card says so instead of guessing. Judge: reels with a narrated voice-over against talking-head reels.

### m03.6 Slides into `transcript.txt`, where the heaviest user already looks
Over 20 minutes, the card drops to the lean picture policy. That is right for the token budget, but a talk's chapter structure is its slide changes, and the heavy user greps `transcript.txt`, not the card. Interleave existing shot letters and OCR titles into the transcript as `[02:14.0 slide F: "Attention is cheap"]` lines. The returns already work (`F` again means the speaker went back to that slide). Do not touch the card. The output is "linked talks with timestamps" built from picture as well as words, at the cost of one file write. Judge: "which slide was up when they said X", "when did they return to the diagram", on the CS50 lecture and one real logged talk.

### m03.7 New senses enter through `look`, never through `see`
`see` has properties worth defending: one flagless command, a content-keyed cache and a fixed cost that keeps the Mac responsive. Every sense added to `see` taxes every video, including the 730 cached cards nobody asked about again. The agent's habit is already to zoom on a 1.4 s span. Put new, expensive or unmeasured senses (face landmarks, full-resolution OCR, a pose estimate, an L-R stereo difference read from the original audio rather than the 16 kHz mono copy) behind `look VIDEO A-B --with faces|text|pose|stereo`. They run on seconds of footage, not on the whole file. A sense earns promotion into `see` only when the zoom log (m03.3) shows agents asking for it repeatedly. This is the incumbent's switching-cost argument made into a pipeline: a trial lane that cannot slow down or corrupt the default path.

### m03.8 Spend CED's unused scores before buying any new audio model
The music-mood work found mood and genre words already inside CED and previously discarded: 88% right, no new model. The same discipline applies to other questions. Mine the 527 scores already computed for classes that answer logged questions, for example crowd and applause (is this live?), "speech synthesizer" (is the voice AI-generated, for the fact-checker), "telephone" and "radio" futz, and "echo" or "reverberation" (indoor or outdoor). Score each one against a held-out AudioSet eval set before it gets a word. The incumbent's lesson is that its instrument was richer than its card, and a new model would throw away a judge that already works (CED's measured precision ladder). Runs: nothing new.

## The provocation I would put to the group
The cheapest sense mediasift can add is knowledge of its own edges. Before it learns to see something new, it should learn to say where it was blind and to let the agents' own zooms show which blindness is worth curing.
