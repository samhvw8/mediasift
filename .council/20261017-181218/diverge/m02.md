## Baseline (the obvious answers)
- Read the container metadata (encoder, creation time, device, CapCut/TikTok tags) and print a one-line provenance header.
- Run a deepfake / synthetic-voice classifier and put "maybe AI" on the card.
- Lip-sync check: does the mouth on screen move with the voice heard.

Reading I picked: "original, edited, re-voiced or staged" means the owner's real jobs (fact-checking a TikTok, checking a re-voiced YouTube dub, crediting a reel), not courtroom deepfake detection. A heavy deepfake classifier fails the cost rules and generalises badly to new generators, so I generate past it.

## Assumptions I broke
(Technique run: five assumptions, each flipped; ideas below are tagged with the flip they live in.)
- A1 "The video is one recording; the card describes its content." Flipped: every video is a stack of layers (picture, voice, music bed, captions, overlays) laid down at different times and places; the card should say where the seams are. -> m02.4, m02.5
- A2 "A voice belongs to a person, and the person heard is the person shown." Flipped: a voice is presumed to be added, synthetic or off-screen until the picture proves otherwise. -> m02.2, m02.8
- A3 "Decode to 16 kHz mono first; nothing useful is lost." Flipped: the downmix and resample destroy exactly the evidence an examiner reads first (stereo image, codec low-pass ceiling, per-layer bandwidth). -> m02.3
- A4 "File time is world time." Flipped: playback time lies (speed ramps, slow-mo by frame duplication, loops, reused clips), and there is a physical clock hidden in the media (mains frequency) that does not. -> m02.1, m02.7
- A5 "The file is the original thing." Flipped: the file is one generation in a chain of copies, re-posts and screen recordings; its history is a sense. -> m02.6

## Ideas
### m02.1 Mains-hum clock: where on earth, and where it was cut
Lights powered by the grid flicker at 100 Hz (50 Hz grid: Vietnam, Europe) or 120 Hz (60 Hz: US, parts of Asia); at 30 fps that aliases to a 10 Hz vs ~0 Hz brightness ripple, which the 32x18 grid means already sampled at full frame rate can show for free; the audio's 50/60 Hz hum (kept at 16 kHz) is a second witness, and a phase jump in the hum (ENF) marks a splice. One header line: "indoor light flickers at 50 Hz grid; mains hum 50 Hz, phase breaks at 01:12, 02:40". For the fact-check job this answers "was this really filmed in Texas?" with physics, at near-zero CPU; the bench writes itself by filming under known mains and splicing clips.
### m02.2 Stock-voice roster: "voice A is TikTok's 'Jessie'"
The speaker-embedding model already runs; keep a small pinned roster of embeddings for the stock TTS voices that dominate reels (TikTok, CapCut, Google and Zalo Vietnamese TTS, ElevenLabs default voices) and match voice letters against it. No new model and no "AI or not" verdict, only a named match with a distance, which a bench of generated clips per voice can grade exactly. It is not obvious because everyone treats voice letters as people; on reels, the most common "speaker" is a product.
### m02.3 Stereo and bandwidth witness before the downmix
Run one cheap stats pass on the native-rate stereo track before decoding to 16 kHz mono: the mid/side ratio per speech line (a voice-over added in the edit sits dead centre and dry over a wide stereo ambience; location speech shares the room's spread) and the spectral ceiling per layer (a voice recorded at 22 kHz laid over a bed that was once a 16 kHz-cutoff MP3 shows two ceilings). Card wording: "voice A: centred, dry, ceiling 20 kHz over a bed capped at 16 kHz -> recorded separately from the background". Directly checks the owner's own re-voicing job (did the Vietnamese voice sit in the original background or float on top) and the "listen to" reels.
### m02.4 Splice marks on quotes
A `say` line that spans a same-shot jump cut (shot letter X -> X within the line, which the cut list already finds) or a step in room-tone level and spectrum mid-sentence gets one mark: "spliced at 01:12.4". For fact-checking, the question "did she say this in one breath?" decides whether a quote is out of context, and today the card hides it: it stitches the words into one clean line. Bench: take real talking heads, cut words out, and require the mark at the right time and none on uncut lines.
### m02.5 Which sounds were there and which were added (continuity across cuts)
Location sound resets at a picture cut (new room tone, new ambience); post-production layers (narration, music bed, laugh track, sound effects) run straight across cuts. Comparing each sound layer's level and spectrum just before and after every cut gives one line: "narration and music continue over 23 cuts -> added in the edit; location sound only in shots C, F, K". This answers "was this said at the scene or narrated later?" and "is this crowd noise real?" from data the card already computes (cut list, CED windows, loudness), and it is the thing an examiner checks first when footage is said to be "raw".
### m02.6 Generation count: how many copies away from the camera
Combine free signals into a short provenance chain: container atoms (make, model, software, creation_time with offset, ISO 6709 location, handler names of TikTok, CapCut, Instagram), a 16:9 picture inside 9:16 with blurred bars (a re-post of a landscape original), effective resolution far below nominal (upscaled), a screen-of-a-screen (moire, a monitor edge, a cursor), and double-compression blockiness. Card header: "iPhone 15 Pro, 2026-03-04 09:12 +07:00 -> edited in CapCut -> re-posted as 9:16 with blurred bars; effective ~480p; at least 3rd generation". Tells the fact-checker to look for the original, and when the creation date contradicts the claimed event.
### m02.7 Time that lies: loops, slow-mo and reused footage
Exact-frame sequence repeats (not the same place, which shot letters cover, but the same pixels: a 3 s loop, the same B-roll used twice, a "different angle" that is the same clip mirrored) and duplicated-frame cadence (every frame shown twice = 0.5x slow-mo; a 3:2 pattern = frame-rate conversion) are found from the per-frame grid hashes already in memory. Card line: "00:04-00:07 is 00:00-00:03 played again, mirrored"; "00:12-00:20 slowed to 0.5x". Viral clips use both to make an event look longer or bigger, and the agent counting "how many times did it happen" is fooled today.
### m02.8 Whose mouth is the voice (and how late it is)
During speech only, run Apple Vision face landmarks (on the Neural Engine, already a macOS dependency for OCR) at ~10 fps and cross-correlate mouth opening with the speech envelope over lags of +/-1 s. Result per voice: "voice A is the face on screen, audio 0.28 s late" / "voice B matches no visible mouth -> off-screen or dubbed". It answers the re-voice and staging question, and the measured lag also corrects timing for the bench's one shared miss (a gesture timed to a spoken phrase). Bench: shift and swap audio tracks of real talking heads by known amounts.

## The provocation I would put to the group
Every sense so far describes what is in the video, but the one question no member can answer from content alone is how the video was made, and it is also the only sense whose bench we can manufacture perfectly, because we can make the edits ourselves.
