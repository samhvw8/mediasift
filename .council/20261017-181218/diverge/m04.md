## Ideas
### m04.1 Motion feel: "slide-like" vs "film-like", judder and easing, from the grid it already has
The moment: 09-23, the owner rejected the agent's generated video with "như phim vậy ko phải slide" (like a film, not slides) and asked for it to be redone smoother. The agent cannot see this: 1 fps flow sheets of a stuttering animation and of a smooth one look identical. The 32x18 grid is already computed at 15 fps, so its per-frame change series gives three cheap numbers: hold-then-jump ratio (share of change that arrives in single-frame jumps after holds), duplicate frames inside motion runs (judder, dropped frames), and the velocity profile of tracked motion (linear vs eased). One card line, only for screen or generated content: `motion feel: slide-like (holds 2.1 s, then jumps; 0% eased), 18% repeated frames while moving`. The judge is synthetic and exact: render the same p5 animation at 30 fps, at 6 fps, with dropped frames and with/without easing, and check the words.

### m04.2 Unworded voice: the speech the transcript silently dropped
The moment: 10-16, speech-coach: "it filter uhmm hmm .. it might not good for us". The okf already measured it: QUAN's extra errors on the 47 min talk are deletions, 33 of them fillers ("ừ", "ừm"). That is speech dropped silently, which the rules forbid. Combine two signals the pipeline already has: CED says "speech" in a window, and the ASR has no word there. Each gap over ~0.25 s inside a speaker's run becomes `(unworded 0.6 s)` inline in the `say` line, plus a per-speaker count (`voice A: 14 unworded pauses, 9 under 0.5 s`). It names no emotion and guesses no word, so it stays clear of the rejected emotion probes; it works the same for Vietnamese and English because it is language-free. Judge: the 33 known fillers on the talk, plus a hand-marked TED clip from prosody-coach (recall, and false hits on breaths and music).

### m04.3 Recording health: is this file intact before anyone sends it
The moment: 10-15/16, the owner's 2-hour challenge demo: "it boken i'm join it", "how can i recover -webcam.mp4?", "tôi chỉ có webcam nửa cuối" (I only have the webcam for the second half), then joining three crashed pieces and uploading a 5 GB file. The agent shipped it without knowing where the picture froze or the sound dropped out. Adapt broadcast QC to by-products mediasift already computes: runs of identical frames while audio continues (frozen picture; the screen/camera classifier already counts identical frames), audio flat-zero while the picture moves, timestamp gaps and decode errors from ffprobe, black or one-colour stretches, and the A/V offset at each join. One header line only when something is wrong: `health: picture frozen 41:12-41:50 (audio continues); 3 s of audio missing at 1:02:07 (join 2)`. Judge: synthetic corruptions (cut, freeze, mute, shift) injected into bench clips, plus the three real crash pieces.

### m04.4 Cut-to-fit ledger: where a long recording can be sped up without losing anything
The moment: 10-15, "i record but it got overtime ... should i speedup some part of video to make it below 2hr?" The answer needs a ranked list of stretches where nobody speaks and the screen does not change (or only a spinner or a progress bar animates), and the total time they add up to. The card already has silences and picture events, but long-video capping hides exactly these quiet stretches (`+N smaller changes`). Magnify them instead: `mediasift see --cuts` (or a footer line on videos over 20 min) prints `idle: 23 min 40 s in 31 spans; largest 12:03-16:40 (terminal output scrolling, no speech)` with an ffmpeg-ready list. Judge: on bench screencasts, removing the listed spans must keep every bench answer's time range and every speech line; the owner's real demo gives the real target (fit under 120 min).

### m04.5 A batch ear for generated clips: heard text, pace, and the off-by-one shift
The moment: 09-30, HSK learning site: "các audio trong từng từ này đang bị lệch ... từ đầu thì nó thành new word, từ 2 nó thành từ 1" (each word's audio is shifted by one) and "nghe nó rất gấp và bị dính từ" (rushed, words stuck together). The agent generated hundreds of TTS clips and the owner's wife found the bugs. Put mediasift to a new use: `mediasift hear DIR --expect labels.tsv` runs ASR on each short clip and reports heard text, a match score against its own label and against its neighbours' labels (a shift by one shows as clip i matching label i+1), syllables per second and the longest inter-word gap. The rushed complaint becomes a number (`5 clips over 6.5 syll/s; median 3.8`). Judge is free: the labels are the answer key; inject shifts and speed-ups to test. Chinese needs its own ASR choice (pinned), which makes the language gap visible rather than hidden.

### m04.6 The owner's noun, witnessed per shot: `see --check "bench"`
The moment: 10-13, gym-log: "Kéo tạ đơn 2 tay gập người hình như vẫn cần bàn, trong vid youtube hướng dẫn bảo thế" (this dumbbell row still needs a bench, the YouTube tutorial says so). The agent had picked the exercise from titles; the owner had to watch. Instead of captioning everything (baseline), eliminate all but the one noun the task depends on: score each shot's first frame against the given word with a pinned on-device image classifier (Apple Vision's built-in classifier on macOS, a small image-text model elsewhere) and print `bench: seen in shots C, F (00:41, 01:12); not in A, B`. Cost is one image per shot, run only when asked, never on the card by default. Judge: a small set of exercise and UI clips with yes/no per shot; the risk to measure first is whether open-vocabulary image matching does better than it did for music (it was rejected there).

### m04.7 Reverse: check the agent's own timestamped deliverable against the video
The moment: the most repeated real job, "extract transcript then detail summary then link then add it to artifact" (10-02, and on several talks). The owner clicks a `@12:30` link, and only then learns whether it lands on the quote. Reverse the flow: `mediasift verify VIDEO summary.md` reads every timestamp plus quoted or paraphrased phrase in the agent's output and checks it against the cached transcript and events (word overlap within +-5 s; nearest match if not). It prints `7/9 links land; @18:02 "AGI by 2030" is at 21:47`. Pure text matching over files already on disk, no model. Judge: the owner's past summary artifacts are a ready test set, and every miss found is a bug the owner would otherwise have found by clicking.
