## Baseline (the obvious answers)
- Detect faces, hands and people (pose model) so the card can say who gestures, and when.
- Track objects across frames so motion lines name a thing instead of "a gray area".
- Raise the flow-sheet rate (2-4 fps) around speech so brief moments are not missed.

## Assumptions I broke
- "A missing sense needs a new model": a match analyst codes from footage that is already captured. mediasift already computes far more than it hands over. On Tears of Steel, `sensed.json` holds 765 `change` events, 49 cuts, 39 blinks and 6 motions, and about 8 change lines reach the card. `asr.py` builds word timestamps (parakeet, whisper, QUAN's CTC path) and then `words_to_cues` folds them into sentence lines and throws the word times away. The cheapest new sense is data the pipeline already paid for.
- "The card is the deliverable": in match analysis, the highlight reel is not the data. The event file is, and the analyst queries it per question ("all shots from zone 14 after minute 60"). If the agent can query the full log, a count or duration question costs tokens only when someone asks it. That fits the per-token rule better than adding card lines.

Reading I chose: "who did what to whom" maps to actors mediasift already labels (voice letters, shot letters, screen regions), actions (say, change, motion, cut, sound) and targets (the next speaker, the region acted on). Technique order: I ran 15% solutions from most in reach (reach 1) outward. The ideas below are listed strangest first, as the format asks, and each one carries its reach rank.

## Ideas
### m08.1 Contact events: X moved into Y, then Y changed
Reach 7 (a join over existing events, no new decoding). In a match log, a tackle is two players' tracks meeting and one of them changing state. Motion events already store a `path`, and change events store a region. When a path ends inside or next to a region that changes within 200 ms, write one line: "the object moving from the left reaches the center-right area at 12.4; that area changes". That gives who-to-whom in pixels: a hit, a click on a target, a hand reaching a prop. Judge: add scripted collisions to the synthetic bench (the script is the answer key), and count how many stray contacts appear on Tears of Steel and the reels.

### m08.2 Possession chains in speech: who asked, who answered
Reach 5 (ASR punctuation plus voice letters, both already present). Analysts split a match into possessions: who held the ball, how it ended, who won it next. For talks and interviews, a "?"-ended line followed by a change of voice letter within 1.5 s marks an exchange: "Q voice B 12.0-15.3 → A answers 15.3-62.0". This answers "what was the interviewer's question, and who answered it". The roadmap's own evidence includes a real clip that drops the interviewer's question, and the owner's most common job is summarising talks with timestamps. Judge: write 8 question-and-answer questions on the CS50 lecture and two cached talks. Vietnamese needs a check first, because QUAN outputs lowercase text with no punctuation, so the "?" cue may be missing there.

### m08.3 Team sheet: bind voice letters to shot letters
Reach 4 (a join of `speech[].voice` with `shots`). An analyst's team sheet links shirt numbers to names, so every event says who did it. When a voice's lines fall mostly in one recurring shot letter, the usual shot-reverse-shot pattern, that shot is probably that speaker on screen. I measured it on Tears of Steel: voice D falls in shot E for 4 of 7 lines, and voice E falls in shot Y for 2 of 2. Voices C and D share shot E (a two-shot), and voice A is spread over 8 shots. So the binding is real but weak, and it should print only when one shot holds most of a voice's lines and that shot is not shared with another voice. Judge: "who is on screen while voice X speaks" questions on the 6 real videos. The same pass also shows which voices are always off-screen (narrators, voice-overs).

### m08.4 Disputed counts: count twice, print a range when the two counts disagree
Reach 6 (run the short-sound and blink counters again at a second threshold). Analysts double-code a sample of matches, and an event the two coders disagree on is marked disputed, not dropped. For "x2" sharp sounds and blink counts, run the counter a second time at a looser threshold, using CED scores and grid deltas that are already in memory. When the two counts agree, print the count as today. When they disagree, print "3-4". That way a wrong count shows up as uncertainty instead of reaching the agent as a confident number. Judge: the synthetic count questions plus the 200-clip sound bench. Compare exact-right counts, ranges that contain the true count, and ranges that miss it.

### m08.5 Box score: one line of match stats
Reach 2 (arithmetic over `sensed.json`). Every match report opens with a box score. Here it would be a single header line: cuts per minute for each third of the video, each voice's share of speaking time, silence share, longest shot, and text-on-screen share. That is about 30 tokens. It answers pacing and fact-check questions that the timeline makes the agent add up for itself: "is this mostly one person talking", "does the reel speed up at the end", "is this a stitched compilation (the cut rate jumps)". Judge: add 6 such questions to the real bench, and count the arm C answers that are right with the line versus without it.

### m08.6 Phrase-anchored replay: keep word times, add `look --say "this one"`
Reach 3 (the word times are already computed and then discarded). Analysts sync each tag to the broadcast frame, not to the minute. The one shared bench miss is "a gesture at a spoken phrase", and `look` takes a span, so the agent has to guess which part of a 4-second line holds the phrase. Write the word times into `sensed.json`, then let `look VIDEO --say "put it here"` find the phrase's word times and show frames at 6 fps from 0.5 s before it to 0.5 s after. This adds no card tokens and no new model. Judge: reel q06, plus 6 new deixis-at-a-phrase questions on the phone videos and CS50. The rule "a bug gets a failing test first" fits: q06 is that failing test.

### m08.7 The full match file: `mediasift log VIDEO` queries what `see` already sensed
Reach 1 (no new compute, no card lines, only a reader over `sensed.json`). The card is the highlight reel; the event file is the match file. Add `mediasift log VIDEO --kind change|blink|motion|sound --where bottom-left --between 60-120 --count`, which prints the matching events (or just a count) from the 860 events already stored. This turns count, duration and "how many times did the top-right change" questions into one cheap query instead of a `look` at 1.4 s spans. It is also the first step for m08.1 to m08.5, which are all queries over the same file. Judge: rerun the count and duration questions of both benches with and without `log`. The cost is tool-call tokens only when it is used, compared with a card line that is paid for on every read.

## The provocation I would put to the group
Before we invent a new sense, account for the 757 picture events and every word timestamp that mediasift already measured and then threw away before the agent could see them.
