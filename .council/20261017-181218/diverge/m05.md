## Baseline (the obvious answers)
- A better or bigger sound tagger (more AudioSet classes named right, fewer "maybe X").
- Source separation (Demucs-style stems) so the agent gets "voice / music / effects" for the re-voice job.
- A per-line acoustic-space word (phone, radio, far, reverberant) - the "futz" tag already in the roadmap pool.

## Assumptions I broke
- "A video's sound is a list of things that happened": a sound designer hears a mix, a stack of separate recordings, each with its own origin, microphone, room and level. Which layer came from where (the camera mic, a lav, a library, a TTS model, a different day) answers fact-check, re-voice and credit questions that no class name can. The tag list says *what*; nobody senses *whose recording*.
- "16 kHz mono is enough audio to sense with": that decode throws away exactly the two things a mixer reads first - the stereo field (where a sound sits, and whether it was recorded or added in post) and the band above 8 kHz (the fingerprint of each layer's source and codec). Also: the silence between words is not "nothing"; it is room tone, the best evidence of where and when a line was recorded.

Reading I picked: "perceive" means "answer a question about", so each idea names the question it unlocks and a synthetic judge with known answers. Ideas come from a morphological box of 4 dimensions - property heard (provenance / space / layering / repetition), signal (native-rate spectrum / stereo channels / gaps between words / self-similarity / the existing picture cuts), delivery (card word only on disagreement / `look` strip / new query) and judge (resampled or re-mixed synthetic clips with known truth) - and each idea names its cell.

## Ideas
### m05.1 Spectral ceiling per layer: which recording each sound came from
Cell: provenance x native-rate spectrum x card word only when layers disagree x resampled synthetic clips. Decode once at the file's own rate (no extra model, one FFT pass) and measure, per speech line and per music bed, the highest band that carries energy: a voice that stops dead at 4 kHz, 8 kHz, 11.025 kHz or 12 kHz inside a 48 kHz file was recorded or generated at that rate (phone call, old clip, 24 kHz TTS / voice clone) and pasted in, while the music bed under it runs to 18 kHz. The card says nothing when every layer shares one ceiling and one word when they differ ("voice band ends at 12 kHz, music at 19 kHz: recorded apart"), or when the ceiling jumps inside one line (a splice from another source). Nobody proposes it because the mono 16 kHz decode erased the evidence before any sensor ran - the same move as reading the picture codec's own decisions ([codec as evidence]). Judge: LibriSpeech and TTS voices resampled to 8/16/22/24 kHz and mixed over full-band music at known times; count right flags and false flags on the 6 real bench videos.

### m05.2 Room letters: shot letters for the sound
Cell: space/provenance x gaps between words x card letters x synthetic beds. The card gives repeated picture places the same shot letter; give recordings the same treatment from the noise floor in the pauses between words (spectral shape and level of the 0.2-1 s gaps, plus whether the gaps are digital zero, which means gated, studio or generated). Then `say [voice A, room 1]` vs `[voice A, room 2]` answers "were these two clips recorded in the same place and session?", "is this voice dubbed over that picture?" and "which part of this talk was inserted from elsewhere?" - and a voice that keeps its room while the picture changes rooms is a voiceover. Show it only when a video has more than one room. Judge: LibriSpeech lines laid over different real ambient beds (DEMAND or ESC ambient clips) and different gains, assembled in known orders; score letter agreement the same way the voice bench scores speaker letters.

### m05.3 Does the sound cut with the picture? Real time vs montage
Cell: provenance x existing picture cuts + mono spectrum x one word per cut run x re-edited synthetic takes. At every cut the card already lists, compare the sound bed 0.5 s before and after: production sound changes at a cut (new mic position, new room), while a laid-over track or a multicam event carries straight through. A run of cuts where the bed carries ("sound continuous across 6 cuts, 12.0-31.5") tells the agent those shots show one event in real time from several angles; a bed that resets at each cut says it is a montage of moments. That is a core fact-check question ("is this one incident or a compilation?") and a re-voice question (where the background can be cut). Judge: one continuous recorded take cut into picture shots vs the same audio shuffled at the cuts; plus the cut lists of the real bench videos with hand labels.

### m05.4 Same sound twice: the exact-copy detector
Cell: repetition x self-similarity of mel frames already computed x card note on the counted hit x synthetic repeats. Library effects, canned laughs, a trending TikTok sound and a looped whoosh are byte-for-byte or near-exact copies; a real event never repeats exactly. Hash short spans of the log-mel frames the tagger already computes and mark a counted sound or bed that is a copy: `whoosh x4 (identical copies: added in edit)` and, across the cache, "same audio as card 9f3a... at 00:12" for the trending-sound case. For counting it also explains why a clap counted 8 times sounds machine-even. Judge: the same ESC-50 clip spliced in several times vs different clips of the same class (must not match), plus pitch-shifted and re-encoded copies to set the threshold.

### m05.5 Where each sound sits: stereo pan and width
Cell: space x stereo channels at a low rate x a word on counted hits and voices only when off-centre x panned synthetic clips. Decode the two channels once (cheap) and attach the pan of each counted hit and speech line ("knock x3, left"; "voice B, right") plus the width of each bed: dialogue from the camera is near-mono in the centre, while music and effects added in post are usually wide stereo - so width separates "recorded on set" from "added in edit" for free. Pan also answers the bench's deixis gap from the ear side: which of two people on screen is talking, and whether a sound came from off screen. Files with one channel or identical channels get no word. Judge: ESC-50 and LibriSpeech panned to known positions and widths; real videos with known layouts.

### m05.6 Mix depth and ducking: what a re-voice must match
Cell: layering x levels in short vs long speech gaps x one note per music bed x synthetic sidechain mixes. For the real job "re-voice into Vietnamese keeping the background", a mixer needs how far the bed sits under the voice and whether it ducks. Compare the bed's level in short pauses inside speech (where a ducker has not released) with long pauses (released): `(music sits ~15 dB under the voice and ducks while voice A speaks)`. That tells the agent the bed was post-mixed (so a clean music-only stem may exist on the source page), and gives the dub its target gain, without running separation. Judge: synthetic voice-over-music mixes at known gains with and without sidechain ducking; the re-voice output itself as a round trip.

## The provocation I would put to the group
The card tells the agent what was heard, but a sound designer would ask whose microphone heard it - and the 16 kHz mono decode deletes the stereo field, the top half of the spectrum and the room tone, which together answer "was this recorded here, now, in one take?" better than any new tagger.
