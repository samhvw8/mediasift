### m04.r1.1 One seam detector, two users: the fact-checker's cut quote and the owner's own crash joins
Builds on: m01.1 + m07.5 + m04.3
m01.1 and m07.5 find seams in other people's clips; m04.3 checks the owner's own joined 2-hour demo ("it boken i'm join it"). They are the same detector (room-tone jump, too-short gap, broken sentence, plus ffprobe timestamp gaps and frozen frames), so ship it once and print it as `seam` on a `say` line and in the `health` header. What this adds: the owner's three crash pieces are a real-footage answer key that costs nothing, because the join times are the sums of the piece durations, so the seam sense is scored on a real screen + webcam recording instead of only on synthetic LibriSpeech splices. It also answers the question the owner actually asked after the join and nobody could: "did the join lose any of what I said?"

### m04.r1.2 Coach readout: the dropped "ừm" measured against the speaker's own clock
Builds on: m04.2 + m01.3 + m01.4
m04.2 finds voice the transcript silently dropped (CED hears speech, ASR has no word); m01.3 reads pace and pauses against the speaker's own baseline; m01.4 marks overlaps, where ASR loses the most words. Combined, they form what the speech-coach job ("it filter uhmm hmm .. it might not good for us") needs and none had alone: a per-voice ledger that says `voice A: 14 unworded fillers (2.1/min, her first 5 min 0.8/min), 3 pauses over 3x her median before answers, 2 step-ons where words may be missing`. The judge is the 33 fillers QUAN already deleted on the 47-minute talk, plus the owner's own practice recordings, where he can say yes or no to each flagged moment in a minute.

### m04.r1.3 Dub check that hears what the owner's wife heard: pace in the slot, background kept, voice marked synthetic
Builds on: m07.3 + m01.2 + m04.5
The re-voice job (YouTube into Vietnamese, keep the background) and the HSK clips ("nghe nó rất gấp và bị dính từ", rushed and stuck together) fail the same way: TTS squeezed into a fixed slot. m07.3 stops erasing "Speech synthesizer", m01.2 gives the sound a room letter, and m04.5 measures syllables per second against a label. Pushed together they become `mediasift see DUB --against ORIGINAL`: per line, the dub's syllables/s against the original speaker's own pace in the same slot (`line 14: 7.2 syll/s, original 4.1, rushed`); whether the music bed and room letter under the dub match the original's (background kept or lost); and `voice A: synthetic` so the agent knows which track it is reading. The original file is the answer key, so the judge needs no human: speed the dub up, drop the bed, or shift a line, and check the words.

### m04.r1.4 Every link in the summary lands, on a quote that was actually heard, at the moment the room reacted
Builds on: m04.7 + m01.5 + m07.8
The most repeated real job is "transcript, detailed summary, links, put it in an artifact"; the owner finds broken links only by clicking. m04.7 checks each `@12:30` against the transcript, m01.5 ranks lines by the laughter or applause after them, and m07.8 asks whether a word's ASR probability predicts errors. The bolder version runs both directions in one pass: before writing, the agent gets candidate link moments (the lines the room reacted to, which text alone cannot find); after writing, `verify` reports links that miss and any quote in the summary that contains a low-confidence name or number (`@18:02 "Karpathy" heard at p=0.31, check before publishing`). A summary is then wrong only where the audio itself is unclear, and it says where. Judge: the owner's past summary artifacts, with every link clicked once by hand.

### m04.r1.5 Cut-to-fit with a program log: what can go, ranked by what it is
Builds on: m04.4 + m01.6 + m07.4
m04.4 lists idle stretches in the 2-hour demo ("should i speedup some part of video to make it below 2hr?"); m01.6 splits a recording into segments by changes in bed, room and pace; m07.4 notes that the agent cuts long cards with `| head`. Pushed ten times further: on any video over 20 minutes, the top of the card becomes a program log, `00:00-04:10 setup (no speech, terminal), 04:10-38:00 talk A, 38:00-41:50 frozen picture, ...`, with a cut budget per segment (`can drop 23 min: 12 min idle terminal, 4 min frozen, 7 min repeated retries`). That one block is both the agent's map of a long file that survives `| head -40` and the owner's edit plan with an ffmpeg-ready list. Judge: removing the listed spans keeps every bench answer and every speech line, and the owner's real demo must land under 120 minutes.
