## Builds
### m02.r1.1 Two-witness seams on kept word times
Builds on: m02.4 + m01.1 + m08.6
A seam is checked by two independent witnesses at each word gap, now that word times are kept (m08.6): the sound witness (room-tone level and spectrum step, gap shorter than this speaker's shortest natural gap, from m01.1) and the picture witness (same shot letter before and after inside one `say` line, from m02.4). The card prints the witness count, `seam 01:12.4 (sound + picture)` or `seam 01:12.4 (sound only)`, so the agent can weigh it the way an examiner weighs corroboration, and a false alarm needs two senses to fail at the same instant. `look --say "phrase"` then answers the fact-checker's real question in one call: "this phrase crosses a seam at 01:12.4" or "spoken in one take". The bench is manufactured: a script cuts words out of the uncut talks and reels (English and Vietnamese), with and without matched room tone and with and without a covering cutaway, and the script's cut list is the answer key.

### m02.r1.2 Layer map: which voice was there and which was laid on top
Builds on: m01.2 + m02.5 + m08.3 + m02.3
Each voice letter gets one verdict from three cheap witnesses that none of the parents combined: its room letter (m01.2), whether its room and level reset at picture cuts or run straight across them (m02.5), and whether it binds to one shot letter (m08.3). Location sound has a room that matches its shot and resets at cuts; an added layer is dry, keeps one room across all cuts and binds to no shot. One line per voice: `voice A: room 1, in shot E, resets at cuts -> recorded on location`; `voice B: dry room 0 across 23 cuts, no shot -> added in the edit`. Where the native stereo track exists, the mid/side and bandwidth ceiling from m02.3 is a fourth witness. This directly checks the owner's re-voicing job (does the Vietnamese voice sit in the kept background or float on it), and the bench is built by mixing known voice-overs onto known location clips.

### m02.r1.3 `mediasift diff A B`: what the re-post removed, reordered or re-voiced
Builds on: m08.7 + m02.6 + m02.7
Push the match file ten times further: if every video has a queryable event log (m08.7) with per-frame grid hashes and word times, two logs can be aligned. `diff` takes the clip and a candidate source (the full talk, the original upload found by the fact-checker) and prints an edit list: `clip 00:00-00:14 = source 12:03-12:17; source 12:17-12:41 removed; clip 00:14-00:31 = source 13:02-13:19 (voice replaced, picture same)`. That turns "was this quote taken out of context" and "is this the original" into a checkable answer instead of an inference from one file, and it also gives the re-voicing job an exact map of what was kept. Cost is a hash join over data both runs already store, with no new model. Bench: generate edited versions of the sample videos (cuts, reorders, re-voiced spans, crops to 9:16) and score the edit list against the generator's script.

### m02.r1.4 Distinct counts: "5 shown, 3 distinct"
Builds on: m08.4 + m02.7
A disputed count (m08.4) covers coder disagreement, but the commonest wrong count on viral clips is a correct count of a replayed event: the same hit shown three times from a loop, a slow-mo replay or the same B-roll mirrored. Before printing any count, check each counted event's frame hashes and sound window against the others; replays collapse into one. The card says `x5 (3 distinct; 2 are replays of 00:04, one mirrored)`, combined with the range when the threshold counts disagree. Bench: take the synthetic count clips, splice in exact and mirrored replays, and score both numbers.

### m02.r1.5 Machine-read voices from the speaker's own clock
Builds on: m01.3 + m02.2
The speaker's own clock (m01.3) gives a second, model-free witness for the stock-voice roster (m02.2): human speech has a wide spread of word gaps, pace and level, while a TTS voice reads with near-fixed gaps, no hesitation outliers and a flat level across a whole reel. Print it only as measurements next to a roster match, `voice A: matches CapCut "Ngoc" (distance 0.21); gaps spread 0.08 s vs typical human 0.35 s`, so a new TTS voice that is not in the roster still shows up as "reads like a machine" by its rhythm. This keeps the rejected-emotion trap out, because it measures regularity, not feeling. Bench: generated clips for each roster voice plus unseen TTS voices plus real speakers in English and Vietnamese; score roster matches exactly and score the spread rule on the unseen voices.

### m02.r1.6 Provenance box score: one header line for the fact-checker
Builds on: m08.5 + m02.6 + m02.r1.2 + m01.6
The box score (m08.5) gets a provenance half, about 30 tokens, written before the timeline: container chain and creation time with offset (m02.6), generation count, how much of the runtime is location sound versus added layers (from the layer map), the number of seams, and the hot-clock segments that are scripted inserts (m01.6). Example: `iPhone, 2026-03-04 +07:00 -> CapCut -> re-post 9:16; 3 seams; 70% narration added in edit; 00:41-01:05 sponsor read`. It answers "how was this made" before the agent reads a line of content, and only the fields that fire are printed, so a clean original costs one short line. Bench: six to eight "how was this video made" questions per real video, scored with and without the line.
