## Builds

### m07.r1.1 The correction census: count the owner's "after done" fixes, not the zooms
Builds on: m07.7 + m04 (assumption broken: complaints caught by the owner's ear after the agent said done) + m06 (the bug-repro job)
m04 found the best evidence of a perception gap: an observed failure, where the owner corrected media the agent had called finished ("rushed", "shifted by one", "slides not film", "broken"). m07.7 only joins `see` calls to answers. The build is one offline script over 30 days of session transcripts. It finds every owner turn that corrects a media claim or deliverable, whether or not mediasift ran, and labels the sense that would have caught it: picture-timing, motion feel, intactness, speech words, unworded voice, pointer/input, persistent text or object. The cheapest settling observation is the histogram itself. The next sense is the tallest bar that has an answer key on disk. Any sense whose bar is zero (picture-timing, today's whole bench, may be one) is deferred until a correction shows up. Runs: nothing new (one script, run by hand).

### m07.r1.2 One stillness ledger with two triggers, keyed by frame number
Builds on: m06.2 + m04.3 + m07.6
"No response after the click" (m06.2) and "picture frozen while the audio continues" (m04.3) are one detector. Both look for a channel that stays still while the other channel is active. One ledger built on the 15 fps grid series covers both, so there is one threshold to tune instead of two. It also fixes the key-resolution flaw in m07.6. The answer is a frame number, so the key can be written by script from synthetic injections (a freeze, a mute or a delayed reaction spliced into bench clips) at 1/15 s resolution, with no 2 fps key writer in the loop. Cheapest settling observation, before any code: check whether the cached grid distances separate a true duplicate frame from a static slide or a still talking head. Plot the distance histogram on the 4 landscape bench videos plus one injected 3 s freeze. If the two overlap, the grid cannot carry the sense, and it needs a per-frame hash (one ffmpeg `framemd5` pass) instead.

### m07.r1.3 `verify` as the bench that writes itself (ten times larger)
Builds on: m04.7 + m07.1 + m07.6
m04.7 checks one summary. The bolder version runs `verify` on every timestamped deliverable the agent writes from a card (summaries, credited scripts, fact-check claims, repro steps) and appends each claim and its result to `usage.jsonl`. Real jobs then produce a growing bench of real questions with checkable answers, and the hand-written 48-question bench stops being the only judge. The skeptic's addition is to split claims by what can check them. Speech-anchored claims are checked automatically against the transcript. Picture claims and sound claims are marked "unverifiable by text". The size of that unverifiable share, per job type, is the measured demand for picture and sound senses that the brief says the bench cannot see. Cheapest settling observation: run a 50-line matcher over the owner's past summary artifacts. Count the claims, the share that is speech-anchored and the links that miss. If no link misses and almost no claim is a picture claim, the next sense is not a picture sense.

### m07.r1.4 Where the tagger and the ASR disagree: one calibration table for four marks
Builds on: m04.2 + m07.3 + m07.8
Unworded voice (CED hears speech, the ASR wrote nothing), a synthetic narrator (CED says Speech synthesizer, the ASR transcribes it fine), singing over a `say` line, and a low-confidence word are all one thing: two sensors that disagree about the same 3 s window. Build a single disagreement pass over scores the pipeline already computes, so the running cost is zero. Judge all four marks at once on one calibration table with keys that already exist: the 33 known filler deletions on the 47-minute talk (unworded), the official Blender subtitles for Tears of Steel (word confidence AUROC), 10 TTS against 10 human reels (synthetic), and a few music videos with lyric captions (sing). Each mark ships only past its own threshold, and the table records every number, including the marks that fail. Cheapest settling observation: overlay CED speech > 0.5 windows that have no ASR word on the 47-minute talk, and count how many of the 33 fillers fall inside them and how many hits are breath or music.

### m07.r1.5 OCR what stays, near the hand, but first check that the hand is visible
Builds on: m06.1 + m06.8 + m07.2
m06.8 (OCR around the pointer and caret) and m07.2 (OCR text that persists) attack the same blind spot: changed-region OCR misses text that matters but does not change. Together they give one per-shot full-frame OCR. Text that lasts 5 s or more is printed once, and the lines nearest the last pointer stop or caret are printed first. The skeptic's addition fixes two premises before anyone builds a pointer tracker. First, my log audit found that the 20 zooms on the "code screencast" were a unit test, so replaying them is not a valid judge. Replace it with questions keyed from the caret line on the cs50 and python_100s screencasts. Second, macOS screen recording and many screencast tools hide or redraw the cursor. Cheapest settling observation: count the frames with a visible pointer in the owner's bug-repro recording and in the bench screencasts. If fewer than half the frames show one, ship the persistent-text half and park the pointer half.

### m07.r1.6 A batch ear and eye for generated media, with the generator as the answer key
Builds on: m04.5 + m04.1 + m07.4
m04.5 (hear a folder of clips against `labels.tsv`) and m04.1 (motion feel) share a property no real-footage sense has. When the agent made the media, its own inputs are an exact key: the label list, the p5 frame rate and the easing curve. Push this into one `mediasift check DIR --expect manifest` that the generating agent runs before it says done. It reports one summary line per folder, worst items first, and never one card per clip, so 300 clips cost about 10 lines instead of 300 cards cut short by `| head`, which is m07.4's truncation risk. Off-by-one shift, syllables per second and slide-like motion are each a number compared with the manifest, so the judge is in the repo from day one. Cheapest settling observation: rerun the 09-30 HSK clip set (or regenerate 20 clips and shift one label) and confirm that the neighbour-match score flags exactly the shifted run.
