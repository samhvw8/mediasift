## Builds
### m01.r1.1 Two-witness seams: a splice is printed only when independent clocks disagree together
Builds on: m01.1 + m02.4 + m02.1
A courtroom rule ported to editing: one witness is a hint, two independent witnesses are a finding. Each word gap and each `say` line is checked by four witnesses that share no signal path: room-tone step (level and spectrum, m01.1), a gap shorter than this speaker's own shortest natural gap (m01.3), a same-shot jump cut inside the line (m02.4), and a phase break in the 50/60 Hz mains hum, which survives the 16 kHz decode (m02.1). The card prints `spliced 01:12.4 (room tone +6 dB, hum phase break)` only when at least two agree, and names which ones, so the false-alarm rate is set by a rule rather than by tuning each detector alone. The bench gets a second axis for free: inject splices that fool one witness on purpose (matched room tone, no picture cut) and measure how many still get caught by the other three.

### m01.r1.2 Dead-air alarm with a filler track: the speaker's clock counts the ums
Builds on: m04.2 + m01.3 + m04.3
Every radio station runs a silence sensor that pages the engineer after N seconds of dead air, and a producer separates dead air from a filled pause because they mean opposite things. Combine m04.2's unworded voice with m01.3's per-speaker baseline: each pause before a turn is classed as empty (no speech energy), filled (CED says speech, ASR has no word: "ừ", "uhm") or breath, and only outliers against that speaker's own distribution reach the card: `(answer after 2.6 s, filled; her usual 0.4 s)`. The per-speaker unworded count doubles as an ASR-health line (`voice A: 14 unworded spans, 6% of her speaking time`), so dropped fillers stop being silent deletions, and the same sensor flags dead air in a long recording for m04.3's health header. Judge: the 33 known QUAN filler deletions plus listener-labelled "hesitated before answering: yes/no".

### m01.r1.3 Delivery spec: `mediasift qc` as the broadcaster's master-tape rejection for everything the agent makes
Builds on: m04.5 + m04.1 + m01.3
Broadcasters reject a delivered master that misses a written tech spec (EBU R128 -23 LUFS, true peak under -1 dBTP, no clipped onsets, a set head and tail silence), and nobody has to listen to know it failed. Push m04.5 from one HSK folder to every piece of media the agent generates: a spec file per job (HSK clip: 3-5 syll/s, at least 120 ms between syllables, 200 ms head silence, -16 LUFS +-1; reel: -14 LUFS, motion not slide-like per m04.1) and one pass/fail table, with heard text matched against labels and neighbours for the off-by-one shift. "Rushed and words run together" becomes two failed columns (rate over spec, inter-syllable gap under spec) before the owner's wife hears it. It is pure DSP plus the ASR already pinned, the spec is the answer key, and it runs only on the agent's own output, so it never touches the card's token budget.

### m01.r1.4 Bed and sync: room letters across picture cuts, and the re-voice match test
Builds on: m01.2 + m02.5 + m02.3
In radio a "bed" runs under everything and "sync" is sound recorded with the moment; a producer knows which is which by whether it changes when the scene changes. Track room letters (noise floor plus reverb tail, m01.2) against the picture's cut list (m02.5): a room that runs unchanged across 23 cuts is a bed or a voice-over, a room that changes with shot letters is location sound, and the card says `voice A: room 1 across 23 cuts (added in edit); location sound changes with shots C, F, K`. Then point it at the owner's own re-voicing job: compare the dub's room fingerprint and stereo width (m02.3) against the original speaker's room on the same shots, and print `dub sits 14 dB drier and dead-centre over a wide room -> sounds pasted on`, which is the exact thing a Vietnamese viewer would hear. Judge: render dubs with and without matched reverb and require the words to flip.

### m01.r1.5 Trim to time, in tiers: the producer's cut list for the 2-hour demo
Builds on: m04.4 + m01.6 + m01.r1.2
A radio producer hitting a hard out trims in a fixed order: dead air first, then fillers and breaths, then gaps tightened to the speaker's normal, then whole segments, and they know the seconds each tier gives before touching anything. Extend m04.4's idle list into that ladder: tier 1 idle (no speech, no picture change), tier 2 unworded fillers (m01.r1.2), tier 3 pauses longer than the speaker's 90th percentile shortened to their median, tier 4 segments from the hot clock (intro, recap, sponsor, m01.6). The footer becomes a budget: `over 120 min by 11:40; tier 1 gives 23:40 -> done without touching speech`, with an ffmpeg concat list per tier. Judge: after applying each tier, every bench answer's time range and every transcript word must survive, and SponsorBlock still grades tier 4.

### m01.r1.6 Same tape played twice: audio repeats expose canned laughs, looped beds and reused sounds
Builds on: m02.7 + m01.5
m02.7 finds pixels shown twice; a producer hears the same thing in sound, because a cart machine plays the identical laugh or sting every time while a live audience never laughs the same way twice. Fingerprint the 16 kHz stream with a landmark hash (Shazam-style peak pairs, pure DSP, seconds per hour) and report exact repeats: `laughter at 03:41, 05:10, 07:22 is one recording (canned)`, `music bed loops every 12.0 s`, `00:31-00:36 audio reused from 00:02`. This upgrades the reaction ledger (m01.5) from "which line got the laugh" to "was the laugh real", and for reels it names when a trending sound was pasted rather than recorded on location. Judge: insert known repeats and loops into bench audio, and use live stand-up as the no-repeat control.
