## Builds

### m05.r1.1 Room continuity tells on-screen from off-screen, with no face model
Builds on: m05.2 + m08.3 + m03.5
m08.3 binds voices to shots by where lines fall, and m03.5 needs Apple Vision face landmarks to say "on screen". A sound designer hears it another way: production dialogue changes room tone when the picture changes place, because the boom moved with the camera, while a narrator or voice-over keeps one room tone across every shot letter. So join the room letter of each line (m05.2) with the shot letter under it. A voice whose room follows the shot letters is `on screen`, and a voice whose room stays fixed while the shots change is `off screen (voice-over)`. This works on Linux and on footage with no visible mouths. It also repairs m08.3's weak binding: the shot that shares a voice's room is the one to bind. Judge: m03.5's narrated reels against talking-head reels, plus synthetic recuts where one voice's room is held constant across inserted shots.

### m05.r1.2 Audible contact: the hit, the side it came from, and whether the sound belongs to it
Builds on: m08.1 + m05.5 + m05.3
m08.1 finds "X moved into Y, then Y changed" in pixels. A Foley artist's first question about that moment is whether there is a sound for it, and whether that sound is the contact's own. Extend the contact event with the sound pass: a counted short sound within 150 ms of the contact, panned to the same side of the frame as the contact region (m05.5), becomes `contact at right 12.4, knock (right)`. A contact with no sound, or with a sound panned to the other side, is marked `silent` or `sound off-screen`. The silent case answers questions the card cannot answer today: "is the sound effect real or added", "was this clip muted and re-scored". Across a cut run where the bed carries (m05.3), contact sounds that stay in sync show one recorded event seen from several angles. Judge: m08.1's scripted collisions, rendered with matched, mismatched-pan and missing hit sounds as the answer key.

### m05.r1.3 The cue sheet: `log` for sound, and a sound library shared across the cache
Builds on: m08.7 + m05.4 + m03.6
This pushes m08.7 further. In post-production every film ships a music and effects cue sheet: each cue's in and out time, its source, and how often it repeats. Make `mediasift log VIDEO --sound` print that sheet from data already computed: every bed and counted hit with its copy hash (m05.4), its ceiling and its pan. The ten-times-larger version keys those hashes across all 730 cached cards. `mediasift log --same-as VIDEO 00:12` then answers "which other videos use this sound", which covers a trending TikTok audio, a reused stock sting and a talk channel's intro bed, at zero card tokens. Like m03.6, it puts the result where the heavy user already looks: one `[cue 3: bed, copy of 9f3a@00:12]` line in `transcript.txt`, for the credited-script job. Judge: ESC-50 and stock clips planted in known cached videos, plus exact-match versus same-class precision on the 200-clip sound bench.

### m05.r1.4 Masking ledger: where the mix hid the words, measured rather than guessed
Builds on: m03.2 + m05.6 + m05.1
m03.2's `Blind:` block needs a number for "speech under music above X dB". A mixer already has it: the bed-to-voice depth from the ducking measure (m05.6), taken per span. Set X with a measured curve: mix LibriSpeech and Vietnamese speech over beds at known depths, run each ASR backend, and record where word error rises past a fixed limit. A span below that line gets `Blind: 40-75 s speech under loud music, ASR may miss words`. The sound designer's second kind of blindness is the spectrum: m05.1's native-rate ceiling shows when a file carries real content above 8 kHz (cymbals, sibilance, a high whistle) that the 16 kHz decode erased before CED heard it, so the ledger also prints `sound above 8 kHz not heard`. Both blind spots are measured, which keeps the card's "complete for what the sensors detect" promise honest. Judge: m03.2's abstain questions, built on these synthetic mixes, where the right answer is "the card cannot know".

### m05.r1.5 "Listen to this": a spoken gate for sounds, as m03.1 is for gestures
Builds on: m03.1 + m08.6 + m03.8
m03.1 opens a gate for motion at deictic words. The ear has its own pointing words: "listen", "hear that", "this sound", "nghe này", "nghe nè", "tiếng gì". Speakers say them in exactly the reels the owner fact-checks and re-voices. After such a phrase, located by the word times m08.6 keeps, read the next 3 s of CED's 527 scores at a lower threshold than the card uses (m03.8 says those scores are already in memory). The top classes then become a suffix on the `say` line: `say "nghe này" (next: engine idling, maybe motorcycle, 00:31.2-33.8)`. When nothing passes, the suffix is `(next: nothing above the noise floor)`. That is a true answer, and it stops the agent from inventing a sound. m08.6's `look --say` gets the matching ear view: a drawing of the sound over that span at full resolution. Judge: phrase-cued sound questions on the reels, plus synthetic clips where a spoken "listen" precedes a known ESC-50 sound.

### m05.r1.6 Two witnesses for a generated voice
Builds on: m03.8 + m05.1 + m05.2 + m08.4
m03.8 would print CED's "speech synthesizer" class for the fact-checker. Alone, that class may be weak, and a wrong "AI voice" label damages a fact-check more than a missing one. Use m08.4's double-coding rule with independent witnesses. The witnesses are CED's synthesizer score, a voice band that ends at a TTS rate such as 22.05 or 24 kHz inside a 48 kHz file (m05.1), and gaps between words that are digital zero, with no room tone at all (m05.2). The card prints `[voice B: generated? 3 of 3 signs]` only when at least two witnesses agree. With one witness it prints nothing, and the evidence goes to `log`. Each witness fails in a different way (CED on clean studio speech, the ceiling on resampled uploads, the gaps on noise-gated podcasts), so requiring agreement buys precision without a new model. Judge: current open TTS and voice-clone outputs mixed into real reels at known lines, against clean studio and gated human speech as hard negatives. Score precision first.
