## Builds

### m03.r1.1 One deixis gate, two kinds of pointing
Builds on: m03.1 + m06.7 + m06.1
The camera gate (m03.1) and the screencast gate (m06.7) are one rule: a deictic word in a `say` line opens a 0.4 s window, and the one thing that "points" in that kind of footage is attached as a suffix. On camera footage that is the local motion event; on screen footage it is the pointer stop or text selection. What this adds is that the gate becomes the only door the pointer gets onto the card: the jitter filter in `see.listed` stays exactly as it is for every other line, so the noise it removed never comes back, and the reluctant card-only agent still gets the gesture. One judge covers both: the same "what was pointed at when they said X" question set, written for the camera bench videos and for two screencasts, scored together so neither footage kind can regress the other.

### m03.r1.2 Native-rate and stereo audio enter through `look`, not `see`
Builds on: m03.7 + m05.1 + m05.5 + m05.6
The 16 kHz mono decode is an incumbent asset, not only a loss: one decode, one buffer that every model (CED, ASR, voice embeddings) expects, and a fixed cost on 47-minute files. Keep it for `see`, and put m05's evidence (band ceiling, pan and width, ducking depth) behind `look VIDEO A-B --with band|stereo`, decoded from the original stream for those seconds only, which costs one ffmpeg seek and a short FFT. `see` gets at most one header word, computed by a single ffmpeg `astats` pass in C over the whole file (channel correlation and the top band carrying energy), and only when it flags a disagreement ("stereo bed, mono voice"), so the agent knows the zoom is worth running. The zoom log (m03.3) then decides whether any of the per-line words is asked for often enough to be promoted.

### m03.r1.3 The blind-spot ledger, measured by the mixer's ruler
Builds on: m03.2 + m05.6 + m05.1
My ledger needed a number for "speech under music too loud for ASR"; m05.6's gap-level measurement is that number. Bed level in the pauses inside speech, against the voice level, gives a per-span signal-to-bed ratio, and the ledger writes `Blind: 01:10-01:42 voice ~3 dB over music; words here may be missing` only where the ASR bench shows recall falls below a measured line. m05.1's band ceiling adds a second honest entry: a voice whose band stops at 4 kHz (phone) is where the speech model is outside what it was benched on. The ledger now says *why* the card may be deaf, in units a dubber can also use as the target gain, and the judge is m05.6's synthetic mixes at known gains: does the ledger fire exactly where word recall drops?

### m03.r1.4 Find the caret with the blink detector the card already has
Builds on: m03.4 + m06.8 + m06.5
m06.8 needs to know where the caret is, which sounds like a new tracker. But the picture pass already detects blinks, and a text caret is a one-cell blink at about 1 Hz in a fixed spot, a pattern almost nothing else on a screen makes. Use that blink as the anchor and write my glyph crops (m03.4) only around it: a native-resolution crop of the 3-5 lines around the caret, one per distinct text state, labelled with times. Typing (m06.5) falls out of the same crops: when the text in the crop grows, the states form one typed line with its corrections. No model, no OCR needed to find the crop, so the Linux box with no Vision gets a text sense too; Vision OCR still writes the card's words on Mac. Judge: replay the 20 code-screencast zooms as questions and count how many the caret crops answer, and their image-token cost.

### m03.r1.5 Narrator or on-screen speaker from three weak witnesses
Builds on: m03.5 + m05.2 + m05.3
My on/off-screen word relied on mouth landmarks alone, which fail on side faces, masks and wide shots. m05 supplies two independent witnesses that need no face: the voice keeps its room (m05.2) while the picture changes place across shot letters, and the sound bed carries straight through cuts (m05.3). The card writes `on screen` or `off screen` on the voice tag only when at least two of the three agree, and writes nothing otherwise, so a single noisy sense cannot put a wrong word on the card and Linux (no Vision) still gets an answer from the two audio witnesses. Judge: narrated reels and talking-head reels, plus m05.3's re-cut synthetic takes; agreement threshold set where false words are near zero.

### m03.r1.6 `steps.md` diffs: the repro script becomes a regression test
Builds on: m06.6 + m03.3 + m06.2
Push m06.6 ten times further: if a screen recording becomes an ordered list of actions and waits, two recordings become two lists, and `mediasift diff BEFORE AFTER` aligns them step by step: `step 4 waited 3.8 s -> 0.4 s`, `step 5 dialog "Invalid token" -> gone`. That turns the bug-repro job into its own verifier: the person records the repro, fixes the bug, records again, and the agent reads a short diff instead of two cards. The incumbent part that makes it cheap is the content-keyed cache: both recordings are sensed once and the diff is pure text work with no model. Every confirmed diff is also logged as a note (m03.3), so real repro pairs become bench items with known answers (the fix either removed the wait or it did not).
