### m05.r2.1 Ships tomorrow: the input you can hear, a click or keystroke with no picture answer
Builds on: m06.2 + m06.3 + m05.r1.2 + m06.r1.2
m06.2 needs a pointer tracker to know when an input landed, but a screen recording with a mic already carries the input as sound, and today's pipeline already counts short sounds (CED's typing / keyboard / tick-type classes), already pairs each with a picture change within 150 ms, and already holds every frame's grid distance. Ship it from those three tables alone, with no new model and no tracker: a counted click with no picture change in the next 2 s prints `click 00:12.3, no screen response 3.8 s`, three clicks inside 1 s with no response print `click x3, no response` (m06.3), and a typing burst with no text change marks typing into a field the recording does not show. The Foley addition is the source of the click: a physical mouse heard through the room mic carries room tone, while a UI sound played by the app is dry and line-level, so the line can say whether the click was the hand or the app (m05.r1.2's "does the sound belong to it"), and m06.r1.2's frame-timestamp check keeps a recorder stall from being blamed on the app. Judge: m06.r1.3's scripted sessions recorded with the mic on, where the input log is the answer key.

### m05.r2.2 The A/V offset, measured from every hit that has a picture
Builds on: m04.3 + m04.r1.1 + m02.8 + m05.r1.2
A sound editor syncs a reel by its claps and door slams, not by mouths, and the card already lists every counted hit that lands near a picture change. Take the signed offset of each such pair (not only "within 150 ms" but by how much and which way), and the median over five or more pairs is the file's A/V offset with no face model; per segment of a joined recording (m04.r1.1's join times), it shows the join where sync slipped (`audio 0.24 s late after join 2 at 41:12`), and an offset that grows steadily marks a frame-rate drift that m04.3's health line cannot see. This also gives m02.8's lag correction without Apple Vision, so it runs on Linux, and it is the cheapest attack on the bench's one shared miss: a gesture timed to a phrase is wrong by exactly the offset when the file is out of sync. Ships from existing data; judge by shifting bench clips' audio by known amounts and by the owner's three crash pieces.

### m05.r2.3 The stem sheet for a re-voice: what the dub must match, and where it floats
Builds on: m04.r1.3 + m02.r1.2 + m05.6 + m05.1
m04.r1.3 checks a dub's pace and whether the background was kept; m02.r1.2 says which voice was laid on top. A dubbing mixer asks one more thing: does the new voice sit in the mix the old one sat in. Measure the same four numbers on the original and the dub, per line: the bed's depth under the voice and whether it ducks (m05.6), the voice's spectral ceiling against the bed's (m05.1: a 24 kHz TTS voice over a 48 kHz bed floats on top), the room under the voice in its gaps, and the bed level in pauses. The `--against` card then prints only where they differ: `line 14: bed 4 dB under the dub (original 15 dB, ducked): background crushes the voice`, `dub dry, original had room 1: sounds pasted on`. The original is the answer key, so the judge is a round trip: re-mix a known dub with wrong gain, no ducking or no room, and check each word.

### m05.r2.4 Breaths are not fillers: the coach ledger with the breathing counted apart
Builds on: m04.2 + m04.r1.2 + m01.3
m04.2's main risk is false hits on breaths, because CED hears "speech-like" sound where ASR writes no word. A dialogue editor removes breaths and fillers as two different things, and CED already has a breathing class, so split the unworded gaps in two: breaths and voiced fillers, each counted against the speaker's own clock (m01.3). That turns a flaw into a second measure the coach job needs: `voice A: 14 fillers (2.1/min), 31 breaths, 6 taken mid-phrase` names no feeling, yet a breath taken in the middle of a phrase is the measurable mark of reading too fast or running out of air, and it works the same in Vietnamese and English. Judge: the 33 known fillers on the 47-minute talk plus hand-marked breaths on one TED clip, scored for recall of each class and for confusions between them.

### m05.r2.5 Diff by ear: align a re-post with its source when the picture lies
Builds on: m02.r1.3 + m05.4 + m02.7
m02.r1.3 aligns two files by grid hashes and word times, but the usual re-post tricks (mirror, crop to 9:16 with blurred bars, zoom, caption overlay, a 1.1x speed-up) break picture hashes and shift word times. The sound bed survives all of them: hash short spans of the log-mel frames the tagger already computes (m05.4), search a small set of time-stretch ratios, and align on the bed rather than on the voice. Pushed further, the edit list reports each layer separately, `clip 00:00-00:14 = source 12:03-12:17 at 1.10x; bed same, voice replaced 00:06-00:11; picture mirrored` (with m02.7's mirror check run only after the ear has found the match), which is what the fact-checker needs when a quote is put into another speaker's mouth. Judge: the sample videos re-posted by script with every trick at once, scored against the script.

### m05.r2.6 Fresh: the room you hear when nobody speaks tells you where the screen recording was made
Builds on: m05.2 + m06.r1.4 + m04.3
m06.r1.4 names screens by their persistent text and m05.2 gives recordings room letters; on a screen recording with a mic those two together say something neither does: whether a long recording is one session or several stitched together. A 2-hour demo whose room letter changes while the screen letter holds (or whose fan noise and hum floor step at one moment) was paused and resumed or joined there, which gives m04.3's health line a join witness that needs no timestamp metadata, so it works after a re-encode that erased the timestamps. Card line only when it fires: `room changes 41:12 with no cut: recording joined or resumed here`. Judge: the owner's three crash pieces (join times known from their durations) plus scripted sessions recorded in two rooms and joined.
