### m08.r2.1 The VAR protocol: ask "for what purpose" three times, and review only match-changing calls
Builds on: m07.r1.1 + m07.r1.3 + m08.4
Why add a sense? To answer more questions. For what purpose? So the deliverable built from the card is right (the summary link, the fact-check verdict, the credited script, the dub). For what purpose? So the owner stops catching errors after the agent said "done". VAR answers the same ladder by reviewing only four match-changing categories (goal, penalty, red card, mistaken identity), never every foul. Port that list: **link** (a timestamp points to the wrong moment), **attribution** (the wrong voice or person is said to have said it), **count** (how many, how long), **identity** (which on-screen person or text is meant). m07.r1.1's correction census and m07.r1.3's `verify` both bin only into these four, so the next sense is chosen by the category with the most overturned calls, not by the bench. 15% version, today: run the 50-line matcher over the owner's past summaries and fact-checks and tally the four bins by hand.

### m08.r2.2 The agent is the match coder: what an agent saw in `look` becomes a coded event the next agent can query
Builds on: m03.3 + m08.7 + m07.r1.3
In Sportscode or Hudl, an analyst codes events by hand onto the same timeline the tracking data sits on, and the next analyst filters both together. Today, when an agent zooms with `look` and sees "she points at the whiteboard at 00:12.3", that finding dies with the session. The build adds the agent's claims (from `mediasift note` and from `verify`) as events in the content-keyed cache with `by: agent`, so `mediasift log --kind gesture` returns sensor events and earlier agents' coded events side by side. Two things come free: a second question on the same video costs zero new looks, and every place where an agent's code and a sensor event disagree is a ready-made bench item with two witnesses. Runs: one append-only JSONL next to each cached card, nothing on the card.

### m08.r2.3 Ball-in-play time for every channel (stillness ledger, ten times larger)
Builds on: m07.r1.2 (pushed further) + m01.r1.5 + m08.5
m07.r1.2 finds one picture freeze or one missing reaction. A match analyst reports effective playing time for the whole game: the ball is in play about 55% of a football match, and that one number reframes everything else. Give every channel an in-play or dead state from data already computed (picture: 15 fps grid distance; voice: ASR words plus m01.r1.2's filled and empty pauses; bed: CED non-speech level), and print one box-score field only when it is unusual: `in play 58%: picture still 3:10 while voice runs; 2 freezes; 41 s dead air`. That answers "is this a slideshow over narration", "how long did the app hang", and gives m01.r1.5's trim ladder its tier-1 seconds directly. 15% version: one histogram of cached grid distances and CED silences on the 6 bench videos plus one injected freeze, before any card change.

### m08.r2.4 The telestrator: freeze the frame at the spoken word
Builds on: m08.6 + m03.r1.1 + m07.6 + m03.4
A pundit stops the replay on the exact frame and draws on it. Join my phrase-anchored zoom (`look --say "this one"`, m08.6) with the deixis gate (m03.r1.1): the word times give the instant, the gate gives the region that moved within 0.4 s, and `look` returns one 6-frame strip at 15 fps across word-time plus or minus 0.2 s, cropped at native resolution around that region (m03.4's crop rule) instead of whole tiles. The card gets no new line; the agent's own eyes get the gesture at a resolution 2 fps tiles never had. The same tool re-keys q06 (m07.6), so the bench's one shared miss and the fix for it use one instrument. Judge: re-keyed q06 plus about ten "what did they point at when they said X" questions, scored with the strip and without it, counting image tokens.

### m08.r2.5 Possession and turnovers: who took the floor from whom
Builds on: m08.2 + m01.4 + m01.r1.2
My possession chain (m08.2) logs who asked and who answered, but treats every turn as a clean pass. An analyst also logs turnovers: possession won by a tackle, not given. m01.4's step-ons are the tackles, and m01.r1.2's filled pauses ("ừ", "uhm") are a player shielding the ball, so a speaker who fills the pause keeps possession and one who leaves dead air invites a cut-in. The box-score field becomes `floor: A 62% (won 4 of 5 contested turns), B 31%`, and `log --kind turnover` lists each one with its time. That answers "who dominated", "who interrupted whom", and "did the host let the guest finish", all from ASR word times and voice embeddings already on disk. Judge: AMI's reference overlap labels for turnovers, plus the 47-minute talk's known filler deletions.

### m08.r2.6 The crowd's replay button as a free highlights key
Builds on: m01.5 + m07.1 + m08.5
Broadcasters build highlight packages from the moments the crowd reacted to. For YouTube, the crowd already voted: `yt-dlp --dump-json`, which `see URL` already runs, returns the "most replayed" heatmap and the creator's chapters for many videos, and today mediasift throws both away. Keep them in `sensed.json` and print one line only on talks: `most replayed: 03:41 (x2.8 of median), 12:05`, so "summarise and link with timestamps" gets the moments viewers chose, joined to the m01.5 reaction ledger (laughter, applause) as a second, independent witness. It is also the free judge m01.5 lacked: score "which line got the biggest reaction" against the heatmap peaks on talks that have both. 15% version, today: count how many of the owner's logged URLs carry a heatmap and chapters in their cached info JSON.
