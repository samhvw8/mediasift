## Builds

### m03.r2.1 The assumption that flips the plan: "the agent opens a second door"
Builds on: m08.7 + m08.r1.6 + m03.r1.2 + m07 (assumption broken: 0 real `look` calls)
Half of this council's builds, my own `look --with band|stereo` included, hide a sense behind a follow-up tool (`look`, `log`, `diff`) so it costs no card tokens. m07 found that real agents ran `see` about 7 times and `look` zero times. If that holds, every "behind a door" design reaches nobody and only card lines matter, which flips the plan. The incumbent card already does something none of these doors do: it names its own follow-up in its header. The build is a signpost rule: a withheld field family gets one footer line only when it holds something (`more: 765 picture events, 41 room changes -> mediasift log --kind ...`), and the usage log counts how often a signpost is followed. Settling observation before any door is built: add the signposts, then count follow-ups over the next 20 real sessions. Zero follow-ups means the door designs move to the card under the same bench rule, or they are dropped.

### m03.r2.2 Survive `| head` without giving up the timeline order
Builds on: m07.4 + m08.7 + m03.r1.1
m07.4 reorders the card so truncation keeps the important part. But the chronological timeline is the reason the bench scores 32/32 on sequence and deixis: an agent reading "what is on screen when X is said" finds the answer on the same or the next line. Reordering would break the function that earns the card its score. The incumbent-preserving version moves only the parts that are short and position-free (voice legend, counts, persistent text) to the top, and keeps the timeline in time order. When the card is longer than the measured truncation point, `see` prints the head plus the time range it covers and `log --between` for the rest (`timeline 00:00-04:10 of 12:30; rest: mediasift log --between 250-750`). A truncated read then loses the end of the video and says so, but never loses what a voice letter means. Judge: m07.4's rerun with cards cut at 120 lines, scored against the old and new layouts and against the uncut card, so sequence questions cannot fall.

### m03.r2.3 Keep the word times, keep the sentence lines: the deixis gate anchors on the word
Builds on: m08.6 + m03.r1.1 + m07.6
`words_to_cues` folds words into sentence lines, and that fold is what keeps the card short. m08.6 is right to keep the word times, but they belong in `sensed.json`, not on the card. My deixis gate opened its 0.4 s window from the line, and on a 4 s line that is a guess. With the word times stored, the gate opens the window at the deictic word itself ("this", "here", "đây", "này"), so the suffix lands on the right gesture and the card gains no line. Vietnamese is the extreme user: QUAN's text has no punctuation, but its CTC path still gives word times, so the gate works where the "?" cue in m08.2 does not. Order follows m07.6: re-key q06 at 15 fps first. If the key was wrong, the gate is judged on new phrase questions only.

### m03.r2.4 Persistent text read once per place, not once per cut
Builds on: m07.2 + m07.r1.5 + m03.r1.4
The changed-region OCR exists for a reason the persistent-text idea could lose: full-frame OCR on every frame is slow on 47-minute files and floods the card with the same banner. The shot letters already solve the repetition. A video with 49 cuts may have only 15 places, so OCR the first frame of each shot letter, which gives 15 Vision calls, not 49 or 1,000. A string seen in every appearance of a letter, or across letters, is persistent and is printed once (`on screen 00:00-00:45: "Theo WHO: 1/3 ..."`). On Linux, which has no Vision, the same per-letter frames go into the caret-crop sheet from m03.r1.4, so the agent still sees the text as an image. The caption band is left to roadmap step 5 and is not touched here. Judge: m07.2's count of strings that appear in the cached flow-sheet tiles but never on the card, with Vision calls per video recorded as the cost.

### m03.r2.5 One decode, one buffer: every DSP witness rides the pass that already runs
Builds on: m03.r1.2 + m01.r1.1 + m08.r1.4 + m01.r1.6
m01 and m08 propose six DSP witnesses: room tone, gaps, hum phase, ENF sync, landmark hashes and the speaker's clock. Each one is cheap alone. Six separate passes over a 47-minute file are not, and six readers of one file are six things that can break. The incumbent decode gives one 16 kHz mono buffer that CED, ASR and the voice embeddings already share. The build is an admission rule: a witness ships only if it reads that buffer, or the log-mel frames CED has already computed, inside the existing sound pass, and it records its own seconds in the run timing. The mains hum (50/60 Hz) and the room-tone floor fit easily within 8 kHz of bandwidth. Anything that needs the native rate or stereo goes through the `look` door (m03.r1.2) and so falls under the m03.r2.1 test. Judge: a timed run on the 47-minute talk before and after, plus the splice bench from m01.r1.1. The rule fails if the sound pass grows by more than a fixed share.

### m03.r2.6 The owner's corrections score the blind-spot ledger
Builds on: m07.r1.1 + m03.r1.3 + m03.2
My ledger writes `Blind: 01:10-01:42 voice ~3 dB over music` where the card may be deaf, but its only judge is a synthetic mix. m07's correction census finds the heaviest user's real complaints after the agent said done. Join the two: each correction that falls in a span the ledger flagged is a hit, and a correction outside every flagged span is a blind spot the ledger did not know about. The second list is the most useful output: it names the next sense from failures that happened, and every item already has the owner's own correction as its answer. Runs: nothing new. The census script reads `sensed.json` ledger spans next to the transcripts.
