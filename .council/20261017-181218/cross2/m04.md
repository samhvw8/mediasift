## Builds
### m04.r2.1 The better question: "what will the person notice on the first play that the agent signed off on?"
Builds on: m04.r1.6 + m02.r1.6 + m06.r1.2 + facilitator provocation
The brief asks what an agent cannot perceive in a video; the owner's words ("rushed", "shifted by one", "slides not film", "broken", "dropped the ums") show that the pain is what the agent handed back without noticing. Rewritten, the question becomes a gate: `mediasift accept FILE --job dub|join|clip|tts|summary`. The job word picks only the checks that job has failed on before (health and recorder gaps from m06.r1.2 for `join`, pace and layers for `dub`, the label shift for `tts`), and the output is a short pass/fail list in m02.r1.6's box-score form, not a card. Unlike the parents, this adds a moment in the workflow: it runs before "done", so each item on the after-done bench (m04.r1.6) becomes a check that either fires or does not, and a check earns its place only when it turns one of those owner catches into a machine catch.

### m04.r2.2 Before and after the fix: diff two bug-repro recordings by their steps
Builds on: m06.6 + m06.r1.3 + m02.r1.3
m06.6 turns a screen recording into a numbered list of steps, and m02.r1.3 aligns two event logs into an edit list. Push both further, toward the question a person with a bug actually asks next: "did my fix work?" `mediasift diff before.mp4 after.mp4 --steps` aligns the two step lists and prints only what changed: `step 4: waited 3.8 s -> 0.3 s; step 5: dialog "Invalid token" -> gone; new step 6: toast "Saved"`. Neither parent had a second recording of the same flow, and that pair is the bug report's real proof. The judge comes from m06.r1.3: script the same session against a build with an injected delay and one without, and score the diff against the two scripts' logs.

### m04.r2.3 Two clocks for one crashed demo: screen and webcam aligned by the voice they both heard
Builds on: m04.3 + m06.r1.2 + m05.2
The owner's crashed 2-hour demo ("it boken i'm join it", "tôi chỉ có webcam nửa cuối", I only have the webcam for the second half) came as separate screen and webcam files, and nothing told him which parts of each existed or whether they still lined up. Both microphones heard the same voice, so cross-correlate their speech envelopes to align the files, then use m06.r1.2's frame timestamps to mark where each recorder stopped writing frames and m05.2's room letters to confirm both files share one room. One coverage line per source: `screen 00:00-1:58:10 (no frames 41:12-41:50: recorder); webcam 59:30-1:58:10, drifts +0.4 s by the end`. That gives the owner the answer to "what do I actually have, and can I put them side by side" before he joins and uploads a 5 GB file. Judge: the owner's real pieces, plus synthetic drops and drift added to a two-camera bench clip.

### m04.r2.4 "Slides, not film", split by cause: the animation code or the encoder
Builds on: m04.1 + m06.r1.2 + m02.7
m04.1 measures that a generated video feels like slides; m06.r1.2 asks which link in the chain caused a stillness; m02.7 reads duplicated-frame cadence from the grid hashes. Combined, the motion-feel line also names the cause the agent must fix: `motion feel: slide-like; holds come from the content (frames keep arriving, nothing moves between keyframes: add easing or tweens)` or `motion feel: judder; 1 in 3 frames duplicated at the encoder (render fps 20 into a 30 fps file: change the export rate)`. The owner's complaint on 09-23 turned into a redo with no stated cause; this line names which of two different fixes applies. Judge: render one p5 animation four ways (eased, keyframed, low render fps, dropped frames) and check the stated cause, not only the feel.

### m04.r2.5 The dub measured twice: a target before it is made, a check after
Builds on: m05.6 + m02.r1.2 + m04.r1.3 + m05.r1.6
m05.6 reads how far the bed sits under the voice and whether it ducks; m02.r1.2 says whether a voice sits in the location room or floats as an added layer; m04.r1.3 compares the dub against the original. In the re-voice job ("re-voice YouTube into Vietnamese keeping the background") the owner only heard the result, so use the same measurement twice. Before making the dub, `see ORIGINAL --mix` prints the targets (`voice 14 dB over bed, bed ducks 6 dB, room 1, 4.1 syll/s`); afterwards, `accept DUB --job dub` prints the same numbers for the dub next to them, adding m05.r1.6's generated-voice signs only where the owner asked for a natural voice. What this adds is a round trip with numbers the TTS and mix step can be tuned to, so the agent fixes "voice floats 9 dB too loud and does not duck" itself before the person listens.

### m04.r2.6 "Bị dính từ" as a number: the batch ear reads the gaps a human reader leaves
Builds on: m04.5 + m02.r1.5 + m05.r1.4
The HSK complaint was "nghe nó rất gấp và bị dính từ" (rushed, words stuck together). m04.5 caught the rush with syllables per second, but "stuck together" is about gaps, and m02.r1.5 already measures the gap spread that separates a machine reader from a human. Apply it to the batch of generated word clips: for each multi-syllable clip, the shortest gap between syllables and the clip's leading and trailing silence, scored against one human reference read of 20 of the same words: `212 clips: 37 joined (gap < 30 ms, human median 90 ms), 12 start with no lead-in`. m05.r1.4's masking curve gives the threshold where ASR starts to merge words, so "joined" is defined by a measured break point, not a guess. Judge: the labels plus TTS output at known speeds and inserted pauses; the owner's wife's list of complaints is the real check.
