## Builds
### m01.r2.1 The radio-play bench: the cue sheet is the answer key for every sound question
Builds on: m07.1 + m07.r1.6 + m01.r1.1 + m01.2
This build answers the provocation by making the bench's weakest part, a key writer who cannot hear, the point. A radio drama is built from a cue sheet ("INT. kitchen, room A; B interrupts A at 0:41; door slam 0:52; cut to street"), so the script is an exact key for what a listener hears. A script renders short plays from that cue sheet: TTS voices in Vietnamese and English (pinned voices), room impulse responses for the room letters, ESC-50 foley for counted sounds, splices with and without matched room tone, planned step-ons and planned long pauses. Every sound sense in this council (seams, room letters, step-ons, hesitation, canned repeats, on/off screen) then gets a key with times correct to the sample, written without anyone hearing it. m07.1's SDH cues stay as the real-footage check, so a sense ships only when the synthetic score and the SDH score agree.

### m01.r2.2 Too clean to be a conversation: missing flaws are the evidence
Builds on: m01.4 + m08.2 + m03.r1.5 + m01.r1.1
This turns the flaw around: a radio producer knows that real talk is messy. People step on each other, breathe before they answer, and share a room tone, and their turn gaps follow a natural spread with some overlaps. A dialogue stitched from separate clips, or a fake "interview" made from two monologues, is too clean: no step-ons in five minutes, every gap 300-600 ms, a different room per voice, and no shared bed under the turns. Join m08.2's question-and-answer chains to my step-on counter and to m03.r1.5's room witnesses, and print one footer line only when two of these agree: `conversation: 0 overlaps in 41 turns, rooms differ per voice -> likely assembled, not recorded together`. The same features also mark a call recording: a lower floor on turn gaps, half-duplex dropouts where one voice gates out when the other starts, and a 4 kHz ceiling. Judge: real podcasts and AMI meetings as "together", and the same speakers re-cut into alternating clips as "assembled".

### m01.r2.3 Cut from the bottom: `see --lines N` trims the card like a producer trims to time
Builds on: m07.4 + m01.r1.5 + m08.r1.5
m07.4 found that agents run `| head -120` and silently lose the end of the video. This build pushes it further. Radio news is written so that an editor can cut it from the bottom, and a producer who is over time trims in tiers. So the card gets a priority tier on every line (legend and box score; counts and `say` lines; blinks and plain changes; texture), and `see --lines 120` drops the lowest tier across the whole timeline instead of dropping the last minutes. It ends with `trimmed: 214 tier-3 lines, all in mediasift log`. Each voice letter is also defined inline at its first use (`[voice C = female, off screen]`), so any cut keeps the meaning of every letter it contains. Judge: rerun the real-footage bench with cards cut at 60/120 lines: tail-truncated against tier-trimmed, scored on answers and tokens.

### m01.r2.4 The tape log: the speaker's clock goes into `log`, not onto the card
Builds on: m08.7 + m08.6 + m01.3 + m08.r1.6
Producers log tape before they edit: every take, its in and out times, and a note like "good, slow on the key line" or "2 s hesitation". m08.6 keeps the word times, and from them plus per-word RMS the pipeline can store each voice's baseline (median gap before a turn, syllables per second, level) and per-word deviations in `sensed.json` at almost no cost. `mediasift log VIDEO --kind pace|pause|level --voice A --outlier 2x` then answers "where did she hesitate", "where did he speed up", "when did he raise his voice", and the card gets no tokens until m08.r1.6's query counts show agents asking. Vietnamese works from syllable rate (one syllable per QUAN token), with no punctuation needed. Judge: listener-labelled yes/no-at-T questions plus the radio-play bench's planned pauses (m01.r2.1).

### m01.r2.5 The stressed word opens the gesture gate: prosody times the shared bench miss
Builds on: m03.r1.1 + m01.3 + m08.6
The one shared miss is a gesture timed to a phrase. m03.r1.1 opens a gate on a deictic word from a list, but a gesture's peak lines up with the stressed syllable of the phrase, which is a known result in gesture research: beat and pointing gestures land on the pitch accent. A producer hears that stress as a local rise in level plus a lengthened syllable against this speaker's own norm. So the gate opens on the most stressed word in each `say` line, measured from word duration and RMS against the speaker's clock. It needs no word list, which matters for Vietnamese, where "đây" and "này" are not always said, and the motion event within ±250 ms of that word is attached as a suffix. Judge: reel q06 re-keyed at 15 fps (m07.6), plus the ten "what moved when they said X" questions, scored for the deictic-list gate against the stress gate.

### m01.r2.6 Room continuity across angles: ENF says when, room letters say where
Builds on: m08.r1.4 + m01.2 + m08.r1.2
m08.r1.4's mains-hum trace proves that two clips were recorded at the same moment, but a viral "second angle" can be real footage of the same moment with its sound replaced. A location sound recordist checks continuity by ear: the same room has the same tone and reverb from every microphone in it. Compare room letters across the clips that ENF aligned. Same time and same room means one incident with live sound. Same time and different rooms means at least one soundtrack was laid in. No ENF overlap and the same room means the same place on another day. These phases then join m08.r1.2's phase list across files, not only within one. Judge: two phones recording one event, and the same pair with one track swapped for a studio voice-over.
